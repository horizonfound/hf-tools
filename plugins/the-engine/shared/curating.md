# Curating a set: choosing the photos, then ordering them

Read this every time you choose photos for a reel, a carousel, or any group of edited photos someone will see one after another.

**Two sources, kept apart.** *What goes in* follows Nicole's rules for choosing (Nov 6, from her handoff's SELECTS.md): the best photos, chosen on their own merit. *The order* follows the Assistant's own flow rules (Dustin, Oct 25), which Nicole approved on the reel she called "really great selects." That split is the default until Dustin and Nicole say otherwise (decision 10; this file is the one place to change it). Each rule below says whose it is. Where something is your own judgment, it says so, and it is not a rule. Never turn one of Nicole's comments about a particular photo or clip into a rule she didn't say. If a situation isn't covered, choose what Nicole would call the best, and ask the photographer when in doubt.

## What goes in: the best photos (Nicole, Nov 6)

1. **The photos are the point,** of every reel and every carousel, a behind-the-scenes reel too. Footage only supports them; it never decides which photos go in (the reel skill has Nicole's footage rules).
2. **Choose the best photos:**
   - the most beautiful and engaging, the ones that stop the scroll;
   - the ones that tell the best story, the story this shoot has;
   - variety, in distance, light, people, action and mood;
   - emotion: laughing, crying, comforting, hugging, a child's real reaction;
   - the ones that help tell the story the photographer is trying to tell. If they wrote notes or chose how it should feel, that steers it.
3. **Similar photos are allowed when together they tell a story.** Keep a run of near-similar frames when each one moves the moment forward. Her example: a child with paper boats, holding them, then the boats in their hands, then setting them in the water, then the boat floating away; each frame adds a step. **Leave out a near-twin that adds nothing new,** such as the same expression a fraction of a second later.

**How many.** About 8 to 14 for a reel and 5 to 8 for a carousel is a guide, not a rule. The shoot's best photos and its stories decide. (Instagram allows up to 20 in a carousel, and the reel page up to 40.)

**How it should feel.** With behind-the-scenes footage, the reel page asks how it should feel (Heartwarming, Nostalgic, Fun, Dreamy, or their own words) and has a box for notes ("Want to direct the reel?"); `reel_page.py show` prints both when they have filled them in, and they may tell you the same in the chat. Their notes come first when they say something specific. The feel decides which of the best photos lead and how the order builds. Nicole named the idea but hasn't said how each feel should change the picks, so how a feel turns into picks is **your judgment, not a rule**: for example, tender moments leading for Heartwarming, play and laughter for Fun. When it's unclear, ask them. A feel never lets a weaker photo in.

## How to choose, step by step (what made the picks Nicole approved)

1. **See the whole shoot at once,** on the numbered contact sheet of every finished photo. Never open photos one by one to start with.
2. **Find the stories in the shoot:** the threads that run through it, for example the day itself, one person's little moment, two people together, a child busy with something. A good set tells one or more of them from start to finish.
3. **Shortlist the strongest frames for each story:** real expressions and real reactions, plus one clear scroll-stopper to open on.
4. **Settle the close calls side by side.** Put each group of near-similar frames next to each other and choose the strongest: the peak of the expression, the sharpest frame, the cleanest frame. For a reel, `reel.py strip` with just the group as its picks (`--picks 7,8,9`) draws them in a row; open a photo larger only when the strip can't settle it. This is the one place to look closer: a handful of comparisons, never photo by photo.
5. **Keep sequences that tell a story; drop twins that don't** (rule 3 above).
6. **Order them** by the flow rules below.
7. **For a reel, mark its rhythm: every reel, fully (Nicole, Nov 10).** The marks are the reel's rhythm. A reel of hers with three of thirteen photos marked played nearly every photo for one beat: "All of the photos are on the same beat... you should also be choosing which photos should be rolling longer and quicker. Base it off of what's going to make the video engaging with the photos... it needs to be engaging, it needs to be beautiful." So, as you order a reel:
   - **Longer (`+`), about a third of the photos:** the scroll-stopper you open on, the emotional peaks (a kiss, a laugh, a tender moment), a wide frame with a lot to take in, and the closer.
   - **Quicker (`-`), about a quarter:** details (shoes, hands), quick fun bursts (a peace sign, a spin), and the middle of a fast run.
   - **The rest plain.** Spread the marks so the reel breathes: never three Longers or three Quickers in a row, and a Longer comes after a few quick ones.
   - The vibe then shapes it. On every beat: a Longer photo aims for about three beats, a Quicker one a single beat, and plain photos one beat or two in a mix that is the reel's own; those are aims, and the song's hits still decide, so now and then a Longer photo lands shorter. Calm and Build stretch everything. The photographer can change any mark on the reel page, and theirs win.

   A carousel has no marks.
8. **Check the order as it will play.** Read your order as a strip, left to right; `reel.py strip` draws it. Fix any jolt.
9. **Tell them in a few words** how it flows and what was left out and why (below).

## The order: how the set flows (the Assistant's own, Dustin, Oct 25)

By the time you see them, the photos are edited and each already looks great on its own. Once you know which are in, your job is to make them look great **as a group**, flowing from one photo to the next in the most harmonious way possible.

- **Open on the scroll-stopper:** the most striking, emotional frame, readable at a glance on a phone.
- **Variety of distance.** Wides (the place, the whole scene), mediums (people together, connection), close-ups (faces, hands), details (the ring, the shoes, the texture). Move between them: wide, then in closer, then a detail, then back out. Never three of the same distance in a row.
- **One step at a time.** Each photo should feel like the natural next one. Neighbors share something: the color, the light, the direction someone is looking or moving, a shape, the same people. When two things change at once (bright, warm and wide straight to dark, cool and close) it jolts. Change one thing at a time.
- **A path for color and light.** Let warmth and brightness travel somewhere (golden light toward sunset, light toward deeper) instead of bouncing back and forth between warm and cool, bright and dark. An odd one out (the single blue frame, the one black and white) sits where it reads as a breath on purpose, or beside another like it.
- **Direction leads the eye.** Someone looking or walking toward the next photo pulls the viewer forward. Two people facing each other across a cut feels like a conversation; facing away feels like a break.
- **No near-twins side by side.** Two similar frames that are both in go apart, with other photos between them. A run kept on purpose because it tells a story (rule 3 above) stays together, in the order it happened.
- **An arc.** Arrive in the place, move into the connection, take in the details. Let the emotion build, then come back down, and close on a quiet, final frame: walking away, a look back, a wide with small figures, the story resolving.

**Where the two meet (decision 10).** The flow decides the order, not what goes in. When one of the best photos jolts where it is, find it a better place (swap neighbors, a detail beside it as a breather) rather than leaving it out; what goes in was settled above. (For a reel, how a wide photo fares on a tall screen is the reel skill's own rule: tall photos first.) If the shoot is missing something the flow needs, say it kindly and specifically: "These are all close-ups. Got any wides from this one?" or "Everything is bright and sunny; is there a quieter frame to end on?"

## How you show it

The order in one short message, with a few words on how it flows, not a review of each photo: "Opens wide on the beach, moves in to the two of them, then hands and the ring, ends on the walk away." Add one line for what was left out and why: "Left out 8 and 9, the same laugh a moment after 7." When the pick goes on a page (the reel page, the carousel's picker), there is nothing to ask: they move things there. In the chat, ask if they want to go with it.

Their taste wins. Their own order is always kept on the page, and yours never replaces it. If they move things, do it their way; mention once if the new order puts two near-twins or three close-ups together, then go with what they chose.
