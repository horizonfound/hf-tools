# Curating a set: making edited photos great together

Read this every time you choose photos for a reel, a carousel, or any group of edited photos someone will see one after another.

## The job (Dustin, Oct 25)

By the time you see them, the photos are edited and every one already looks great on its own. Your job is not to judge whether a photo is good. **Your job is to make them look great as a group:** curate the set so it flows from one photo to the next in the most harmonious way possible. That is the main thing you bring, and it comes before everything else in the choosing.

A great photo that breaks the flow stays out. A smaller set that flows beats a bigger set that jumps.

## What makes a set flow

- **Variety of distance.** Wides (the place, the whole scene), mediums (people together, connection), close-ups (faces, hands), details (the ring, the shoes, the texture). Move between them: wide, then in closer, then a detail, then back out. Never three of the same distance in a row.
- **One step at a time.** Each photo should feel like the natural next one. Neighbors share something: the color, the light, the direction someone is looking or moving, a shape, the same people. When two things change at once (bright, warm and wide straight to dark, cool and close) it jolts. Change one thing at a time.
- **A path for color and light.** Let warmth and brightness travel somewhere (golden light toward sunset, light toward deeper) instead of bouncing back and forth between warm and cool, bright and dark. An odd one out (the single blue frame, the one black and white) either goes, or sits where it reads as a breath on purpose.
- **Direction leads the eye.** Someone looking or walking toward the next photo pulls the viewer forward. Two people facing each other across a cut feels like a conversation; facing away feels like a break.
- **No near-twins side by side.** Same pose, same spot, same crop: keep the stronger one, or put other photos between them. The exception is a deliberate beat, like three frames of one laugh in a burst.
- **An arc.** Open on the scroll-stopper (the most striking, emotional frame, readable at a glance on a phone). Then arrive in the place, move into the connection, take in the details, and close on a quiet, final frame: walking away, a look back, a wide with small figures.
- **One look.** If one edit stands apart from the rest (a different color treatment, black and white among color), leave it out or pair it on purpose.

## How you work it

1. Look at the whole contact sheet first. Notice the distances, the light, the colors and the moments before picking anything.
2. Pick for the group, then order it. Read your order as a strip, left to right, the way it will play. `reel.py strip` draws it so you can check it in one look.
3. Fix any jolt: swap neighbors, move a detail in as a breather, drop the one that fights.
4. If the shoot is missing something the flow needs, say it kindly and specifically: "These are all close-ups. Got any wides from this one?" or "Everything is bright and sunny; is there a quieter frame to end on?"

## How you show it

The order in one short message, with a few words on how it flows, not a review of each photo: "Opens wide on the beach, moves in to the two of them, then hands and the ring, ends on the walk away." Add one line for any trade-off: "Left out 14. It's lovely, but it's a twin of 9." Then ask if they want to go with it.

Their taste wins. If they move things, do it their way; mention once if the new order puts two near-twins or three close-ups together, then go with what they chose.
