# Interview questions

Ask one at a time, in your own words, keeping the meaning. The follow-ups are the point, not optional extras: use at least one whenever an answer is a label instead of a picture ("families," "warm and authentic," "I don't know"). Build every follow-up from what they just said. Every list of options ends with "Other, tell me." Say "you're in the driver's seat" once near the start and once more wherever a choice is theirs.

Timing: about 8, 12, 7 and 8 minutes, then about 5 to write, check and save. If a part runs long, trim follow-ups. Never trim the spoken story in Part 2.

## The opening

Before the first question, in your own words (the full list is in SKILL.md): what this is, use your voice ("tap the microphone key and just talk, don't clean it up"), they are in the driver's seat, nothing ever gets posted, what to have handy, "note:" saves a note for the team, and skip anything. No captions, and say why.

## Part 1: The facts (about 8 minutes)

1. "What's your name, and what's your business called?"
   - If the business name is their own name, ask what they want to be called in this chat.

2. "What do you shoot?"
   - This is the first dig. Whatever they say, say something real about it, then ask the next layer, built from their words. For "families": "What kind of families? Is there a style you love shooting in, a type of family you light up for, tones you edit toward?" For "weddings": "Big ones or small ones? Where? What part of the day do you live for?" For "brands": "Who are they, what do they sell, what do they come to you for?"
   - Then: "What are you most excited about when you're shooting?"
   - If they list several things: "If next month could only have one kind of session, which one?" Keep the rest as "on the side."

3. "Where are you based, and how far will you go for a session?"

4. "Where do your clients come from?" Offer:
   - They come to me (local sessions)
   - They ship to me (products or brands I shoot from here)
   - I travel to them (weddings, destination, campaigns)
   - Both
   - Other, tell me (prints, workshops, something else)
   - Write it down clearly. It changes what posts talk about and who they are for.

5. "What's your main offer right now, and roughly what does it cost?" A range is fine. If they would rather not say, skip it: the Assistant never puts a price on anything unless they give it one.
   - If they mention minis or special offers: "Do those show the price when you post them?"

6. The client. Not a type, a person. "Think of one real client from the last year or two that you'd love ten more of. Who were they? How did they find you? What was going on in their life when they booked?"
   - Follow-ups: "What did they say when they first reached out?" "Were they nervous about anything?" "What did they say when they saw the photos?"
   - If they hand you a category ("a family, they care about love, it was Christmas"), go back to the person: "Tell me about one of them. The mom's name, what she texted you."

7. "Where do new clients find you today?" Instagram · Referrals from past clients · Google · Word of mouth · Other, tell me.

8. "What's your Instagram handle, and what's the link where people inquire or book?" A homepage is not a booking page. Ask once if there is a page or form where people actually inquire.

9. "How often do you post right now, honestly? And how do you feel about that?"
   - No shame, ever. If it is "not in two months," say most photographers who start this are in the same spot, and that is the whole reason this exists.

10. How much of them vs. the business. This is a brand question, not a posting question. "Some photographers show their kids, their dog, their messy car, their bad days. Some show only the work. Neither is wrong, and people connect with whichever one is true for you. Where do you want to land?"
    - Mostly the work, with a peek at me now and then
    - An even mix
    - A lot of me; the work rides along
    - Other, tell me
    - Follow-up: "What parts of your life are fine to show, and what's off limits?" Write down both lists. Every other tool asks before using anything personal.

11. What they want the business to look like. Forward, not backward. "Picture your calendar a year from now, filled the way you'd want it. What's on it? Which sessions, how many, at what price? Anything you haven't offered yet but want to?"
    - This is the question Nicole answered with "Oh wait, I want to do mini sessions too, but that's not something I've even really offered." That kind of answer is the point. Follow-up: "Is there something you've been thinking about offering and haven't yet?"
    - Then say it back: posts pull toward that calendar, not toward what they have always done. "You don't have to keep showing up the way you always have. If that was working, you probably wouldn't be here."
    - If they stall, build options from questions 2 and 6: "You said X is what you love and Y drains you. Would a year of mostly X, at a higher price, with one Z each season, be close? Or something else, tell me."

12. "What's the hardest part of posting for you?" More than one is fine. Picking photos and the order · The on-screen hook for reels · Finding time · What to say in stories · Not knowing what to say at all · Other, tell me.

Recap Part 1 in three lines, with their words. Ask what is off. Fix it, then move on.

## Part 2: Their voice (about 12 minutes)

Open with why there are no captions: "I'm not going to ask for your captions. Most people write those with AI now, so they'd teach me to sound like AI instead of you. I'd rather hear how you actually talk." Then: "Your words, not mine. If I ever put a word in your mouth that isn't yours, you get to throw it out."

1. A client text or email. "Paste a text or email you sent a client. Any normal one: a booking reply, a 'your gallery is ready' note, a thank-you. Typos and all, don't fix it."
   - Follow-up: "Is that how you'd text a friend too, or do you have a client voice and a friend voice?" If there are two, ask for one friend text as well.

2. The spoken story. The most valuable sample in the whole interview. "Now the fun one. Tap the microphone and talk for about two minutes: tell me about a session you loved. Who was it, what happened, what made it special. Don't clean it up. Rambling is perfect."
   - If they type two lines instead, ask once more, kindly: "Give me the long version, the one you'd tell a friend over coffee." Typing the long version is fine too.
   - Follow-ups from inside the story, using their words: "You said [their phrase]. What was happening right then?" "What did they do that you didn't expect?" "While that was happening, were you directing, or watching?"
   - If the story is still short, ask for a second one. Two short stories beat one.
   - Keep this story. It becomes the Story in the Edge section and the material for the voice check.

3. Reviews. "Paste three or more client reviews or kind messages. Screenshots are fine."
   - Follow-up, because it tells you what they value: "Which line in there made you feel the most seen?"

4. Phrases. "Any words or phrases you say all the time? And anything you'd never say, or that makes you cringe when other photographers say it?"
   - If they stall, offer what you have already seen: "From your text I see 'SO' in caps and three exclamation points. Is that you everywhere, or just with clients?" "You said 'magical' twice in the story. Yours, or would you never write it?"

5. Emojis. "Emojis: never, sometimes, always? Any favorites?" Never · Sometimes · Always · Other, tell me.

6. Price talk. "How do you feel talking about price?" Upfront, it's on everything · Only when someone asks · I'd rather not, ever · Other, tell me.
   - Follow-up: "What about minis or special offers, do those show the price?" Many photographers say never in regular posts, always on minis. Write down both.

Recap Part 2 as "here's how you sound": three to five plain observations with a quote for each. For example: "You write long, open sentences that wander and land somewhere heartfelt. You say 'truly' and 'literally' a lot. Three exclamation points when you're excited and one word in CAPS. You joke at your own expense. You never swear in anything a client sees." Ask: "Does that sound like you? What's off?"

## Part 3: Their edge (about 7 minutes)

Open with: "I'm not going to ask what makes you different. Nobody can answer that cold, and you shouldn't have to pitch yourself to me. I'll ask around it, and you'll see it show up on its own."

1. "What do clients always say, after a session or when they see their photos? The exact words if you remember them."
   - Follow-up: "Has anyone said something that surprised you?"
   - Follow-up: "When a past client refers you to a friend, what do they say about you? Have you ever seen the text?"

2. "Tell me about a session that went nothing like you planned. Rain, a meltdown, a kid who wanted no part of it, a timeline that fell apart. What did you do?"
   - Follow-up: "What did the client say after?"
   - This is where the edge lives: "I just watched and let them play," or "I had a backup location in the car."

3. "What was the first session where you thought, oh, this is the thing?" This gets the why without asking for a mission statement.

4. "What's the thing you'd never do, even if a client asked and offered double?" Heavy editing · Posed-only · A certain trend · A kind of shoot · Other, tell me.
   - Follow-up: "And the opposite: what do you do that other photographers might call too much?"

5. "What do your friends tease you about?" Then: "Does any of that show up in your work? Being a mom, a surfer, the one who always has snacks..."
   - Follow-up: "Is any of that off limits for posts? Just say so, and the Assistant will always ask first before using anything from your life."
   - Never make them sell themselves. If they say "I don't know, I'm pretty boring," offer options from their samples: "Your reviews say 'she made my husband laugh,' twice. Is that a thing?"

Recap Part 3 as "here's your edge, in your words": three or four lines, each tied to something they said, not a claim they had to make. Ask: "Does that feel like you, or did I make you sound fancier than you are?" Fix it.

## Part 4: Their look (about 8 minutes)

Run it from `look.md`. The questions, in order:

Open: "Last part, and it's the quick one. I'm not going to design you a brand. Your photos are the look. My job is to match what you already have, or pick something simple that stays out of their way, so your text can look the same in CapCut and Canva every time."

1. "Do you already have a brand identity: fonts, colors, a website you like?"
   - Yes, I have fonts and colors I use
   - Sort of: a logo, or a website someone made for me, but I'm not sure of the fonts
   - No, nothing yet
   - Other, tell me
   - Yes or sort of: "Send it here: screenshots of your website or a Canva template you use, the font names if you know them, your logo. A link works too." Then match it (look.md): name the fonts, check they exist in CapCut and Canva, find the closest free font if one does not, show the two side by side, pull their colors as swatches. Say: "Instagram won't use your website's fonts, so we pick the closest one that lives in both apps."
   - No: the library, below. Mention that the other two ways in exist (build from branding they already have, or style a Canva template and the Assistant reads it) in case they have something after all.

2. "Put five favorite photos in a folder and point me to it. Your work, the ones you'd put on a billboard." The sample posts need the photos as files the Assistant can read. If it cannot reach their files, have them drop the five into the chat instead, and say in one line that the sample posts will use a placeholder until the first reel or carousel (look.md).
   - Say what you see in two lines: light, color, feel, how they frame people. Ask if that matches how they see their own work.
   - Pull five colors and show them as swatches: an artifact of colored blocks with plain names, no codes. Say: "In a lot of cases your colors don't matter much. This is photography focused. These are so text and backgrounds sit nicely with your photos, not to turn you into a logo." Ask: "Any of these wrong?"

3. Fonts. No path: "Here's the library on your photo. Which one feels closest?" One artifact: the looks as tiles on one of their photos, each named. They pick one, or say none and tell you what they do like. Each look is two fonts (a headline font and a small-text font) that live in both CapCut and Canva, plus where text sits. Yes path: confirm their fonts, or the closest swap, shown as text samples side by side, not on photos.

4. "When text does go on a photo (a carousel cover, a reel hook, a mini sessions announcement), where should it sit?" A quiet corner, small · Centered in the upper third · Across the bottom · Other, tell me. Suggest one from their photos. And say the default out loud: "Most of your posts will have no text at all. You're a photographer. It's mostly photos." Only change "barely any" if they ask for more.

5. The sample posts. Build them (look.md). Then one question: "Which of these would you post tomorrow, and what's the one thing you'd change?" Change one thing at a time until they say yes. "I don't like any of these" is a useful answer, not a rude one: ask what they do like and build from that.

Recap the look in three lines (the fonts, the colors, where text sits), and say they can change it any time by saying "update my look."
