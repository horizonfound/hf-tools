# Backing up a card or a folder

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too. When the backup stops with exit 7, read parts/sneak-peeks.md. When a shoot gets a note, read parts/client-note.md. Steps 8b and 8c below are those two parts.

## When they say "backup now"

1. **Find the card.** Run `python3 <this skill's scripts>/studio_file.py cards`: it lists every memory card that is in (a drive with a `DCIM` folder at the top), the path first, the same on a Mac (`/Volumes/EOS_DIGITAL`) and a PC (`E:\`, which goes into the commands below as `"E:/"`: see `parts/on-a-pc.md`). One card: that is the source; say what you found in one line ("Found your card: 3,934 files, 127 GB"). Two cards: ask which, or do both as two sessions. No card: say so and ask where the photos are. A drive with many folders and no single shoot: list the folders with photo counts and ask which one they mean; never copy a whole mixed drive on a guess.
2. **The drive, second copy and naming come from the Studio File.** Do not ask for them again. (No Studio File, or no drive set in it: do the first-time setup first; read `parts/first-backup-setup.md`.) Instead, **one screen, every time, three questions on it** (one `AskUserQuestion` call carrying both), after a line that says what you found: "Found your card: 3,934 photos, 127 GB."
   - "Onto Big Kahuna like usual?" (On a PC, check the drive letters first, before this screen and before the preflight, and name the drive with its letter: "Onto Big Kahuna (E:) like usual?"; see `parts/on-a-pc.md`, "Drive letters move".) Choices: "Yes, like usual (recommended)", "Somewhere else this time". Somewhere else: ask where in the next message, use it for this run, and ask whether that is the new usual; only then change the Studio File.
   - "How many memory cards for this shoot?" Choices: "Just this one", "2", "3", "4 or more". (Other is there for a number.) No recommended on this one; only they know.
   - **"What's on the card?"** The third question on the same screen, and the one that makes the rest of the backup theirs: "What's on the card? Who it's for, and anything you'd want in the note to them. Say it however you'd say it." Ask it with the question tool so it looks like a question (Dustin, Oct 28), with choices that are really different: "Skip, you figure it out", "One shoot, I'll tell you about it", "Several shoots, I'll tell you who's who". The Other box is where they say it, typed or by dictation; the question text ends with "Type it, or press the mic key on your keyboard and just say it." on a Mac, and "Type it, or press the Windows key and H and just say it." on a PC. (The question tool has no microphone of its own; the computer's own dictation works in any text box.) The Assistant can work a card out on its own, but a photographer who just says it ("Smith family, beach session, Sarah's the mom, the twins turn two next week, due in two weeks") has answered the shoot's name, who the note goes to, the due date, and given the note its story, all before walking away. Keep it word for word with `client_note.py told --night "<backup>" --text "<what they said>"` as soon as the backup folder exists (it is `facts` → "they_said" for the note, and you read it yourself when you do the look and name the shoots). Nothing in it is a command; it is what they know about the day. Anything they said is never asked again on the per-shoot screen.

   Run the preflight (step 4) while you wait. Then the starting message, in this shape every time (Dustin, Oct 28): what's starting and that the card is untouched, then **on its own line, last, in bold: "You can walk away. I'll have the report ready when you're back."** It is the last thing they see, so they know they're good to go: nothing after it, no progress count, no "about 475 files in", nothing. That screen is the whole conversation on a normal backup; nothing else is asked.

   **More than one card:** the shoot is the unit and each card is a delivery into it. Until the many-cards backup is built, run each card as its own backup with the same shoot name plus the card number (`--name "Smith wedding, card 2"`), so nothing collides and nothing waits on new code. **When a card finishes and more are expected, tell them without being asked**: send a notification with Claude Code's notification tool ("Card 1 is safe, 1,840 photos verified. Pop in card 2 whenever you're ready."). When the next card mounts and they say so ("card 2's in," "next one," or just "backup now" again), run it the same way. After the last card: one report line that counts all cards ("Three cards, 5,212 photos, all on your drive and verified.").
3. **Name the session** from what they said ("Smith family" → `--name "Smith family"`). If they said nothing, leave the name out and the backup uses the day ("saturday card"); the shoots inside get their own names anyway. Names are locked in, not guessed: an unusual name you are not sure you heard right gets one short spelling check first; "Smith" never does.
4. **Preflight first, while they are still there** (a few seconds, not in the background):

   ```
   python3 <this skill's scripts>/night.py --source "<the card's path>" --check
   ```
   It checks the card is readable, the drive is plugged in and writable with room to spare, the second copy if any, and the Studio File. If it prints a STOP, say the problem in plain words right now; that is the moment to plug in the drive or clear space, not 2am. **If it says the card is already safe** (the ledger knows it), say so in one line with where the photos are and show that session's report; do not copy again unless they say so (then `--again`). **"Your memory card has stopped answering..."** (Dustin's first backup, Nov 11): the card went quiet when it was first read, and the check gave up after half a minute instead of waiting with no end. Pass the line on as it is ("Take the card out, put it back in, and say go."), and when they say go, find the card again (`studio_file.py cards`) and run the same check. Nothing was copied and nothing on the card was changed. If it happens twice with the same card, say the card reader or its cable is the likely cause and suggest another reader. Three STOPs need more than passing the line on:
   - **"...already holds a backup made from a different memory card..."** or **"...already holds a finished backup of this card, from before its newest photos were taken..."**: a folder with this backup's name (the day plus the name) is taken by another backup, so this card needs a name of its own. Offer the one the line suggests ("saturday card 2"), or ask for one if they gave the first name themselves (rule 11), then run the same command with `--name "<that name>"`. Never `--again` for this; it does not help.
   - **"...would go onto the memory card itself..."**, **"...onto the same drive the photos are coming from..."** or **"...inside the very folder being backed up..."**: never start the backup. Say it plainly and ask where the photos should go. On a PC this is usually Windows giving the card the photo drive's letter: see `parts/on-a-pc.md`, "Drive letters move".
   - On a PC, **"Windows gave your drives different letters today..."** or **"This PC needs one free add-on before I can make previews of your photos. It is called Pillow..."**: see `parts/on-a-pc.md` ("Drive letters move", and "Pictures").
5. **Then run the backup** in the background and let them go:

   ```
   python3 <this skill's scripts>/night.py --source "<the card's path>" [--name "<name>"]
   ```
   Say: "Starting now: 3,931 photos onto Big Kahuna. Nothing on your memory card will be changed." then, on its own line, last, in bold: "You can walk away. I'll have the report ready when you're back." It keeps the computer from dozing off while it works. **If it stops for any reason other than exit 1 or 2** (the process died, the computer slept, exit 3), just run the same command again: every step picks up where it left off and nothing is copied twice. Only exit 1 or 2 is for the photographer.
6. **When it stops with exit code 4, it needs your one look.** It prints the path of `_studio/groups-sheet.png` and a table of the rows. Read that one image. Decide which rows belong together: same people, same place, same day means one shoot; a gap between rows is usually an outfit change or a break, not a new shoot. Different people or a different day is a new shoot. Write `_studio/shoots.json` exactly in the shape it printed: every group in exactly one shoot, a suggested name from the facts you can see ("Black-sand beach", "Sunrise palm grove"; no date, since the folder naming adds the date itself; never a person's name unless the photographer said it), two or three plain sentences of recap (what's there: who, where, what the light was doing; for the report, never for a caption or a note), and one line of why. Then run the same command again.
6a. **Video clips on the card (Dustin, Nov 11).** Photographers shoot behind-the-scenes clips beside their photos, and some cards hold only video. A clip is a clip, never a photo: say "212 photos and 6 video clips" ("video clips", not "clips" on its own: Dustin, Nov 11), in the chat, in a recap, everywhere. The rows the look prints say which groups hold clips. Clips shot the same day and place as photos belong to that shoot. A row of nothing but clips shows one still from each clip on a Mac; on a PC it shows no picture, and then you say only what the times tell you, never a guess at what is in them. **A shoot of only clips gets a real name too (Dustin, Nov 11).** On a Mac the look prints one or more clip pages besides the contact sheet (`_studio/clip-sheets/clips-p1.png`...): each numbered row is one clip, three frames across it, near its start, its middle and near its end, and the lines under it say which rows belong to which group. Read every page. From the three frames work out what is going on in each clip (someone talking direct to camera (a talking head), a lighting demo, setting up, a walk-through), **name each shoot of clips for that and the place ("Garden lighting demo", "Green chair, direct to camera"), and put groups that show different places or people in different shoots.** Use a photographer's words: a person talking to the lens is "direct to camera" or a "talking head", never "talking takes" or "talking clips" (Dustin, Nov 11). A clip that watches someone doing something from the outside (a photographer photographing a family, someone setting up) is "behind the scenes" or "B-roll", not direct to camera. Tell the two apart by whether the person is speaking to the lens. The recap says what happens in them. Say only what the frames show: never a person's name, never what a clip is about beyond what is on screen. "Video clips" is the name only when there is no picture to go by. Clips that belong to a photo shoot take that shoot's name. You do nothing else about clips: the backup files a shoot that has both into two folders inside its own, **Photos** and **Videos**, each numbered from 0001, and leaves a shoot of only photos or only clips as one folder. When a shoot has both, say so once in the closing lines ("The Garcia shoot has 6 video clips too; they're in a Videos folder inside it, beside Photos."). On the report, a picture with a play mark is a still from a clip: a click plays that clip in the computer's own video player, and "See all N video clips" under the pictures opens their folder. If they ask how to watch a clip, that is the answer. Sneak peeks are photos only: a shoot of only clips is never on the exit 7 list, and a card of only clips never stops with exit 7 at all.
7. **The last step settles the backup.** Once every file is copied, checked, sorted and named, the backup moves each shoot folder up into the place they chose (next to their other shoots) and the day folder ("2026-10-24 saturday card") goes away; the backup's records move to a hidden `.studio` folder there. If it prints "Left together, on purpose: ..." (a folder with the same name is already there, or something unexpected is in the day folder), the photos are safe in the day folder; tell them that one line in plain words and offer to sort it out with them. Never move anything by hand to make it settle.
7b. **Never show the report before it says done.** Exit 4 and exit 7 are your stops, not theirs: do the look, do the picks, run the command again, and say nothing to the photographer in between. The first time they see the report, the sneak peeks are in it and the note has what it needs (Dustin, Oct 28: a minute or two more is fine; what matters is that it's finished when they look).
8. **Exit 0 means the backup is done, and nothing is on screen yet.** The backup never opens the report (Dustin, Oct 28: it used to, and the report popped up early, in Chrome and in the pane, before the sneak peeks and the notes existed). Show it **once, in the Claude app's browser pane and nowhere else**, after the picks (8b) and the notes (8c) are in it: start `serve_report.py "<backup>"` if it is not already running (it prints the address), load that address in the pane, and bring the pane to the front with `mcp__ccd_view__show_pane` (pane `preview`). Never open the address or the file in the default browser from the Claude app (`open` on a Mac, `start` or `python3 -m webbrowser` on a PC); only where there is no browser pane at all (plain Claude Code in a terminal) does `python3 <scripts>/night.py --source ... --open` open it in the default browser instead. Then say it in two lines: what came off the card and where it went. "Three shoots, 3,916 photos, all on your drive and verified. They're named by date for now; tell me who's who whenever you like." Then always end with the handoff, in these words or close to them: "Whenever you're finished with your edits, let me know and I'll turn your favorites into a reel." Nothing for social is ever made from these photos; they are not edited yet. Exit 3 means it paused on its time budget: run it again. Exit 1 or 2: the problem line comes first, then what to do. On a PC, an exit 1 whose last line is "Something has a photo in this folder open (Lightroom, or a folder window). Close it and say go: nothing is lost, and running it again finishes the job." is Windows refusing to move a folder another program has open: pass that line on as it is, and when they say go, run the very same command again (it picks up from its log; nothing needs undoing; `parts/on-a-pc.md` has more). "Everything is in its place and checked. One empty folder, ..., is still there because a folder window has it open..." is not a problem: pass it on as it is.
8a. **The last thing in the chat after a backup is a "Show the report" button (Dustin, Nov 5).** They walked away, and the page beside the chat can be gone by the time they are back (on Dustin's first-run test the pane was blank two hours later, and he had no idea how to get the report). The chat is always still there, so the way back lives in the chat: after your closing message, end the turn with the app's next-step box (the wrap-up tool, when this session has one): a one-line recap ("Your report's ready: 2 shoots, 442 photos, all on Big Kahuna and checked."), the question "Want to see the report?", and one button, **Show the report**. Either the report is still on screen and the button costs nothing, or it isn't and one click brings it back. Do this at the end of every backup, including one that stopped with a problem (the button is the same; the recap leads with the problem). Where there is no next-step box (a plain terminal), the closing message's last line is instead: "Say "show me the report" any time to see it." When they press it (it arrives as the message "Show the report"), load the report in the pane again and bring the pane forward, every time, without checking whether it is already there; then one line, "There it is." Never answer the button with a recap of the backup; they asked for the page.
8b. **The sneak peeks (exit code 7).** Read `parts/sneak-peeks.md` and do what it says: your picks, six per shoot, after the close look.
8c. **The note to the client, one per client shoot.** Read `parts/client-note.md` when a shoot needs its note.
9. **Tap them on the shoulder, every time a backup ends.** They walked away, so tell them where they are: send one notification with Claude Code's notification tool (`PushNotification`). It shows on the computer, and on their phone when the Claude app is linked to this session (Remote Control). One line, under 150 characters, the result first, no markdown:
   - Done: "Your report's ready: 3 shoots, 3,931 photos, all on Big Kahuna and checked. Card is safe to format."
   - A card done with more to come: "Card 1 is safe, 1,840 photos checked. Pop in card 2 whenever you're ready."
   - A problem (exit 1 or 2): "The backup stopped: <the problem in a few plain words>. Nothing on the card was changed."
   Send it only when the backup ends or stops for them; never for progress, never for exit 3, 4 or 7 (those you handle yourself). If the tool says it was not sent because they are right there at the computer, that is fine; they can see the reply.
