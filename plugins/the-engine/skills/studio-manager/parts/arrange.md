# Changing the photos on a page

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too. The quoted lines below are the default wording; Dustin and Nicole may change any of them.

**The photographer chooses; you pick only when they ask (Nicole, Nov 13: "I choose, not you.").** These pages are where they choose, and every change is saved to the kit as they make it, so nothing is lost if a page is closed. Nothing is picked for a reel, a carousel or stories until they ask for it, by pasting the line a page gives them or in their own words. When they do ask, choosing and ordering the photos is your job, done the way `shared/curating.md` says. A shoot with no post kit has no page: make the kit first (`parts/post-kit.md`, or the reel skill's Step 1 for a reel on its own).

**Each piece has its own page.**
- **The reel: the reel page** (Nicole's reel maker, `reel_page.py`). The reel's photos in a row at the top (their own, or your pick once they have asked for one), in the order it plays, with Longer and Quicker on each photo and all the shoot's photos below; then the song, the vibe, and **Make my reel**, which makes the CapCut project there and then. See "The reel's page" below.
- **The carousel: the carousel's picker** (`arrange.py --piece carousel`). The carousel's photos in a row (theirs, or your pick when they asked for one), in the order people swipe, every photo of the shoot below it; "I'm happy with this order" makes the slides. See "The carousel's page" below.
- **The carousel's designer**, where they lay the slides out by hand and add their own words ("lay out my carousel", "design my carousel", "add text to my slides"): "Laying out the slides: the designer", below. A single post is one slide made there ("make a single post").
- **The stories designer**, the same page made for Instagram stories ("make my stories", "lay out my stories", "design my stories", "add text to my stories", and the line its page gives them, "pick my story photos for <shoot>"): "Making stories: the stories designer", below.

The plain singles `kit.py build` makes (the Singles folder) have no page of their own; they are changed by saying it, and you run `kit.py picks` again (a single post they lay out themselves is made in the designer, "A single post" below). The plain stories `kit.py build` makes (one photo each) are changed the same way; the stories they make themselves are in the stories designer. (The older reel picker, `arrange.py --piece reel`, still works if you run it by name, but nothing links to it and you don't open it: the reel page took its place, decision 3, `kit.REEL_DOOR`.)

They can also get to the reel page without you: the kit page's Reel card ("Create my reel →", or "Make a new version →" once a reel is made) opens it. The kit page has no door to the carousel's picker any more (its Carousel card opens the designer); open the picker for them when they ask to change or reorder the carousel's photos. A post kit being made opens only the kit page (`parts/post-kit.md`): never a picker or the reel page in the same breath, and never a pick.

## The reel's page

```
python3 <scripts>/reel_page.py open "<kit>"
python3 <scripts>/reel_page.py show "<kit>" [--reel "<reel's name>"]
python3 <scripts>/reel_page.py make "<kit>" [--reel "<reel's name>"] [--capcut-song "<name>"] [--hook "<line>"] [--name "<name>"] [--no-song] [--dry-run]
python3 <scripts>/reel_page.py reels "<kit>" [--new [--name "<name>"] | --open "<reel's name>" | --rename "<reel's name>" --to "<name>"]
```

**When to open it:** whenever they say "make a reel", "change the reel", "reorder the reel", "let me move those around" about a reel, or anything that means the same, and whenever you have picked a reel's photos because they asked (the reel skill's Step 2). **open** gets the page ready (it numbers the photos if that hasn't been done, makes the small copies the page shows, and starts the page server if it isn't running) and prints the page's address on its last line. Load that address in the Claude app's browser pane and bring the pane forward (`mcp__ccd_view__show_pane`, pane `preview`). Never Chrome. The page needs the kit's photos numbered (`kit.py sheet "<kit>"`; **open** does it if it hasn't been done). **Opening the page is not a reason to pick:** with no pick of yours it opens on "I'll drag them in myself", and they put the photos in. When they did ask for your pick, save it first with `kit.py picks "<kit>" --reel ...` (your numbers are the page's).

Then say one or two lines. **When you have not picked** (the usual case): "Your reel page is open beside us. Choose Just photos (or add your footage), put your photos in, add your song, and press Make my reel. If you'd rather I pick the photos, tell me which ones you'd like in the box under Put your photos in order and copy the line there into the chat." **When they asked for your pick,** in place of a paragraph of numbers: "My pick for the reel is on the page beside us: 12 photos, in the order they'll play. Opens wide on the beach, moves in to the two of them, ends on the walk away. Choose Just photos (or add your footage), move anything you like, add your song, and press Make my reel." No question, nothing to wait for: **the page asks the song and the vibe, never you.**

**What the page does by itself, in Nicole's words and in her order:**
- **Behind-the-scenes footage?** Two choices: "Add behind-the-scenes footage" or "Just photos". Make my reel waits until they have chosen one. Beside them is a third card, **"Talking to camera"** ("You talk about the shoot; your photos go with what you say. Opens its own page."): it is a link, not a choice. It opens the talking reel page for the same shoot, changes nothing on this page and never gets the tick; the reel page still wants one of the first two before Make my reel. Everything about a talking reel is in `parts/talking-reel.md`. With footage, "Add my footage" makes a Footage folder for this shoot and opens it; they drag their videos in and the page lists them by itself, with "Good to know: longer footage takes longer". The page gives them a line to copy into the chat, "Look through my footage for <shoot>" (with " and follow my notes" when they wrote in "Want to direct the reel?" or picked how it should feel), and under "Watch your footage and pick your own" a player for each video with a strip of stills; "+ Use this moment" adds a 4-second moment of their own. Each moment is a card: ▶ plays it, − and + change its length by half a second, **✂ Trim** picks exactly where it starts and ends, Remove (with Undo), and Select for the style. **Trim** opens a panel over the page: the clip plays on a loop with its sound, and under it is a strip of stills from the video, about 8 seconds either side of the clip, with the part they are keeping outlined. They drag the left handle for where it starts, the right handle for where it ends, or the middle to slide the whole clip; the arrows beside Start and End move an end a quarter second; a line says what they have ("Starts 0:31.6 · ends 0:34.0 · 2.4 s"). A clip is 1 to 15 seconds and never runs past the ends of its video. **Done** saves it; Cancel, the ×, Escape or a click outside the panel closes it and changes nothing. When they say they can't get to a later moment without making the clip longer, or want a clip to start somewhere else, this is the answer: "Press Trim on that clip's card." You have no command that trims a clip; it is theirs to do on the page. Their videos are only ever read; the page plays a small copy of a video that can't play as it is (an iPhone's High Efficiency video), made once in the kit's hidden folder.
- **Put your photos in order.** "Let my assistant choose" (your pick, once they have asked for one) or "I'll drag them in myself" (their own order); both are kept, so they can switch back and forth. Until you have picked, "Let my assistant choose" says "Your assistant hasn't picked for this reel yet. Tell it which photos you'd like below, then copy the line into the chat." Under the two choices is a box, **"Tell your assistant which photos you'd like in this reel"** (optional, and only for this reel; her examples: "Only the ones in front of the white backdrop." "Just the kitchen and the cupcakes."), and under it the line to copy into the chat: **"Pick my reel photos for <shoot>"**, with the reel's name after the shoot when the shoot has more than one reel, and **", and follow my note"** on the end when they wrote a note ("Pick my reel photos for Beach, Kitchen, and follow my note"). Drag to move, tap a photo below to add it, × to take one out, Longer and Quicker on each photo, Undo. **They can select several photos at once, the way photo programs work:** a click on a photo in the order selects it (an outline and a check mark), Shift-click selects everything between it and the last one clicked, Cmd-click (Ctrl-click on a PC) adds or drops one, and Escape lets them all go. Dragging any selected photo moves the whole selection to where it lands, in order; a tag on it says how many photos are moving. With two or more selected a bar says "N selected" with **Longer, Quicker, Neither, Take them out** and **Clear**. In "All your photos" a plain click still adds a photo or takes it out; Shift-click and Cmd-click select there too, and then they drag any of them up into the order or press **Add them to the end**. Each of these is one change: one press of Undo takes the whole batch back. What is selected is only on the page; you can't see it and nothing saves it.
- **Add your song.** "Add my music" makes a Music folder for this shoot and opens it; they drag a song in, or a screen recording of a song playing (the page takes its sound once and keeps it beside the recording, or in the Music folder when the recording sits in a folder of theirs outside Your Content). A new file is picked by itself ("Got it: <name>."). "No song" is there too.
  - **A short song that opens with a glitch.** A screen recording often starts with a glitch and a moment of silence. For a song under 40 seconds the reel starts after the last silent gap in the song's first 12 seconds, on the first 1 of a bar after it; with no gap it starts at the top. The page's Plan line says where ("starting the song 4 seconds in"). A longer song starts where it picks up, as before. You don't set this and there is nothing to ask them; if they wonder why a reel skips the first second or two of a recording, that is why.
  - **A recording they replaced.** If they put a new recording in with the same name as one before, the next Make my reel takes its sound again, as "<name> sound 2.m4a" beside the first one. The first sound is never written over, and the song list still shows the recording once. Nothing for them to do.
  - **There is no Remove on a song.** The page has no button that removes a song, and you have no command for it: never move, rename or delete a song or a recording, and never offer to. If they ask how to get a song off the list, say plainly that there's no button for that yet, and that the list shows whatever is in the shoot's Music folder (**Add my music** opens it), so a song they take out of that folder themselves leaves the list. A reel that had that song picked then says "That song isn't there anymore. Press Add my music and drag it in again."
- **What style of behind the scenes is this?** (only with footage) Moment first, The reveal, Back and forth, Bookends. Each style keeps its own selected clips; the page puts the clips among the photos the way the style says, and the reel is cut on the beat as always.
- **Pick the vibe.** On every beat (recommended), A little of everything, Calm, Build. Whichever they pick, the cuts land on beats you can hear in their song (the reel skill's Step 4 has how, if they ask).
- **Make my reel.** The CapCut project is made there and then; the page says "It's in CapCut: <project>" with an Open CapCut button. Pressed again it makes another version beside the first ("... reel 2"); a reel already in CapCut is never changed.

**Several reels from one shoot (Nicole, Nov 12: "I don't want to deselect everything and lose it. I just want to start a new one.").** Under the page's title is a bar, **Your reels**: a drop-down of the shoot's reels, **+ Start a new reel**, and **Rename** (the name turns into a box with Save and Cancel). Start a new reel keeps the reel they were on exactly as it is and opens a blank one: "I'll drag them in myself" with nothing in it, no pick of yours, footage not chosen, no song, and every clip you have found for the shoot, as found. The reels are "Reel 1", "Reel 2" and so on until they rename them. **Every reel is fully separate:** its photos and their order, Longer and Quicker, its song, footage or just photos, the style, the vibe, its clips and their lengths and which are selected, their notes, and your pick. Nothing done in one reel changes another. The songs in the Music folder and the videos in the Footage folder are the shoot's, shared by all its reels.

- **Every command acts on the reel that is open on the page** (the one being worked on): `kit.py picks --reel`, `footage.py scan` and `moments`, `reel_page.py show` and `make`. When the shoot has more than one reel, each of them says first which reel it is about. Read that line, and if it is not the reel they meant, stop and name the right one.
- **To act on another reel without opening it,** name it: `kit.py picks "<kit>" --reel 19+,4-,10 --for-reel "Reel 2"`, `footage.py scan "<kit>" --reel "Reel 2"`, `footage.py moments "<kit>" --file <file> --reel "Reel 2"`, `reel_page.py show "<kit>" --reel "Reel 2"`, `reel_page.py make "<kit>" --reel "Reel 2"`. A few words of the name are enough when they fit only one reel. The page does not move: it shows that reel's new pick or clips when they open that reel.
- **When the line they pasted names a reel** ("Pick my reel photos for Beach, Kitchen"), run `reel_page.py note "<kit>" --reel "Kitchen"` first: it checks that the reel named is the one open on the page and prints that reel's note. If it is another reel, it says so: pick for the one they named with `--for-reel`, and say which reel your pick went to.
- **From the chat** ("start another reel for the beach shoot", "call that one Kitchen", "go back to the first reel"): `reel_page.py reels "<kit>" --new [--name "Kitchen"]`, `--rename "Reel 2" --to "Kitchen"`, `--open "Reel 1"`. Then load the page's address in the pane again so it shows. Two reels of one shoot never share a name; if the name is taken, say so and ask for another.
- **A new reel has no pick of yours and sits on "I'll drag them in myself".** The page says "Your assistant hasn't picked for this reel yet. Tell it which photos you'd like below, then copy the line into the chat." Pick for it only when they ask. After you do, your pick waits under "Let my assistant choose": tell them to press it, in a few words.
- **A reel can never be removed** (nothing is deleted). If they want one gone, say so plainly and offer to rename it or use it again: "I can't remove a reel, so nothing of yours is ever lost. Want me to rename it, or start that one over?"
- **In CapCut** the projects are named for the shoot, not for the reel: the first one made is "<shoot> reel", the next "<shoot> reel 2", whichever reel on the page it came from. `reel_page.py show` lists the newest project made from each reel; say which is which when they ask.
- Each reel's first mix is its own: making a reel again with the same photos, vibe and style gives the next mix of that reel.

**"Look through my footage for <shoot>"** (and "... and follow my notes") is the line the page gives them to copy once their videos are in. Read the reel skill's section "Behind-the-scenes footage" (`../assistant-make-a-reel/SKILL.md`, beside this skill's folder) and follow it: `footage.py scan` makes the contact sheets and prints their notes and feel first, you read the sheets and take a close look at the promising stretches, and `footage.py moments` saves what you chose. The page shows the moments by itself, as cards under "Select your clips". Never write moments into the reel page's records any other way. `reel_page.py show` prints whether footage is on, the style, the clips selected, the videos in the Footage folder and their notes and feel, if you need to know.

**"Pick my reel photos for <shoot>"** (and "Pick my reel photos for <shoot>, <reel name>", and either with ", and follow my note") is the line the page gives them to copy when they want your pick. **It is the only time you pick a reel's photos**, with their asking the same in their own words ("you choose the reel photos", "pick again"). In order:
1. `python3 <scripts>/reel_page.py note "<kit>"`, with `--reel "<the reel's name>"` when the line names one. It changes nothing. It prints which reel the pick is for and whether that is the reel open on the page, **their note for that reel's photos, word for word**, and the command that saves the pick for that reel only. A name that fits no reel stops it and lists the reels: ask which they meant.
2. **The note decides what may go in.** Pick only what it allows ("Only the ones in front of the white backdrop": nothing else, however good). It is their choice of photos, so follow it exactly and never widen it; within what it allows, choose the best and order them the way `shared/curating.md` says. If no photo fits the note, or you can't tell from the sheet which photos it means, say so and ask one short question; never fill in with photos it leaves out. The line says "and follow my note" but the note is empty (they cleared it since): pick from the whole shoot and say there was no note. No note at all: the whole shoot, by curating.md.
3. Read the numbered sheets once (`kit.py sheet "<kit>"` prints where they are) and pick by the reel skill's Step 2, **marking the rhythm as it says (about a third Longer, about a quarter Quicker, the rest plain, never three Longers or three Quickers in a row)**.
4. Save it **for that reel only**: `kit.py picks "<kit>" --reel ...` (the marks ride on the numbers: `19+,4-,10,14`), with `--for-reel "<name>"` when step 1 said the reel named is not the one open on the page. A pick for one reel never changes another. `kit.py picks` prints their note again after saving: read your pick against it once more.
5. Say in one line how it flows, and how you read their note when there was one ("12 photos, all from in front of the white backdrop: opens on the laugh, moves in close, ends on the look back."). An open page shows your new pick by itself within a few seconds while "Let my assistant choose" is on; if it hasn't, load its address in the pane again. If they are on "I'll drag them in myself" (every new reel is), their order stays on the screen and yours waits under "Let my assistant choose": say so in a few words. If they would rather say a change than drag it ("start with the couple"), do it their way with `kit.py picks` again; the page follows the same way.

**show** prints what is on the page: whose order, the order Make my reel uses (`19,4,10+,14-`), the song and the vibe they picked there, and how many reels the page has made. Use it whenever you need to know where they are ("did it work?", "which song did I pick?").

**make** is Make my reel from the chat, for three things only: a song from CapCut's own music (the page doesn't offer them; the reel skill's Step 3 has the licence note to say first), words on the first photo or a trial reel when they ask (Step 5 there), and when the page says "Ask your assistant to make the reel instead" or can't reach its server. It uses exactly what the page would (their order or yours, the song and the vibe they picked there) and makes the reel the same way. If no song is picked on the page yet, it stops: say "Add your song on the page beside us, or pick No song, then tell me." Its last line is "Made: <the CapCut project>".

**Longer and Quicker.** On each photo in the row are Longer and Quicker. Those are the reel's `+` and `-` marks, and they count with every vibe (On every beat, A little of everything, Calm, Build). In Nicole's words, which the page uses too: "Longer stays on screen about twice as long", "Quicker gets the shortest time, one beat". On a short song a Longer photo may get a little less than twice, since nothing is left out to fit. On On every beat a Longer photo aims for about three beats, a Quicker one a single beat, and plain photos one beat or two in a mix of the reel's own (Nicole, Nov 10). Those are aims, not promises: the song's hits and the bar still decide, so now and then a Longer photo or the opener lands on one beat, or a few one-beat photos fall together, most often on an order with no marks. If they ask why a photo flashed by, say that (the reel skill's Step 4), never that it can't happen.

**Your marks and theirs.** Every reel you pick goes to the page fully marked (the reel skill's Step 2); they can change any mark there. The marks they set themselves are kept: when you pick again while the page is open and a photo they marked is still in, it keeps their mark, not yours. When your new pick has the same photos in the same order as what the page shows, it depends on whether they touched it. **An order they never changed takes your new marks**: what the page shows is still exactly a pick of yours, marks and all, so the fresh marks show by themselves within a few seconds (this is how a reel picked before the fuller marks gets them: pick it again with `kit.py picks --reel`). **An order they marked themselves keeps theirs**: once they have pressed Longer or Quicker on any photo, the page keeps what is on it, marks and all, and changing only the marks does not show there. Say that plainly rather than "done" ("The page keeps the marks you set; press Longer or Quicker on a photo to change one"). `reel_page.py show "<kit>"` prints the order Make my reel uses, marks in place, so look before you say which it was.

**A line `ARRANGED: <kit folder> | reel | 12 photos`** from `home.py pending` comes only from the older reel picker, opened by name. Run `arrange.py apply "<kit folder>" --piece reel` (it puts their order into the kit as the reel's pick and clears the line), then `reel_page.py open "<kit folder>"`, load it in the pane, and say: "Your order is on the reel page beside us. Add your song, pick the vibe, and press Make my reel."

## The carousel's page

```
python3 <scripts>/arrange.py open "<kit>" --piece carousel
python3 <scripts>/arrange.py apply "<kit>" --piece carousel
```

**When to open it:** whenever they want to change which photos are in the carousel or their order and would rather do it themselves: "change the carousel", "reorder the slides", "let me pick the carousel photos". **open** gets the page ready (it numbers the photos if that hasn't been done, makes the small copies the page shows, and starts the page server if it isn't running) and prints the page's address on its last line; the page shows the kit's carousel pick. **Pick the carousel only when they ask you to** ("pick the carousel for me", "you choose"): then save yours first with `kit.py picks "<kit>" --carousel ...`; otherwise open the page as it is and they put the photos in. Load the address in the Claude app's browser pane, bring the pane forward (`mcp__ccd_view__show_pane`, pane `preview`; never Chrome), and say, when you picked: "My pick for the carousel is on the page beside us: 7 photos, in the order people will swipe. Move anything you like, then press \"I'm happy with this order\"." When you did not: "The carousel's photos are on the page beside us. Put in the ones you'd like, in the order people will swipe, then press \"I'm happy with this order\"." There is nothing to wait for and no question to ask: the page does the rest.

**What the button does.** The page says "Making your slides..." and, a few seconds later, "Done. 7 slides are in your Carousel folder." with an "Open the Carousel folder" button beside it. The plain slides in the kit's Carousel folder are then in their order: the new ones made from the photos, and the earlier plain slides moved into the kit's hidden folder (never deleted). Canva's slides, and any file in that folder that isn't named like one of this shoot's slides, are left where they are. A plain slide they have edited and saved under its own name counts as a slide: if its place in the order changes it is moved into the hidden folder with the others, kept exactly as they saved it, and you can fetch it back for them. The kit page and the posting queue's picture follow by themselves. If a photo can't be read (the drive is unplugged), the page says which one and that the Carousel folder was left as it was; nothing is half done.

**A line `ARRANGED: <kit folder> | carousel | 7 photos`** from `home.py pending` means they pressed that button. Run `arrange.py apply "<kit folder>" --piece carousel`. It makes sure the slides are in the order the button was pressed on (they almost always already are, and then it changes nothing), clears the line, and prints the order and what it did. Then one line: "Your slides are in the new order: 7, in the Carousel folder."

- **If it printed a line starting `Canva:`**, the kit's Canva slides were made from another order than the carousel has now, so ask: "Your Canva slides still show the old photos. Want me to fill your template again?" [Yes, redo them] [Leave them]. On yes, read `parts/canva.md` and fill the carousel template again from the new order (its last step, `canva_kit.py note`, is what tells the Assistant the Canva slides are up to date again; the line then goes from the page by itself). apply prints this line every time it is true, whether or not it changed anything itself. The page tells them the same thing in its own words: say "redo the Canva carousel" to Claude; that phrase means this.
- **If it printed a line starting `Page:`**, they pressed the button and then moved photos again without pressing it. The slides are the order they pressed on; what they moved afterwards is still on the page, not made (the page has told them to press the button again when it's right). Say so and ask: "Your slides are in the order you pressed the button on. You've moved a few photos since. Want the slides the way the page is now?" [Yes, as it is now] [Leave them]. On yes: `arrange.py apply "<kit folder>" --piece carousel --as-it-is`.

They rearranged the carousel but never pressed the button, and tell you so ("I changed the carousel"): run the same `apply`. With no press waiting, it takes the order as they left it and makes the slides.

**If they would rather say it than drag it** ("swap the third slide for the one of the dog"): `kit.py picks "<kit>" --carousel ...`, then `kit.py build "<kit>"`. build makes the new slides and sets the earlier ones aside the same careful way, and says how many. A fresh pick from you replaces what is on the page, and the order they had is kept (`arrange.py earlier "<kit>" --piece carousel`). A carousel holds at most 20 photos (Instagram's limit); `kit.py picks` refuses more.

**If apply or build stops**, it says why in one line, and nothing was lost:

- **"...were not finished: ... run this again to finish"**: something about the drive stopped it half way (the drive is full, a folder can't be written to, the drive was unplugged). The line says which. Once that is put right, run the same command again and it finishes from where it stopped.
- **"... has a name a new slide needs"**: a file of theirs in the Carousel folder has the same name as one of the new slides (their drive treats capital and small letters as the same). Nothing was moved; if a move had already begun, the earlier slides were put back and the line says so. The line names the file. Tell them, and ask them to rename it or move it out: "There's a file in your Carousel folder called \"<its name>\" that has the same name as one of the new slides. Could you rename it or move it somewhere else? Then I'll finish." Then run the same command again. The page says the same without the file's name and sends them to you.

## Laying out the slides: the designer

The carousel has a second page, the designer, where the photographer builds finished slides by hand. Each box on it is one Instagram slide. Photos sit in frames they can move and stretch (a photo can run across two slides, so the slides join up when people swipe), and they can add their own words in text boxes with a font and a color, set the background, keep their brand colors, start from a template, look at a preview, and press **Save slides**. It is theirs to drive: you open it and step back. You never lay a design out for them.

**When to open it.** "Lay out my carousel", "design my carousel", "add text to my slides", "put words on the slides", "I want this photo across two slides", or anything else that means they want to arrange the slides themselves. They can also get there without you: the kit page's Carousel card ("Design my carousel →", or "Open my carousel →" once there is a design or a slide) opens it, and the carousel's picker shows "Lay out the slides" beside "Open the Carousel folder" once its slides are made. Not in the middle of a whole post kit being made. A shoot with no post kit has no designer; make the kit first (`parts/post-kit.md`).

```
python3 <scripts>/design.py open "<kit>"
python3 <scripts>/design.py show "<kit>"
python3 <scripts>/design.py took "<kit folder>"
python3 <scripts>/design.py tidy "<kit>"
```

**open** gets the page ready the way `arrange.py open` does (it numbers the photos if that hasn't been done, makes the small copies the page shows, and starts the page server if it isn't running). If the shoot has no design yet it makes the first one: one photo on each slide, in the carousel's order, called "<the shoot's name> carousel 1". It prints the page's address on its last line. Load that address in the Claude app's browser pane and bring the pane forward (`mcp__ccd_view__show_pane`, pane `preview`). Never Chrome. Then say one of two lines, by the line the command printed just above the address. If that line starts `NEW:` (the shoot had no design, so one was made just now), say: "The designer is on the page beside us, with one photo on each slide to start you off. Drag the photos where you want them, press \"+ Add text\" for your own words, and press \"Save slides\" when it looks right." If it starts `THEIRS:`, the design on the page is one they have already worked on, so never say it has one photo on each slide; say, with the name the command printed after "Opening": "Your design \"<name>\" is on the page beside us, just as you left it." and, when the command counted 2 or more designs, add: "Your other designs are under \"My designs\"." There is nothing to wait for and no question to ask.

**What the page does by itself.** Every change is saved to the kit as they make it, so closing the page loses nothing, and a shoot can hold many designs. "Save slides" makes each slide 1080 by 1350 and puts the whole set in the kit's Carousel folder, named after the design ("Beach carousel 1 - slide 01.jpg", "... slide 02.jpg"), with an "Open the Carousel folder" button on the same line. **Nothing made earlier is replaced:** the plain slides, Canva's slides, anything they put in that folder and every set saved before all stay exactly where they are. A design saved again after a change becomes a new set beside the first ("Beach carousel 1 (2)"). A new name for the design counts as a change (the new set carries the new name), and so does a photo they exported again from Lightroom, or a font that would not load last time and does now. Saved with nothing changed, the page says "Already saved as ..." and makes no file. A save that is cut off part way can leave some of its slides in the folder for the moment; their next press on Save slides finishes the set under the same name. If the page says a photo was exported again since it opened, they press Reload (it is in the bar at the bottom of the page, just above Save slides) and then Save slides: the page was still showing the old edit.

**Adding slides, and the templates (Nicole, Nov 10).** Two buttons sit side by side at the top. **+ Add slides** adds to the carousel they have open: whatever they pick goes after their last slide and nothing they made is replaced, one slide at a time or a whole template, up to Instagram's 20 slides (past that the page says "Instagram allows up to 20 slides." and adds nothing). **+ New design** starts another design and leaves the open one as it is. Both open the same window: Start fresh, **Single slides** (One with a border, Two stacked, Three across, Four, Nine, Playful mosaic, Mood board, Overlapping collage), Your templates, and Starter templates (Postcard collage, Layered trio, Overlay, column and frames, Across the seam into a collage and Gallery walk come first). So "add a slide", "add another photo slide" or "can I add to this template?" is: "Press \"+ Add slides\" at the top and pick what to add. It goes after your last slide." A slide that is added takes the design's own background, whatever the little picture shows. Film strip, Side by side, Big and small, Title slide and Four-photo grid are no longer offered; a design they made from one of those is still theirs and opens as before. Each of their own templates has a **Rename** link beside its name in that window (Return or a click anywhere else saves the new name, Escape leaves it); there is no command for it, and a template is never deleted. While they drag a photo with "Snap into line" ticked, blue lines and a small blue label show when it is centered on a slide, on the seam between two slides it sits across, or half way up ("Centered", "Centered side to side", "Centered up and down", "Centered on the seam"); holding Option on a Mac (Alt on a PC) while dragging turns that off.

**Which slides count as the carousel afterwards.** The kit page and the posting queue show the newest set they saved from the designer (the Carousel card shows its first six slides and counts them: "7 slides saved in the Carousel folder · 1 saved design"). Before they have saved any, the card shows one small picture of the design they last worked on, "A preview of <name>: your slides aren't saved yet" (stories: "A preview of your stories: they aren't saved yet"): a picture only, nothing in the folder and nothing to queue until Save slides (Save stories). If they then change the carousel's photos on the picker (or you run `kit.py build` on a new pick), the plain slides in the new order are what the kit page shows again; the laid-out slides stay in the folder untouched, and pressing Save slides on that design once more shows its set again. A Canva carousel fetched after a save is shown in its place, the same way. Nothing is ever taken out of the folder to choose between them.

**The words on a slide are theirs.** The rule in SKILL.md holds here as everywhere: the photographer writes what a slide says. You never invent a headline, a name, a date or a line for a slide, and you never put words into a design yourself. If they ask what a slide should say, ask what they want to say, and leave it there: you never suggest a line for a slide, not a cover line and not a reel's hook either (`parts/words.md`). The carousel's caption is a different thing; it lives on the carousel's words page, which step 2 on the kit page opens. Choosing which photos go in the carousel is still yours; where each one sits on a slide is theirs.

**A line `ARRANGED: <kit folder> | design | 7 slides | Beach carousel 1`** from `home.py pending` means they pressed Save slides. The parts are the kit's folder name, the word `design`, how many slides, and the name the set was saved under. There is nothing for you to make: the slides are already in the Carousel folder. Say one line, "Your 7 slides are saved in the Carousel folder as \"Beach carousel 1\". Nothing was replaced.", and run `design.py took "<kit folder>"`, which clears the line. Give it the kit's folder name exactly as the line has it; that works even if they have renamed the shoot's folder since. Never run `arrange.py apply` for a `design` line. A second save before you have said so leaves one line, naming the newest set.

**If they say a save didn't work**, `design.py show "<kit>"` lists the shoot's designs, every set of slides saved from them, and any save that was not finished. An unfinished one is finished by their next press on Save slides, under the name it already has. Never move, rename or tidy files in the Carousel folder yourself. Once a slide is in that folder it is theirs: if they rename, move or edit one, the Assistant leaves it and every other slide where it is.

**If the page says "Too many unfinished saves are waiting"** (it takes sixty presses of Save slides that were each stopped part way, with none finished in between), run `design.py tidy "<kit>"`. It clears the waiting slides of saves that were left alone for an hour or more; none of those ever reached the Carousel folder, and nothing in that folder is changed. Then say "That's cleared. Press Save slides again." The page also clears them by itself: after an hour when it is that full, and day-old ones each time the designer is opened or slides are saved.

**Fonts.** The font list comes from Google Fonts when the computer is online; five families work with no internet. A font that can't be loaded is shown in a plain serif and the page says so in one line, and Save slides asks before saving words that way.

**A single post: "make a single post".** One slide on its own is a single post (Nicole, Nov 6): there is no separate tool, and the designer's own line says so. "Make a single post", "a post with just one photo", "lay out a single post": open the designer as above (`design.py open "<kit>"`), and in place of the opening line above say: "The designer is on the page beside us. One slide on its own is a single post: press \"+ New design\", pick Blank, press − beside Slides until one is left, then drag your photo onto it, add your own words if you want, and press \"Save slides\"." Saved, it goes into the Carousel folder like any set. **Say this before they save it:** the kit shows one laid-out set at a time, the newest saved, so a single post saved after a carousel becomes what the kit page's Carousel card shows ("1 slide saved") and the posting queue lists it as a single post in place of that carousel. Nothing is taken out of the Carousel folder, and pressing Save slides on the carousel's design again makes the carousel the one shown (and the single post leaves the queue). In their words: "Saving it makes the single post what your post kit shows in place of your carousel. Your carousel's slides stay in the folder, and saving the carousel again brings it back." Whether a kit should hold a carousel and a single post side by side is for Dustin and Nicole to decide; until they do, it holds one. Afterwards, `design.py open` opens the newest design, which is now the single post: say which design is open and that the carousel is under My designs. The plain single posts the kit made are already in its Singles folder ("Open the Singles folder" on the kit page's quiet line); if one of those is all they want, point them there. The words on it are theirs, as on any slide.

## Making stories: the stories designer

The designer has a second shape for Instagram stories (Nicole's): each box is one 9:16 story, and they make the stories themselves, behind the scenes, a sneak peek, a few favorites, on the computer instead of on their phone. It is theirs to drive, like the carousel's designer: you open it and step back. **A shoot's stories come in sets** (Nicole's v26), the way a carousel has designs: each set has a name in the box at the top, "My story sets" lists this shoot's sets, and "+ New set of stories" starts another beside the ones they have. A set they made is never replaced by a new one. A shoot whose stories were made before sets has them as its first set, just as they were.

**When to open it.** "Make my stories", "lay out my stories", "design my stories", "add text to my stories", "put words on my stories", or anything else that means they want to make the stories themselves. They can get there without you: the kit page's Stories card opens it ("Make my stories →", "Open my stories →" once there are some), and the designer itself has a Carousel / Stories switch at the top. A shoot with no post kit has no designer; make the kit first (`parts/post-kit.md`).

```
python3 <scripts>/design.py open "<kit>" --kind stories [--design <id>]
python3 <scripts>/design.py show "<kit>" --kind stories
python3 <scripts>/design.py took "<kit folder>" --kind stories
python3 <scripts>/design.py tidy "<kit>" --kind stories
python3 <scripts>/design.py story-note "<kit>" [--design <id>]
python3 <scripts>/design.py story-fill "<kit>" --photos 3,8,12 [--design <id>] [--replace]
```

**open** gets the page ready as for the carousel and prints the page's address on its last line. With no `--design` it opens the set that was changed last; `--design <id>` opens one set by its id (`show` lists every set with its id and name). Load it in the Claude app's browser pane and bring the pane forward (never Chrome). If the line above the address starts `NEW:`, the shoot had no stories yet, and the page opens on its "Add stories" window; say: "Your stories are on the page beside us. Pick how the first one starts: a blank story, one photo per story, or one of the layouts. Press \"+ Add stories\" any time to add more after the last one." If it starts `THEIRS:`, say: "Your stories are on the page beside us, just as you left them." There is nothing to wait for and no question to ask.

**What the page does by itself.** "+ Add stories" puts what they pick after their last story and never replaces anything: a blank white story, one story each for the next five photos not used yet, one of Nicole's four story templates (**Gallery delivered**, four stories for delivery day; **My editing queue**, one story with no photo; **The full story**, ten; **Seamless swipe**, six, with one wide photo across the first three), or a layout (one photo, two up, three strips, four, six, nine or twelve, edge to edge), with the tick "Put this shoot's photos in the frames for me" filling the new frames with photos the stories don't use yet. The words that come with a template ("Gallery delivery day", "for your client's name", "Your words here") are placeholders for them to change: you never fill them in. **My editing queue** fills its list by itself from their editing queue as it is that minute: the shoots still to edit, in the queue's order, each as they named it ("1   Garcia wedding"), twelve at the most and then "+ 2 more", with the line "If your shoot is on here, your gallery is on its way." underneath. It is ordinary text once it is on the story: they restyle it, take a name off, or keep the look with Save as template. If their queue can't be read the window says "I can't reach your editing queue right now." and adds nothing. Each story's label ("Story 6") has a small x that removes that story, even an empty one, and the stories after it move up; "Remove this story" does the same for the story the selected thing is on (Undo brings either back; at one story both are off). **Redo** sits beside Undo (Cmd+Shift+Z or Cmd+Y; Ctrl on a PC), in the carousel's designer too. The stories stay at the top of the window while the photos scroll under them, so a photo far down the list can still be dragged up. "Show Instagram's guides" (on to start with) shades the top and bottom of every story, where Instagram puts its name, buttons and reply bar, so their words stay clear; the shading is never in a saved story. Text starts bold, black on a white box, in Classic (Figtree), and the Look buttons make it Plain, Box or Neon. "Preview" plays the stories the way Instagram shows them, with their Instagram handle at the top (from the grid they had read; "yourname" before that). "Save stories" makes each story 1080 by 1920 and puts the whole set in the kit's Stories folder, named after the set ("Beach stories - 01.jpg", "... - 02.jpg"; a second set, "Beach stories 2 - 01.jpg", or whatever they named it), with "Open the Stories folder" on the same line. **Nothing made earlier is replaced:** the plain stories, Canva's stories, anything they put in that folder and every set saved before stay where they are, and a set saved again after a change is a new set beside the first ("Beach stories (2) - 01.jpg"). The kit page then shows the set that was saved last as the stories, whichever set it came from, and says so; while no stories are saved yet, its card shows a small picture of the set they changed last. Everything else (saving as they work, "Already saved as ...", a photo exported again, a save cut off part way, "Too many unfinished saves", fonts and brand colors, Save as template, which keeps a story's templates apart from a carousel's) works as it does in the carousel's designer, above, with `--kind stories` on each command.

**"Pick my story photos for <shoot>"** (and "..., and follow my note") is the line the page gives them to copy, from **"Let my assistant choose the photos"** beside the photos' heading. The box there has "Tell your assistant what you want", which is optional; what they type is kept with the set, and the line ends ", and follow my note" only when they wrote one. Choosing and ordering the photos is your job (rule 11); the note is theirs and outranks your taste. Do it in this order:

1. `design.py story-note "<kit>"`. It prints **THEIR NOTE** word for word (or that there is none), the **SET** it is about with its id (the set they pressed Copy on, or changed, last, unless you name another with `--design`; with several sets it lists them, and if you can't tell which one they mean, ask one short question), and the **FRAMES** in the order they are filled, each with the story it is on ("stories 1 to 3" is one frame lying across three stories), whether it wants a tall or a wide photo or takes any, and the photo it already has.
2. `kit.py sheet "<kit>"` and read the numbered sheet once, with `shared/curating.md` in mind (two folders up from this skill): the best photos on their own merit, and a good mix that reads as a story from the first tap to the last (wide, then closer, a detail, a quiet one to end on; no near-twins side by side). One look at the sheet, never photo by photo (the core's rule 4). Follow their note first: "start with the big laugh, then the details" decides the order. If the note asks for something you can't find in the photos, say so in a line and pick the nearest; never pretend.
3. Give each frame a photo of the shape it wants (a tall frame a tall photo; the wide frame of Seamless swipe one wide photo that can stand being cut in three).
4. `design.py story-fill "<kit>" --photos N,N,N --design <id>`, the numbers in the frames' order. It fills the **empty** frames; a set with no frames at all gets one story for each photo, each filling its story. `--replace` puts your pick into every frame, also the ones that have a photo: use it only when they asked you to choose again or to redo photos that are already there. It refuses more photos than there are frames, a number that is not on the sheet, a photo given twice, and (without `--replace`) a photo that is already in the set, and changes nothing when it refuses. Running the very same command again is harmless: it answers `SAME:` and writes nothing, so if you are not sure a run went through, run it again. The set as it was before your pick is kept beside it; nothing is deleted.
5. The open page shows your pick by itself within a few seconds (it looks for it for ten minutes after they press Copy). If the page is not open, or it has not changed after a moment, run `design.py open "<kit>" --kind stories --design <id>` and load the address in the pane. Then say one or two lines, not a list of numbers: "Your stories are filled in beside us: six photos, opening wide on the lake, in close for the laugh, ending on the walk back. Move anything you like."

**You never touch the words.** `story-fill` changes photos only: it never adds, changes or removes a text box, and neither do you. If their note asks for words on a story ("put the date on the first one"), fill the photos and say: "I've put the photos in. The words are yours: click a story and press + Add text."

**The words on a story are theirs.** The same rule as on a slide: you never put words on a story, never write a line for one, and never suggest a name, a date or a moment they did not give you. "Behind the scenes" or "sneak peek" is theirs to type. If they ask what a story should say, ask what they want to say; they type it on the story themselves. Stories get no written words from you (`parts/words.md`), and there are no story lines waiting anywhere.

**A line `ARRANGED: <kit folder> | stories | 6 stories | Beach stories`** from `home.py pending` means they pressed Save stories. There is nothing for you to make. Say one line, "Your 6 stories are saved in the Stories folder as \"Beach stories\". Nothing was replaced.", and run `design.py took "<kit folder>" --kind stories`, which clears the line. Give it the kit's folder name exactly as the line has it.

**Canva is a separate finish.** Filling their Canva template (`parts/canva.md`) still starts from the kit's plain photos, never from laid-out slides. They can use either, or both; each leaves the other's slides alone.

## If the page can't show a shoot

The page says "I can't find that shoot's posts. Is the drive plugged in?" when the kit's folder isn't there, and "This shoot isn't ready for this yet" when its photos have never been numbered. `arrange.py open` fixes the second by itself (`design.py open` does the same for the designer, which says the same two lines). The reel page says it in Nicole's words: "I can't reach this shoot right now. Check that the photo drive is plugged in, then reload."; `reel_page.py open` numbers the photos the same way. A photo that can't be read right now (the photos are on a drive that is unplugged) still shows if its small copy was made earlier; if not, its tile says "Can't read this photo right now". Either way one line at the top of the page says why.
