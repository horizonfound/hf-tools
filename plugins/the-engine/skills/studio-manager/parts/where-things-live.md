# Where the Assistant keeps things

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too.

## What lives where

What the Assistant knows about the photographer is in `~/.studio/` (on a PC, `C:\Users\<their name>\.studio\`, the same folder in their own user folder; the scripts find it either way): `studio-file.md` (their drives and naming, readable) with `studio.json` beside it for the scripts, `cards.json`, the ledger of cards already backed up, and `queue.json` (the editing queue and every backed-up shoot; each shoot's `status` is `waiting` for in the queue, `archived` for an uploaded shoot that is not in the queue, `finished` for delivered, with `finished_at`, the day it was marked as delivered, and a delivered shoot they archived also carries `stored` and `stored_at`; a shoot taken out of the queue goes back to `archived` and loses its due date) with `queue-thumbs/` (small copies of three previews per queued shoot, so the queue page works with the drive unplugged). Everything about one backup is in its records folder: `<day folder>/_studio/` while the backup runs, then `<their place>/.studio/<day folder name>/` once it has settled (both hidden in Finder, and in File Explorer on a PC). Every script takes the old day folder, or the records folder, and finds the right place itself (`where.py`), so "rename the beach one" works the same before and after: `manifest.json` (every file and its hash), `index.json` (when each photo was taken, which camera), `groups.json`, `groups-sheet.png`, `shoots.json`, `names.json`, `sort-log.json`, `rename-log.json`, `report.html`, `first-look.html`, `previews/`, `groups/`, `from-the-card/` (camera housekeeping set aside, never deleted). They ask you for these; they do not go looking.

A post kit keeps its own records in its hidden `.kit` folder, inside the kit's folder in Your Content: `kit.json` (the kit's record: the picks as plain photo numbers, and `reel_marks`, the reel's Longer and Quicker marks beside them, and `reel_earlier`, your earlier reel picks, one line each, which is how the reel page tells an order they never touched from one they marked themselves; written only by `kit.py` and `arrange.py` commands, never by a page), `sheet.json` (the numbered list of the shoot's photos; every photo number comes from here; since Nov 17 a photo keeps its number for good: new photos take the next numbers, a photo gone keeps its number unused and its row moves under `retired` (its number and its name) for as long as it is gone, so the same photo put back takes its old number again, and the list carries `added`, `gone` and `back`; an open reel page has it made again by itself, through `kit.py sheet`, when photos are dropped into the Photos folder), `arrange.json` (the order of the reel and of the carousel as the photographer left each on its page: your first pick, the order now, the orders that were replaced, never thrown away), `arrange.lock` (an empty file the page and the commands hold while they write the order, so two never write at once; leave it where it is) and `previews/` (small JPEG copies of each photo that the page shows; the photos themselves are never sent to a page and never changed). For the reel page: `reel-page.json` (what they chose on it: their own order or yours, Longer and Quicker, the song, the vibe, the kind of reel it is (`kind`: `bts`, `photos` or `broll`, since Nov 17; a reel from before then goes by its footage setting), their note for the reel's photos (`photo_ask`: which photos they'd like you to pick from, each reel its own), their direction for how the reel should go (`direct`, Nov 17; read it with `reel_page.py note`), a B-roll reel's words on screen (`onscreen`: the words, the font, the style, where their middle sits as a share of the screen and their size), and every reel it made (each `made` entry since Nov 17 also notes `variation`, which mix it was, and `song_start`, where the song began, as the watch step played it; a B-roll video saved on the page with Save my video is a `made` entry too, with `video`, the file's name in the shoot's Reels folder, and `seconds`, and counts as made for the post kit and the queue); when a shoot has several reels, the one open on the page has its choices at the top of the record and the others wait in `versions`, each with its name and its own choices, clips and, after the first, your pick for it, with `current` naming the open one and each entry of `made` naming its reel; `moments_found` is every clip found for the shoot, which a new reel starts with; the first reel's pick of yours stays in `kit.json`; written whole, with `reel-page.prev.json`, the version before, beside it), `reel-page.lock`, `reel-job.json` and `reel-job.lock` (Make my reel's newest job and how it went), `reels/` (the reel's working files), `reel-preview/` (the watch step's own working files, a dry run of the same plan, apart from Make my reel's) and `reel-video/` (Save my video's own, the same dry run again before the video is made); `footage/` (what the page makes from their videos, never the videos themselves: `facts.json`, what each video's own header says; one folder per video, named by its key, with `preview.json` (how its preview went), `strip/` (a still every 2 seconds) and, only for a video that can't play on the page as it is, `preview.mp4`, a small 540p copy made once; `moments/<id>/`, the still on a moment's card; a look cut off part way leaves "strip 2" beside it, never a folder written over; `looks/`, the footage looks `footage.py` made, each in a folder of its own, "look", "look 2"... (`scan.json`, each video's stills in a folder named by its key, and the contact sheets, "01 <video> - sheet 01.png") and "close", "close 2"... for the close looks, and "picks", "picks 2"... for the sheets of their own clips that `footage.py picks` makes to name them, never cleared or written over), and in `reel-page.json` the moments (each naming its video by file name; Claude's are saved only by `footage.py moments`; a clip of their own (`by: "you"`, made on the editor's timeline) carries `named: true` once `footage.py describe` gave it its name, which a trim then keeps), the moments they removed (kept, so Undo can put one back), Claude's moments from earlier looks (`earlier_looks`, kept, never shown) and the style and the clips selected for each style; the shoot's own `Footage` folder, beside Photos, is theirs too (Add my footage makes it; a Footage folder that is a link is never listed or opened, and the videos in it are only ever read); the shoot's own `Music` folder, beside Photos, is theirs (Add my music makes it; a Music folder that is a link to somewhere else is never listed, opened or written into). A recording's sound is "<name> sound.m4a" beside it (".wav" on a PC); one that can't be read is left as it is and "<name> sound 2.m4a" made beside it. A recording replaced by a new one of the same name gets "<name> sound 2.m4a" too (then "sound 3", up to 99), each beside the ones before, none ever written over; the song list shows the recording once and none of its sounds. Which recording each sound was taken from is in the kit's hidden folder, `reel-sounds.json` (the sound's folder and file name, the recording's file name, its size, its time and a hash of its first megabyte, never a path; written whole, with `reel-sounds.prev.json`, the version before, beside it, one writer at a time through `reel-sounds.lock`); a sound with no line there goes by the files' times (a sound at least as new as its recording is that recording's). A song they remove on the reel page (**Remove this song**, Nov 17) goes to the computer's own Trash with every sound taken from it, through `this_computer.py`'s `trash` (below), and its lines in `reel-sounds.json` are dropped, the version before kept; the reel that had it picked goes back to no song, and nothing else is written down about it. While a sound is being made it sits under a hidden name starting with a dot and ending ".part-<number>"; one left behind by a make that was stopped is the Assistant's own half-file, never a song. In CapCut's projects folder, a reel is made in a hidden folder (".<name>.making-...") and only takes its name once every file is in it; one left behind by a stopped make has no project in it. For the carousel's slides: `slides-older/<the date and time>/` (the plain slides a new order replaced, moved here and never deleted, with `moved.json`, the record of what moved, written before the first file did; a folder holding only `moved.json` is the record of slides being put in with nothing to set aside; a record that ends "undone" is a move that could not be finished and was put back), `slides-new/` (where new slides are made before any of them goes into the Carousel folder; slides that were made here and never put in, because a photo failed or the run was cut off, are the Assistant's own scratch and the next run clears them and makes its own), `arrange-job.json` (how the page's own slide-making went: being made, done, already in that order, or the plain words of a problem; the page reads it) and `arrange-job.lock`, `slides.lock` and `kit.lock` (empty files held while slides are being made or the kit's record is being written, so two runs never move files or write the record at once; leave them where they are). Beside the Studio File in `~/.studio/`: `arrange-waiting.json` (which kit's order is waiting for you after they pressed "I'm happy with this order", and which kit's laid-out slides were just saved; `home.py pending` prints each as an ARRANGED line) and `arrange.log` (what the page's requests carried, for reading after a problem).

The designer (the page where they lay a carousel's slides out, `parts/arrange.md`) keeps a shoot's designs in the same hidden `.kit` folder: `designs/<id>.json` (one design: its name, its layout, a counter that goes up with every change; saved as they work, written only by the page server and `design.py`), `designs/<id>.prev.json` (that design as it was before its last change) and `designs/earlier/` (each design as it stood at the end of a day; nothing is thrown away, and there is no way to delete a design). `designs/<id>.preview.jpg` (and the same in `story-designs/`) is a small picture of that design's first slides, which the designer's page sends after each save and whenever the design is opened, so the post kit's Carousel or Stories card can show the design they are working on before any slides are saved; it is the Assistant's own picture, replaced each time, never counted as a design, and nothing of the photographer's. `designs/sets/<id>-<a code>.json` is the record of one press of Save slides: the set's name, its files with their hashes, and the layout that made it, written before the first slide is moved ("moving"), then "done"; "undone" means it could not be finished and was put back. A press of Save just this slide (Nicole's v27) leaves a record of the same shape with `one`, the slide's number, and `again`, which time round it was saved: its one file goes into the Carousel folder as "<the set's name> - slide 03 updated.jpg" ("... updated 2.jpg" the next time; a story's "<the set's name> - 03 updated.jpg" in the Stories folder), beside the set, never over anything, and nothing in `kit.json` changes for it (`design.py show` lists it as "Slide 3 on its own"). `design-staging/` is where finished slides wait until every one of a set has arrived; nothing in it is the photographer's, and a set that never finished arriving is cleared once it is a day old, the next time the designer is opened or slides are saved in that shoot (`design.py tidy` clears ones left alone for an hour). `design-slides-older/` holds the rare slide that had already gone into the Carousel folder when its set could not be finished and was put back: those are set aside there under their own names, never cleared. `design.lock` is an empty file held while a design is written; leave it where it is. **The laid-out slides themselves go into the kit's Carousel folder**, the folder the photographer sees, as "<the design's name> - slide 01.jpg" and so on, beside the plain slides and Canva's; a later save of the same design is "<the design's name> (2) - slide 01.jpg". Nothing in that folder is replaced or removed to make room, and a slide they rename, move or edit there is left exactly as they made it. `kit.json` gains `laid_out` (every set saved, with its files) and `carousel_set` (which slides the kit page and the posting queue show as the carousel: the newest laid-out set, or plain again after a new order from the picker); both are written only by commands. Three things are shared by every shoot and live beside the Studio File in `~/.studio/`: `design-templates/<id>.json` (a template they saved: a layout with empty frames, no photos in it), `brand.json` (their brand colors, up to 16) and `design-fonts.json` (fonts they loaded by name, listed under "Yours" in the designer). The stories designer (the same page for Instagram stories, `parts/arrange.md`) keeps a shoot's sets of stories apart from its carousel designs, in the same shapes: `story-designs/<id>.json`, one file for each set of stories (the stories a shoot had before there were sets are simply its first set, in the same file), with its own `sets/` and `earlier/`, and `story-staging/`, `story-slides-older/` and `stories.lock` in the hidden `.kit` folder. A set's record may also hold `ask`, the note they wrote for you under "Let the Assistant choose the photos" (their words, up to 600 letters; read it with `design.py story-note`, never from the file), `asked`, the time they last pressed Copy there (how `story-note` and `story-fill` know which set they mean when none is named), and `filled_by` once `design.py story-fill` has put your pick in. A carousel design's record (`designs/<id>.json`) may hold the same three since Nicole's v27 (`ask`, `asked`, `filled_by`; read with `design.py note`, filled with `design.py fill`). Its saved stories go into the kit's Stories folder named after the set, "<the set's name> - 01.jpg" and so on (the same set saved again after a change: "<the set's name> (2) - 01.jpg"), beside the plain and Canva stories, nothing replaced; `kit.json` gains `stories_laid_out` (every saved set) and `stories_set` (which stories the kit page shows: the set saved last); and a story's templates are in `~/.studio/story-templates/`, apart from a carousel's.

A talking reel (`parts/talking-reel.md`) has one folder of theirs and the rest hidden. **Theirs:** `Talking`, inside the kit's folder beside Photos, Footage and Music, made and opened by Add my video on the talking reel page; the videos of them talking go there and are only ever read. **In the kit's hidden `.kit` folder:** `talking.json` (the record: which video, their cuts, splits, speeds, zooms, the photo and video stretches, the captions' spot and size, who places the photos, and what was made; when a shoot has several talking reels, the one open on the page is at the top of the record and the others wait in `versions`, each with its name and its own video, edit and photos, with `current` naming the open one; written whole by the page server and by `talking_rec.py` commands, one writer at a time through `talking.lock`), `talking.prev.json` (the record as it was before its last change), and inside the record `earlier` (each other video's own edit, brought back when they go back to that video) and `replaced` (what a `--replace` or a second Edit my video replaced, the last 12); a record that can't be understood is set aside as `talking.damaged-<date>-<time>.json`, never written over. `talking-words.json` holds every word they said with its times, the sentences and the video's facts, written once per video and never by a save from the page (one writer at a time through `talking-words.lock`); `talking-words.prev.json` is those words as they were before they were last made again. Inside `earlier`, a video marked `made-again` is one whose words were made again while the record was on another video, so its kept edit starts afresh when they go back to it. `talking/<video key>/preview.mp4` is the small copy of a talking video that the page plays (540 on its short side), and `talking/facts.json` the videos' facts (written one at a time through `talking/facts.lock`): **each talking video, and each one exported again, leaves one small copy, up to about 1.3 GB for a 30-minute talk, and they are kept on purpose** (nothing is deleted; say so plainly if they ask where the room on the photo drive went). A small copy that couldn't be played is set aside beside it as `talking/<video key>/preview.unusable-<date>-<time>.mp4`, kept, never deleted; it counts toward the same room on the drive. The job files are the Assistant's own working files, each replaced by the next: `talking-job.json` and `talking-job.lock` (the job that turns a video into words, the lock held for as long as it lives), `talking-make-job.json`, `talking-make-job.lock` and `talking-make-status.json` (a Make it in CapCut from the page and what the maker said happened), `talking-make.json` (the last plan handed to CapCut's writer; it has real paths in it, so it is never shown on a page) and `talking-make.lock` (held while one make writes). **On the computer's own disk, in `~/.studio/talking/`** (never on the photo drive, so it is there when the drive is unplugged): `Assistant Transcriber.app` (the name the Mac's Speech Recognition box and System Settings show), the small app the Mac's Speech Recognition runs in, built on their Mac the first time; `built.json` (what it was built from, so it is rebuilt only when that changes); `permission.json` (what the Mac last said about Speech Recognition, which `talking_rec.py setup --check` prints); `older/` (an earlier build set aside, never deleted); `turn.lock` (one talking job at a time on the computer); and `work/<job>/`, the scratch sound of a job at work, removed when the job ends, or when the job is stopped for taking too long. A job cut off outright (a power cut, a force quit) can't tidy up: its sound stays there until the Assistant next sees that the job is gone (the next look at that shoot's talking page, usually) or, at the latest, until the next talking video is turned into a script, and is cleared then. Nothing else of the photographer's is kept there. What went wrong, in the Mac's own words, is only ever in `~/.studio/logs/talking.log`. The made reel is a CapCut project, "<shoot> talking reel", in CapCut's own projects folder like any reel. Asked "where is my talking reel?", the plain answer is "on its page, with that shoot's post kit, and in CapCut once you've pressed Make it in CapCut", never a file name.

A B-roll reel saved on the reel page (Save my video, Nicole's v27) is a file of theirs: `Reels`, inside the kit's folder beside Photos, Footage and Music, made by the first save and opened by the page's Open the folder, holds the finished videos, "<short> B-roll.mp4" (" - <reel name>" when the shoot has several reels; a second save is "... 2.mp4"). Nothing in it is ever written over, and you never move, rename or copy a video in it; the page writes a half-made file under a hidden name first and gives it its real name only when it is whole.

The words for a post (`parts/words.md`) are in the kit's hidden `.kit` folder too: `post-words.json` (for the reel and for the carousel: what they answered, the drafts of the caption, the hooks and which one they picked, a caption of their own; written only by `words.py` commands and the words page) and `post-words.prev.json` (the record as it was before its last save; nothing is thrown away). Asked "where is my caption saved?", the plain answer is "with that shoot's post kit; it's on your words page", never a file name. Their Brand Homebase is one file with one exact name, `brand-homebase.md`, in the Assistant's folder on their drive (the folder that holds Your Content); the hidden `.studio` folder inside that folder and `~/.studio/` are looked in after it. A `.txt` or a PDF beside it, or a brand file of another business of theirs, is not read. `words.py show` prints the place it would be read from when there is none.

The posting queue and posting-day reminders (`parts/posting-queue.md`) are in `~/.studio/`: `posting.json` is the queue (every piece, its light, its date, the photo it shows in the grid; a reel of a shoot with several reels also carries `version`, which reel it is, and `reel_name`, and every reel after the shoot's first has a piece of its own whose `id` is the shoot's reel piece's with the reel's id after it, as in `...-reel-v2`; a piece they marked Posted carries `posted_at`, the day they said so, and one switched back to Not posted (`unposted`) has `posted_at` cleared and goes to the end of the line with a new date, its reminder fields kept so the same calendar event follows it), `feed.json` and `feed/` are their Instagram grid as it was last read (`read_at` is when; the posted pieces the posting page still shows in the grid are those with a `posted_at` after `read_at`, or in the last 30 days when the grid was never read), and it also holds the reminders: `remind` (the calendar they chose and the time last used) and, on each reminded piece, `remind_on`, `remind_time` and three fields that say which event on the calendar is ours (`event_uid`, `event_sig`, `event_cal`; written only by a reminders round, never by hand). `posting-thumbs/` holds a small picture per piece. `reminders/` is the reminders' own folder: `made.json` (which event was made for which post, noted the moment it is made), `last.json` (what the last round said), and on a PC the calendar files themselves, one per reminded post, named like the reminder ("Post your Beach reel today (3b854a).ics"), with `state.json` (which files have not been opened since they last changed). Asked "where did that calendar file go?", open it for them (the command is in `parts/posting-queue.md`, "On a PC") rather than sending them to a hidden folder. `logs/reminders.log` holds the real words of a calendar fault; it is for you, never for them.

**A second drive named for one backup (`night.py --second`) gets its copy the same way, after the shoots are sorted and put away (Nov 19 review; Nov 23):** `night.py` runs `second_copy.py` at the end, so trouble with that drive never stops a backup. When the copy is refused before a byte is written (no room, say), the reason is kept in the backup's records in `second-missed.json` (`words`, never a path). The same file holds the one-time note that a second drive saved by an earlier version is no longer used by itself (`asked_each_time`; the Studio File's `drives.second_asked_each_time` remembers it was said, and the saved drive stays on file, unused). A backup named for the day ("thursday card") that meets another card's backup of the same day takes the next name ("thursday card 2").

**A second copy made after the backup (Nov 19; like the photo drive since Nov 23).** On the other drive: the same shoot folders and photo names as on the photo drive (Photos and Videos inside a shoot that has both), and one hidden folder, `.studio-second/<the backup's name>/`, that holds a copy of the backup's `manifest.json`, a copy of `second-copy.json`, `from-the-card/` (what the sort set aside, as in the backup's own records) and `set-aside/` (the computer's own hidden notes from a folder that emptied after a rename; never deleted). A backup still in its day folder is mirrored inside a folder of that name, and moves up when the backup settles. In the backup's own records: `second-copy.json` (how far the copy has got: `layout` ("main": like the photo drive), `place` (where it goes), `folders` (the folders the copy made itself), each file checked or not, where it is on the second drive (`at`) and whether it was read from the card or the drive, any problems, the line the report shows, and `follow` (waiting for its drive, or a problem, after a rename); an earlier copy to a different drive is kept as `second-copy.older-<date>.json`), `second-follow-log.json` (every rename made on the second drive after a rename or a join on the photo drive, written before it is done), `manifest.before-second-copy.json` (the backup's manifest as it was before it learned of the second copy, kept once), and `second-missed.json`. The backup's `manifest.json` names a second copy only once every file of it is checked, with `second_layout: "main"`. **A second copy made before Nov 23** has no `layout` and no `second_layout`: it is one folder named like the backup ("2026-11-19 thursday card") holding every file at its place on the card, with a hidden `_studio` folder inside; it is left exactly as it is. On the computer itself: `~/.studio/second-copy.lock` and `~/.studio/second-copy.running.json` (one second copy, or one follow, at a time; which copy is running), and its log in `~/.studio/logs/second copy.log`.

**The report's other options (Nov 19) keep no record of their own.** This shoot continues on another card writes the same `shoot.json` into each card's records that `shoot_report.py link` writes. Taking a card off again (`shoot_report.py unlink`) keeps each `shoot.json` as it was beside it first, as `shoot.before-unlink-<when>.json`. Change how my shoots are named and Use a different drive next time change the Studio File (`~/.studio/studio.json`: `naming`, `drives.session`, and a dated line under `notes` for the drive); the renames it asks for are in each backup's `rename-log.json`, as every rename is. Write a note to my client only shows a line to copy.

**A first backup's place (Nov 19).** The first backup chooses where shoots go by itself and saves it in the Studio File like any drive (`studio_file.py defaults`); nothing is saved until the backup's checks have passed. When they chose the computer itself, shoots go in a folder called `Shoots` inside their Pictures folder, made the first time, with a note in the Studio File saying so.

**The vault (Nov 19).** The vault is a real folder in Your Content, like any post kit: `My vault` (or `My vault 2` when a shoot already has that name), with its own Photos, Footage, Music, Talking, Carousel, Stories and Reels folders, which are theirs. What makes it the vault is the mark in its record, `.kit/kit.json` (`"name": "My vault", "vault": true`); it is found by that mark, never by its name, and it is never one of the shoots in a list of shoots. It is made only by `vault.py ensure` (or `vault.py add`, or the press of Open the post kit on My vault's card, which does the same), never because a page was opened. Every other post kit is in it by itself, archived or not, and **no photo is copied to be in it**: its numbered list, `.kit/sheet.json`, is references. Each row has the usual number, file, size and shape, plus `kit` (the folder name of the post kit the photo is in; empty for a photo in the vault's own Photos folder) and `src` (that post kit's own number for the photo). Two shoots may both hold IMG_0001.jpg; in the vault they are two rows with two numbers. `vault.py` is the one writer of that list (`kit.py sheet "My vault"` and an open reel page both go through it), the list before is kept as `sheet.prev.json`, and `.kit/vault.json` holds what keeps the numbers still: `numbers` (which vault number each photo has, by `<kit folder>#<its number there>`, or `own:<file name>` for the vault's own), `next` (the next number to give), `out` (what was taken out of the vault with Remove from my vault or `vault.py out`, kept so it can be put back at its old number: `photos`, each under the same key its number is kept by, and `clips`, each as `<shoot's folder>:<the clip's id>`, a clip made in the vault itself as `:<id>`, with when it was taken out; taking something out or putting it back writes only this file, with the file as it was before kept as `vault.prev.json`, and never a photo, a video or a shoot's post kit) and `from` (what the list was made from, so a list that is up to date is not made again). A photo keeps its vault number for good; one that is gone keeps its number unused under `retired` and comes back to it; a post kit whose photos can't be reached right now keeps its rows. A post kit with no numbered list of its own yet is not in the vault until it has one (`kit.py sheet`, or opening that kit): **the vault only reads other post kits and never writes anything into one.** A post kit whose folder is renamed by hand keeps its photos' vault numbers and what was taken out of the vault: the kit's own record still holds the folder name it was made under, and the vault moves its own keys from the old name to the new on its next relist (never when two kits claim the same old name, or a kit by the old name is still there). A clip a vault reel had already taken from that shoot still points at the old name and needs taking again. The file as it was before any write of `vault.json` is kept as `vault.prev.json`. A `vault.json` that can't be read at that moment stops the command with nothing changed ("I can't read your vault's list of photo numbers right now"); one that is damaged or gone is put back from the list beside it, and the damaged file is kept as `vault.damaged-<date>.json`. The same for the vault's own record: while it can't be read no second vault is made. Everything else the vault keeps (its small copies in `.kit/previews/`, its reels, designs, words, its contact sheets in `.kit/reels/`) is where any post kit keeps it, in the vault's own folder. Photos added to the vault itself (`vault.py add "<folder>"`, or + Add a shoot on its page) are real copies in the vault's own Photos folder, each checked against its original; the log of every such copy is `.kit/vault-adds.json` (each run's folder, its `status`, `copying` until it is finished, and each file's name in that folder, `size`, `mtime`, `sha256` and the name it got), written before the first byte is copied (a file's `state` is `copied`, `there` (already in; with `in`, the post kit's folder, when the vault has that very photo from a shoot and no copy was made), `could not read`, `did not match` or `no name free`). A copy that did not read back as its original is never left among the photos: it is kept in `.kit/copies-set-aside/` as `<name>.studio-mismatch`. And `.kit/vault-add-job.json` is only what the page follows while a copy runs. `vault.py show` says where it is and how many shoots and photos it holds, `vault.py members` lists the post kits in it, `vault.py relist` brings its list up to date, `vault.py out` lists what was taken out (and takes one photo or clip out), `vault.py back` puts one back.

**The vault's sheets (Nov 19).** `vault.py relist --sheets` draws the whole vault's numbered contact sheets into the vault's hidden `.kit/reels/`; `vault.py relist --sheets --only "<numbers>"` draws a shortlist's sheets into `.kit/reels/shortlist/` beside them (48 to a sheet, each photo under its own vault number). Both are the Assistant's own scratch pictures; read only the sheets the command just printed. The vault's reel page keeps nothing new for its photos step: the note is `photo_ask` in the reel's record, as in any post kit.

**The vault's clips (Nov 19).** A video is never copied to be in the vault. The vault's reel page lists the videos in the vault's own Footage folder and then every shoot's, a shoot's named `<shoot's folder>/<file name>`; a clip in a vault reel names its video `Footage/<shoot's folder>/<file name>` and, when it was added from a shoot's clip, keeps `from: {kit: <shoot's folder>, id: <that clip's id>}` in the vault's own `reel-page.json`. The facts, stills and small copies of a shoot's video that the vault needs are the vault's own, in `<vault>/.kit/footage/`, made the first time that video is wanted; nothing is written into the shoot's post kit. What they typed under "Or tell the Assistant what to look for" is `vault_ask` in the reel's saved choices. A video saved from a vault B-roll reel is in the vault's own Reels folder.

**What is in each photo, for search (Nov 19).** Each post kit keeps `.kit/photo-words.json` in its hidden folder: per photo number, `key` (the photo's name, size and time, so a photo exported again is looked at again), `tags` (this computer's own: Apple's Vision on a Mac, from the small copies only; `tags_by`, `tags_at`; `unread` when no picture could be read), and `words` (your description, only when they asked: `words_by`, `words_at`, and `words_key`, the photo it was written for). The version before is `photo-words.prev.json`; it is written whole under `photo-words.lock`. A save never starts from a file it could not read: if `photo-words.json` can't be read at that moment nothing is saved (try again), and a damaged one is kept beside it as `photo-words.damaged-<date>.json` while the save starts from the version before. `photo_words.py` is its one writer; **no photo is ever renamed or changed for it.** The vault's photos that come from a shoot have their words in that shoot's own file, under the shoot's own number; the vault's own file holds only the photos added to the vault itself. `photo-words-job.json` and `photo-words-job.lock` beside it are the background look's own notes (how far it has got), and its log is `~/.studio/logs/photo-words.log`; `~/.studio/photo-words.lock` keeps it to one kit at a time. On a PC there are no tags (no Vision), only your descriptions. These words are for finding photos and nothing else: never a source for a caption, a note or a hook. The search rule itself (everyday words ignored, every other word must match) is in `vault.py`, and `vault.py find` is how you search from the chat.

The home base (`parts/hello.md`) stores almost nothing of its own. The Back up a card screen reads every backup's own records where they are now (the editing queue's `queue.json` names each backed-up shoot; the backup's `manifest.json` says whether the card is safe to format and whether a second copy was made; `shoot.json` in a backup's records links the cards of one shoot). The Make posts screen reads the kits in Your Content, and keeps one small list, `~/.studio/kit-archive.json`: the folder names of the post kits they archived by hand (`kit-archive.prev.json` beside it is the list before the last change). An archived kit is only on that list; its folder is never moved. `~/.studio/home-thumbs/` holds three small pictures per kit for that screen, made the first time they are shown. The home page keeps three small files of its own (the third, the hello, is described just below): `~/.studio/home-recap.json`, whether they hid the monthly recap card (`"hide"` is a month like `2026-11`, `all`, or empty; `home-recap.prev.json` is the choice before), and `~/.studio/faces.json`, where the faces are in the small copies of a kit's photos, so the hello's photos are framed on the people in them (found by the Mac's own Vision on this computer, nothing sent anywhere; it is only a memory of what was already looked at, and deleting it just means looking again; a PC has no face finder, so there the photos are centered). The page's hello (Nicole's v30) is kept in `~/.studio/home-hello.json` (written whole, the version before in `home-hello.prev.json`): the greeting and line it chose and when, so a reload within about three hours keeps it, the post kits it has seen, the last few it said (never the same twice running) and their last visit (for the welcome-back lines after two weeks away). Deleting it just means a new hello is chosen. Sample photos for a brand-new Assistant, if the plugin carries any, are in the plugin's own `samples` folder (`samples.py show` lists them); they are only read, count in nothing, and stop showing once the first post kit is in Your Content. **+ A new shoot** (the home page's tiles, `new_shoot.py`) keeps a log in the Assistant folder's hidden `.studio/new-shoots.json` (each picked folder, the shoot it made, every photo's name in the kit and its hash, written before the first copy) and what the page follows while it copies in `~/.studio/new-shoot-job.json` (state, words, how far; the shoot's folder name, never a path); a copy that did not read back as its original is kept, never deleted, in the kit's hidden `copies-set-aside` folder. A shoot made this way says `"source": "picked"` in its `kit.json`. The monthly recap and Your year (`wrapped.py`) keep no record of their own: every number is counted fresh from the kits' own records, the posting queue and the backups. A story saved as a picture goes into **Stories to share**, a folder inside their Assistant folder, beside Your Content (made the first time a story is saved; each picture gets a new name, never over one that is there). Whether a kit's photo fan has played is kept in the browser pane too. If that list is ever unreadable (damaged, or changed by hand into something else), the next Archive or Bring it back sets it aside whole as `kit-archive.unreadable-<date>-<n>.json` in the same folder and starts a clean list; the list before stays as it was.

**Video clips (Dustin, Nov 11).** A shoot that came off the card with photos and video clips has two folders inside its own: `Photos` and `Videos`, each numbered from 0001 in shooting order, a photo's sidecar beside its photo. A shoot of only photos, or only clips, is one folder with the files right in it, as always. A rename carries both folders along. In the backup's records, a still from a clip (for the report and the contact sheet) is in `groups/` beside the photo previews, and `groups.json` lists it under `clip_previews`; each group there also carries its count of `photos` and `clips`. On a PC no stills are made yet, so a shoot of only clips shows no picture. A sideways clip turned upright in the backup copy is in the records' `clip-rotations.json` (which clip, the turn, where in the file the orientation note sits, the bytes before and after, and the hash of the file as it is now); in `manifest.json` and `index.json` that clip's `sha256` is the file as it is now and `card_sha256` is what the card holds, with `turned` saying the turn. The card itself, and a second copy on another drive, are never turned.

- **Which version this is, and whether a newer one is ready (Nov 17):** `release.json` at the top of the installed plugin (a number, a date, a few lines of what is new); GitHub's copy of the same file is the newest. `~/.studio/update-check.json` remembers when GitHub was last asked, what it said, and a "not now". `updates.py` reads and writes both; nothing else does.

**Nothing of theirs is ever deleted (Nov 17).** You never delete a file or a folder of the photographer's, and the Assistant keeps no Trash of its own. The one way anything of theirs leaves its place is the computer's own Trash: Finder's Trash on a Mac, the Recycle Bin on a PC (`this_computer.py`'s `trash`, the only door: it refuses a link, a path outside the one folder it is told to work in, and a drive with no Trash, and it is never a delete). What went there is theirs to bring back: open the Trash in Finder and choose Put Back (on a PC: open the Recycle Bin in File Explorer and choose Restore). Nothing is written down about what went there. You have no chat command that moves anything to the Trash; asked to delete something, say you never delete, and that moving it to the Trash is theirs to do in Finder (File Explorer).

Reels brought over from the older pages (`bring_older.py`, `parts/post-kit.md`): the older pages kept a shoot's reel in `.studio/reel-pages/<shoot folder name>.json` and its talking reel in `.studio/talking/<shoot folder name>.json`, in the hidden `.studio` folder beside Your Content; those files are only ever read. A reel that was brought says where it came from in the kit's own record: `brought_from` in `reel-page.json` (per reel id) and in each brought talking reel inside `talking.json`, holding the older file's name and the reel's id there, never a path. `<kit>/.kit/brought-older.json` is the note of each run: what it was about to bring, written before any record changed (a shoot whose photos had no numbers yet is numbered first, by `kit.py sheet`, which also keeps its own working list in `~/.studio/reels/`), and what it did. A reel brought over keeps the id it had on the older pages (v1, v2...) when that id is free, since a post in `posting.json` names its reel by that id; an empty reel may hold the place of one that could not be brought.
