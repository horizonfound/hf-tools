# Finished photos in, with no card

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too. After the photos are sorted, read parts/post-kit.md for the kit.

## Finished photos in: "make my posts" / "I've got photos" / "I dropped something in" / "can you sort these"

The second front door. The Assistant made them a folder at setup (or the first time they ask, including "set up my Assistant folder", which the home page tells them to paste): **The Assistant** on their photo drive (or in Pictures), with **Drop a shoot here** and **Your Content** inside. `posts.py show` says where it is; no folder yet → `posts.py places` for the choices and one question, "Where should your content live?" [<their photo drive> (recommended)] [Pictures, on this Mac] ("on this PC" on a PC; `posts.py places` says which) [Other], then `posts.py setup --where "<that>"` (on a PC, the top of a drive is passed as `"E:/"`, and Pictures may be in OneDrive: `parts/on-a-pc.md`, "Where their posts live") and tell them: "Made a folder called The Assistant on Big Kahuna. Drag any folder of finished photos into Drop a shoot here and tell me. Everything the Assistant makes for you lands in Your Content."

They drag anything into Drop a shoot here: one tidy shoot, three shoots in one folder, a year of chaos, screenshots mixed in. **Look before asking:** `posts.py look`. It reads the photos' dates, splits them into groups by day and by the gaps between photos, draws one contact sheet (a row per group), and prints what it sees. Read the sheet once so you can say what each group is ("a beach session", "a family at the park"), never "group 1". Then say it back in plain words and ask **one** question with buttons:

> I looked through the 140 photos you dropped in. They're from three different days: a beach session on June 24 (62), a family at the park on March 14 (41), a wedding on May 3 (29), and 8 with no date on them, probably screenshots. What did you have in mind?
> [Separate them into three shoots] [One compilation from all of it] [Just the beach one] (Other: tell me)

Then `posts.py sort`: `--separate g03=Beach "g02+g04=Garcia wedding"` (merge groups that are one shoot with `+`; a group nobody names gets the folder it came in as, or "Shoot", plus the date), `--one "Spring favorites"`, or `--only g03=Beach` (the rest stay in the drop box). Photos with no date: `--undated g03` to put them with a shoot, `--undated own --one "Phone screenshots"` for their own folder, or leave them (the default) and say so. The photos are **copied** into Your Content and every copy checked against the original (a hash of each file, like the backup); nothing is moved, nothing deleted. What they dropped stays in the drop box exactly as they dropped it, because Finder (and File Explorer on a PC) moves a folder dragged within the same drive, and a photographer who did that by accident needs it right there to drag back (Dustin, Oct 30). Say what happened: "Copied 132 photos into Your Content: Beach (62), Garcia wedding (29), Park (41), every one checked. What you dropped is still in the drop box; drag it back where it came from if you moved it in by accident, or clear the drop box whenever you like." Photos the Assistant already copied are left out of the next look (it says so), so a drop box nobody cleared is never offered twice. **Dragging is the risky way in** (Finder, and File Explorer on a PC, moves files dragged between two folders on the same drive, and nobody knows that rule), so the first way in is the computer's own folder picker: the home page's "Select the folder with your finished photos" button opens it (on a PC it opens Windows' own folder window; if that window can't open, the page asks them to tell you the folder, see `parts/on-a-pc.md`; it is never "drag it in"), and `posts.py bring "<folder>"` copies only the finished photos into the drop box under the folder's name, checked, originals untouched, heavy files named and left. When they say where their photos are in words ("make posts from my Final Exports folder", "they're in Pictures/Aston Martin"), do the same: find the folder, `posts.py bring`, then the look. If someone did drag and their folder emptied, tell them what Finder (File Explorer on a PC) did and how to put it back (click in the Drop a shoot here window and press Cmd+Z, Ctrl+Z on a PC, or drag them back), then carry on with the copy. **Only finished photos are ever copied** (JPEG, and HEIC/PNG for phone photos and screenshots); RAW files, TIFFs, Photoshop files, Lightroom catalogs and videos stay in the drop box, and the look names them: "Left where they are, not copied: 12 RAW files, 3 TIFFs, 1 Photoshop file." Never offer to copy those; if they ask, say the Assistant works from finished JPEGs and those stay where they are. A name already in Your Content stops it (exit 6): ask for another name. If the same drop was cut off, the next look or sort finishes it first. A bring that stopped part way (a file held open, a full drive) carries on into the same drop-box folder when run again; nothing is copied twice.

One shoot in the drop: no "what did you have in mind"; just the name ("I'll call this one Beach, June 24. Say if it's something else.") and sort. Then straight into the kit for it (or for each, if they want kits for all; ask once: "Want post kits for all three, or just one to start?"). A whole year dropped in: say the number and offer the sort once ("2,300 photos from 31 days. Want me to sort the whole lot into shoots in Your Content so it's done once? Takes a few minutes."), nothing moved until they say yes.

Everyday phrases: "what's in my content folder" → `posts.py list`; "show me the beach kit" → the kit page in the pane; "redo the captions" → the words step again with whatever they added; "make the reel for the park shoot" → the reel skill's Steps 1 and 2 (your pick, then the reel page, where they add the song, pick the vibe and press Make my reel).
