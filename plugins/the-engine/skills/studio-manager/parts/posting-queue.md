# The posting queue and the Instagram grid

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too.

## The posting queue: every social piece, with a light on it

Every reel or carousel the Assistant helps with is in the posting queue with a light: **red** not started, **yellow** in progress (the Assistant did its part; they're finishing it in CapCut or Canva), **green** ready to post. Only green pieces get a date and count in the tank. Nothing posts, ever: dates only.

- **"The beach reel's ready" / "the carousel is done" / "it's exported":** a reel or a carousel normally becomes ready to post when they press **Add to my queue** on its post kit page or its words page, and that button only works once the piece is made and has its caption. `posting_queue.py ready` does not check for a caption: it marks the piece ready whatever it has. So look first: `words.py show "<kit>" reel` (or `carousel`) prints what is written. With no caption, don't mark it ready silently; offer the words in one line ("It has no caption yet. Want to do its words first? Two minutes, and then Add to my queue is right there.") and follow `parts/words.md`. If they say no, or it already has its caption, or it is a single post (those have no words page): `posting_queue.py ready "beach reel"`. It gets the next spot in line and a date at their rhythm; say both back: "Down for Saturday. You've got about two weeks of posts ready." If the command also prints a line about a reminder, say that too (see the reminders section below).
- **"I posted the beach reel" / "that one went out":** `posting_queue.py posted "beach reel"`.
- **"How much content do I have?" / "how am I doing on posts?":** `posting_queue.py tank`, said back in one line.
- **"Post the carousel first" / "move the beach reel up":** `posting_queue.py move "beach reel" --to 1`; the dates follow the order.
- **"Change my posting schedule to every other day":** `posting_queue.py interval "every other day"`; the dates are redone from the front.
- **"Show my posting queue" / "show my grid" / "what's lined up to post":** the page, in the Claude app's browser pane only: run `posting_queue.py serve` in the background if `~/.studio/posting-url.txt` (on a PC, `C:\Users\<their name>\.studio\posting-url.txt`; `parts/on-a-pc.md` says how to find it) has no live server, load that address in the pane and bring the pane forward. The page shows the tank, their grid as it will look (newest on top, drag to reorder and the dates follow), and the three lists; Ready, Posted and Skip work on the page too. A click on an upcoming post in the grid opens that post's panel (next section).
- **"Read my Instagram grid" / "read my grid again" / "put my real feed in there" (and the first time the posting page comes up with no feed in it, offer it in one line):** the Assistant reads their profile page in the Claude app's browser pane and keeps their last posts, full size, behind the grid. Steps: (1) the handle: `posting_queue.py feed` says it if it is known; if not, ask once ("What's your Instagram handle?") and keep it with `posting_queue.py feed --handle <name>`. (2) **Before the page opens, say what they are about to see** (Nicole's first run, Nov 5: a box from Instagram, "See photos, videos and more from...", Sign up / Log in, appeared over her grid and she thought the Assistant was logging her in): "I'm opening your Instagram page beside us. Instagram may put a sign-up box over it. You don't need to log in or do anything; I can read your grid behind it." Then open `https://www.instagram.com/<handle>/` in the pane. That box over a grid that is still there behind it is not a problem: carry on. Only if there is no grid at all behind the wall (the snippet returns nothing) say so and let them sign in there once; the pane keeps it. Claude's own "Stay signed in to your sites / Import cookies" offer is separate and never needed for this: if they ask, Not now. (3) Run the snippet `posting_queue.py grab-script` prints, in that tab, with the pane's javascript tool. It scrolls once so the grid fills in and returns a small list: which post, the address of its full-size picture, reel / carousel / pinned. The page sends nothing anywhere. Save exactly what it returns to a file in the scratchpad and run `posting_queue.py feed --import <that file>`: the Assistant fetches each picture on this computer with its own curl, which comes with a Mac and with Windows 10 and 11 (about a second for all of them on a Mac; never yet tried on a PC) and says "Your grid, read Wed Oct 28: the last 12 posts on @handle." (4) Show the posting page (the page servers restart themselves when the Assistant has been updated, so there is nothing to stop or start; a page that says "One moment" comes back by itself in a couple of seconds), and say what was read in one line. Pinned posts stay at the top of the grid, the way Instagram keeps them; the green-lit pieces come next, newest first; then the rest of their feed, up to five rows. **The page cannot hand the tiles over itself** (instagram.com's page rules refuse any connection to 127.0.0.1, and the app's browser pane refuses local connections from an outside page too, websockets included), so do not try the POST route, a websocket, or the clipboard; the list through the javascript tool is the way, and it is the fast one. Outside the desktop app, with no pane: they can open their profile in any browser, paste the snippet into the browser's console, copy what it prints into a file, and run `feed --import` on it.

## Two shoots whose names are nearly the same

Both can be in the posting queue: each kit's pieces are kept apart by the folder they came from, so adding the second never touches the first. What does not work is finding one by a few words when the words fit both.

- `posting_queue.py add --kit "beach"` with two kits that fit stops with `could mean more than one: ... Say which.` Run it again with the folder's whole name, as the line printed it.
- `ready`, `posted`, `move` and the rest stop the same way when the words fit two pieces. Use more of the name. When the two pieces carry the very same name (the list then shows it twice), no words can tell them apart: ask which shoot they mean in their own terms (the date, who it was), read `posting.json` in the Assistant's own folder, find the piece whose `kit` is that shoot's folder, and give the command that piece's `id` in place of the name.
- Never guess which of the two they meant, and never mark one ready to see what happens. On the pages there is nothing to tell apart: each kit's own **Add to my queue** button and each post in the grid is its own.

## A post's panel: its grid photo, its words, its reminder

On the posting page the outlined posts in the grid are their upcoming ones (ready to post, with a date). A click on one opens its panel right under that row; **Done** closes it. A post that is not ready yet has no panel, and neither has a single photo from the Singles folder (one photo, no words page), so neither has a reminder button. When they ask where something in this section is, that is the answer: "Open your posting queue, click the post in your grid, and it's right under it."

**Its words.** The panel has **Copy caption**, **Copy my hook** (a reel's; it says "Pick a hook on your words page first." until one is picked) and **Open my words**. With no caption it says "No caption yet." and offers **Write my words**. These words are read from that post's words page; nothing is written or changed in the posting queue. "Change the caption", "a different hook", "it's too long": that is `parts/words.md`, every time. The panel and the reminder pick up the change by themselves.

**The photo it shows in the grid.** The strip in the panel is that post's own photos; a click picks one, and the grid shows it at once. What the page tells them, and what you say if asked:
- A reel: "Pick the photo your reel shows in your grid. When you post it, tap Edit cover and choose the same photo so your feed matches." The pick only changes this page. Instagram shows whatever cover they choose when they post, so they choose the same one there.
- A carousel: "Instagram shows a carousel's first slide in your grid, so pick the slide you want people to see first." If they pick a slide that is not the first, the page warns that their real grid will show slide 1 instead; to match, they move that slide to the front in the carousel designer (`parts/arrange.md`).
- "I can't find this post's photos right now. Open its post kit once, then try again." means the kit's drive is unplugged or its photos' small copies are not made yet: have them plug the drive in, show the kit page once, and try again.
There is no command for picking the photo. It is done on the page.

## Posting-day reminders: on their calendar, with the caption inside

On the day a post is due, their phone can tell them, with the caption in the reminder ready to copy. Each reminded post is one short event on their calendar on its posting day, at the time they chose for that post, with an alert; its title is "Post your <shoot> <kind> today" and its notes hold the reel's picked hook, the cover to choose, and the caption. Nothing posts: it is a reminder to post it themselves.

These are not the lines `posting_queue.py reminders --mark` prints at the start of a conversation. Those are said in the chat. These sit on their calendar.

**Offered, never assumed.** No post has a reminder until they ask for one on that post. Never turn one on for them, never suggest a time as if it were set, and never copy one post's time to another: each post gets its own.

**It is set on the page. The chat cannot set one.** There is no command that turns a reminder on, changes its time or turns it off. (Which calendar they go on is the one thing the chat can change: "Changing the calendar", below.) So when they say **"remind me to post the beach reel"** (or "set a reminder", "change my reminder to 8", "turn that reminder off"):
1. Make sure the piece is ready to post and has a date (`posting_queue.py list`). A post with no posting day has no reminder button: there is no day to be reminded on. If it isn't ready, that comes first (above).
2. Show the posting page and say where the button is: "Click the beach reel in your grid. Under it there's **Set a reminder to post**. Press it, then **Remind me**, and pick a time."
3. What they will see: "Want a reminder on its posting day?" [Remind me] [I'll post it myself]; the first time on a Mac, "Which calendar does your phone show?" (below); then "What time?" with 9 am, Noon, 6 pm, 8 pm, or a time of their own and **Set**. The page says "Reminder set" and the button turns into the bell with the day and time. **Change time** and **Turn off** are in the same place.
Don't say a reminder is set until `reminders.py status` says so.

**After that it keeps itself in step.** A new date (a move, a new rhythm), an edited caption or hook, "not ready yet", "posted" and "skip" all change or remove the event without anyone asking. On a Mac the commands return at once and may print "(The reminder on your calendar is being brought into step in the background.)"; say it in plain words ("I've moved its reminder too") only after `python3 <scripts>/reminders.py status` shows the new day.

**"Is my reminder set?" / "what reminders do I have?":** `python3 <scripts>/reminders.py status`, said back in plain lines. It says which calendar, and for each reminded post its day and time and where it stands: on the calendar, waiting until the post is ready and has a date, waiting for a calendar to be chosen, or not on the calendar yet.

### The first time on a Mac: which calendar, and the Mac's own question

- **"Which calendar does your phone show?"** is asked once, on the page, with their calendars as buttons. The right answer is the calendar they see on their phone (often iCloud's or Google's), since that is the one that makes the phone speak up. If they don't know, have them open the Calendar app on their phone and read the calendar names there. The choice is kept for every later reminder. `python3 <scripts>/reminders.py calendars` lists the same names in the chat, but it asks the Calendar app, so run it only when they ask.
- **Changing the calendar** ("I picked the wrong calendar", "put my reminders on my Google calendar", or a reminder that fails with "That calendar isn't in Calendar any more"). The page asks the question only once and has no button to ask it again, so this is done from the chat, and only when they ask or the old calendar is gone: (1) `python3 <scripts>/reminders.py calendars` for the names; (2) ask them, with the names as buttons, "Which calendar does your phone show?" (never choose for them, and never guess from a name that sounds right); (3) `python3 <scripts>/reminders.py calendar "<the name, exactly as listed>"`. It prints "Reminders now go on the <name> calendar. They were on <the old one>.", and the reminders already made are moved there in the background (only the Assistant's own events; nothing else on either calendar is touched). A name that isn't on the list changes nothing and prints the list. Say it is done only after `reminders.py status` names the new calendar for each post. Not tried on the real Calendar app yet, least of all with a calendar that was deleted: if a reminder is still on the old calendar afterwards, or there are two, say so plainly and have them delete the stray one in Calendar.
- **The Mac asks permission the first time**, in a box of its own, whether this may control Calendar. Tell them before they press Remind me, so it is no surprise: "Your Mac will ask once whether this can use your Calendar. Say yes (OK or Allow); that's how the reminder gets there." While the page waits it says "Finding your calendars…" and, if it takes a while, "If your Mac asks whether this can use your Calendar, say yes."
- **If they said no** (the page says "I can't reach your Calendar right now." and that this Mac hasn't been allowed to use Calendar yet): it is turned back on in **System Settings, Privacy & Security, Automation**: find the app they run the Assistant in (Claude) and switch **Calendar** on under it. Then **Try again** on the page. The Mac does not ask a second time by itself, so this is the only way back.
- Nothing is made before a calendar is chosen. Until then `reminders.py status` says "No calendar chosen for reminders yet."
- Honest about what is proven: the real Calendar app and the Mac's permission box have not been tried yet, only a pretend calendar. If what they see differs from this, believe the screen, help from what it says, and tell them plainly it is new.

### "Reminders not updated" and "I couldn't update your calendar just now"

A command may end with a line in brackets, `(reminders not updated: ...)`, or the page may say "I couldn't update your calendar just now." with **Try again**. What it means: the posting queue itself is saved and right (the date, the order, ready or posted all happened); only the calendar is behind. Nothing is lost and nothing is doubled.

1. Don't run the queue command again. It worked.
2. `python3 <scripts>/reminders.py status` to see which posts are behind.
3. If the words name the cause, fix that: Calendar not allowed (System Settings, above); "That calendar isn't in Calendar any more" (they choose another: "Changing the calendar", above); "Calendar took too long to answer" or "wasn't open and couldn't be started" (wait a moment). A post whose kit drive is unplugged keeps its day and time; its caption is put in once the drive is back.
4. Then try again: **Try again** on the page, or just show the posting page (it brings the calendar into step every time it opens), or `python3 <scripts>/reminders.py sync`. Exit 1 with the same line means it still can't; say so plainly and leave it. The real words of the fault are in `logs/reminders.log` in the Assistant's own folder, for you, never for them.
5. Tell them in one line, only if a reminder they are counting on is affected: "Your beach reel is down for Saturday. Its reminder hasn't moved yet; I'll get that sorted."

### On a PC: a calendar file they add themselves

A PC has no Calendar app the Assistant can talk to. There a reminder is a calendar file (one per post) that they open and accept in their own calendar app. The page's words change to match: "I'll make a calendar file to add to your calendar, with your caption in it.", then **Add to my calendar**, later **Add it again** after a change and **Take it off my calendar** when it is turned off. There is no "which calendar" question: their calendar app asks when the file opens.

In the chat: commands that change a reminded post print, for example, "Reminder changed, ready to add to your calendar again: ..." and "1 calendar file is waiting to be opened in your calendar app. Nothing changes on your calendar until then." Say that to them, and offer to open it: `python3 <scripts>/reminders.py open` opens every waiting file in their calendar app (it prints "Opened: ..." or "Nothing is waiting to be opened."). Only run it when they are there to say yes to it; it puts a window in front of them.

Say these limits plainly, before they rely on it. None of it has been tried on a real PC yet.
- Nothing is on their calendar until they open the file and accept it, and again after every change (a new date, a new time, an edited caption). On a Mac that happens by itself; on a PC it never does.
- Their calendar app decides whether a changed file replaces the earlier event or adds a second one. If they see two, they delete the older one.
- Turning a reminder off makes a file that cancels it. Their app may ignore it; then they delete that one event by hand.
- **The alert may not come through.** Google Calendar's import and Outlook are reported to drop the file's alert or swap in their own default. The event can be there and make no sound. Have them open the event once and check it has an alert.
- Google Calendar is a website: the file has to be imported there by hand (Settings, Import).
- A reminder they deleted by hand may not come back from the same file.
- The Assistant cannot see their calendar. `reminders.py status` can say "opened in your calendar app" or "waiting to be added to your calendar"; it can never say the reminder is really there. Never tell a PC photographer "your reminder is set": say "the file's ready" or "it's been opened in your calendar app; check it's there and has an alert."
