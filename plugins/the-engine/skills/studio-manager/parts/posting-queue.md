# The posting queue and the Instagram grid

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too.

## The posting queue: every social piece, with a light on it

Every reel or carousel the Assistant helps with is in the posting queue with a light: **red** not started, **yellow** in progress (the Assistant did its part; they're finishing it in CapCut or Canva), **green** ready to post. Only green pieces get a date and count in the tank. Nothing posts, ever: dates only.

- **"The beach reel's ready" / "the carousel is done" / "it's exported":** `posting_queue.py ready "beach reel"`. It gets the next spot in line and a date at their rhythm; say both back: "Down for Saturday. You've got about two weeks of posts ready."
- **"I posted the beach reel" / "that one went out":** `posting_queue.py posted "beach reel"`.
- **"How much content do I have?" / "how am I doing on posts?":** `posting_queue.py tank`, said back in one line.
- **"Post the carousel first" / "move the beach reel up":** `posting_queue.py move "beach reel" --to 1`; the dates follow the order.
- **"Change my posting schedule to every other day":** `posting_queue.py interval "every other day"`; the dates are redone from the front.
- **"Show my posting queue" / "show my grid" / "what's lined up to post":** the page, in the Claude app's browser pane only: run `posting_queue.py serve` in the background if `~/.studio/posting-url.txt` has no live server, load that address in the pane and bring the pane forward. The page shows the tank, their grid as it will look (newest on top, drag to reorder and the dates follow), and the three lists; Ready, Posted and Skip work on the page too.
- **"Read my Instagram grid" / "read my grid again" / "put my real feed in there" (and the first time the posting page comes up with no feed in it, offer it in one line):** the Assistant reads their profile page in the Claude app's browser pane and keeps their last posts, full size, behind the grid. Steps: (1) the handle: `posting_queue.py feed` says it if it is known; if not, ask once ("What's your Instagram handle?") and keep it with `posting_queue.py feed --handle <name>`. (2) **Before the page opens, say what they are about to see** (Nicole's first run, Nov 5: a box from Instagram, "See photos, videos and more from...", Sign up / Log in, appeared over her grid and she thought the Assistant was logging her in): "I'm opening your Instagram page beside us. Instagram may put a sign-up box over it. You don't need to log in or do anything; I can read your grid behind it." Then open `https://www.instagram.com/<handle>/` in the pane. That box over a grid that is still there behind it is not a problem: carry on. Only if there is no grid at all behind the wall (the snippet returns nothing) say so and let them sign in there once; the pane keeps it. Claude's own "Stay signed in to your sites / Import cookies" offer is separate and never needed for this: if they ask, Not now. (3) Run the snippet `posting_queue.py grab-script` prints, in that tab, with the pane's javascript tool. It scrolls once so the grid fills in and returns a small list: which post, the address of its full-size picture, reel / carousel / pinned. The page sends nothing anywhere. Save exactly what it returns to a file in the scratchpad and run `posting_queue.py feed --import <that file>`: the Assistant fetches each picture on this Mac with the Mac's own curl (about a second for all of them) and says "Your grid, read Wed Oct 28: the last 12 posts on @handle." (4) Show the posting page (the page servers restart themselves when the Assistant has been updated, so there is nothing to stop or start; a page that says "One moment" comes back by itself in a couple of seconds), and say what was read in one line. Pinned posts stay at the top of the grid, the way Instagram keeps them; the green-lit pieces come next, newest first; then the rest of their feed, up to five rows. **The page cannot hand the tiles over itself** (instagram.com's page rules refuse any connection to 127.0.0.1, and the app's browser pane refuses local connections from an outside page too, websockets included), so do not try the POST route, a websocket, or the clipboard; the list through the javascript tool is the way, and it is the fast one. Outside the desktop app, with no pane: they can open their profile in any browser, paste the snippet into the browser's console, copy what it prints into a file, and run `feed --import` on it.
