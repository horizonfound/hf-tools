# The sneak peeks: step 8b of a backup, and picking again

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too.

8b. **When it stops with exit code 7, it needs your picks: the sneak peeks.** The backup has already thinned each shoot to its moments (one frame per burst, at most a hundred, spread across the shoot) and laid them on numbered pages of twenty (`_studio/peek-sheets/<shoot>-p1.png`, `-p2.png`...); it prints which pages belong to which shoot. First read their taste notes, `python3 <scripts>/peek_sheets.py taste` (what they've said about past picks: "more wide shots," "nothing backlit," which frames they swapped in); they outrank the general advice below. Then read every page of a shoot (a few images, never the photos one by one: rule 6), and choose **six per shoot, always** (Dustin, Oct 28), the way a photographer picks a client preview: every frame one they are glad to see, the crops mixed and leaning wide, light and color that sit well together, no near-twins. Order them for the report's mosaic: the hero first (a scroll-stopper that survives a square crop; it gets the big slot), two that set it off, three that keep changing (a wide, a medium, a close, a detail, a different look); no two neighbors alike. If they told you about the day ("the twins turn two", "she was nervous about the wind"), let that steer what you pick. **Then the close look, always (Dustin, Oct 28: two of six picks were a soft-focus frame and half-closed eyes, which the small pages hide).** From the pages, shortlist ten to sixteen per shoot (two or three candidates for each slot) and lay them out big: `python3 <scripts>/peek_sheets.py closer "<backup>" s1=61,64,71,18,11,9,... s2=...`. It writes `_studio/peek-sheets/<shoot>-close1.png`, `-close2.png`... with four photos per page at full preview size. Read every one. At that size, drop without mercy: the subject soft or out of focus (sharp background, soft face); eyes half-closed, mid-blink or rolled back, unless they are plainly shut on purpose; a mouth caught mid-word; a stiff or awkward pose; a crew member, a light stand or a stray hand in the frame; a hand or foot cut off by the edge. Only then choose the six from what is left, in mosaic order. Takes a minute more per shoot and that minute is the whole difference. Then `python3 <scripts>/peek_sheets.py pick "<backup>" s1=14,3,27,9,41,18 s2=...` (hero first) and run the same command again: it marks each pick in Lightroom with the keyword "sneak peek" (a new sidecar beside the photo; the photo itself is never changed; a sidecar already there is left alone), makes their previews, and the report shows them as the shoot's mosaic, with the line "I pulled six to get things rolling. Not feeling them? Just say what you'd rather see." and two buttons: **Pick again** (a box for what they'd rather see; it waits for you, see "First thing, every time" in SKILL.md) and **Show me more** (the close-look shortlist as a grid; they click six in order and press Use these, and the report's server saves them as the photographer's picks, marks them, sets aside the keyword of any dropped pick, and rebuilds the report; nothing for you to do). Both feed their taste notes, so the next first pick is closer. **They are a first pass, never a verdict:** when they say "pick different ones," "more of the kids," "I don't love these," it's the same as Pick again with that note: `peek_sheets.py ask --shoot --note` (so the note is kept), then re-pick straight away. If they ask, that is the whole idea: edit those few tonight, send them to the client with a note, and the rest can wait. For a backup done before this existed, `peek_sheets.py make "<backup>"` then `pick`, then `sneak_peeks.py --night` and `report_page.py` by hand.
