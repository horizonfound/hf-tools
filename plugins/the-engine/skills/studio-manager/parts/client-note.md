# The note to the client: step 8c of a backup

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too.

8c. **The note to the client, one per client shoot, to go with the sneak peeks.** A shoot gets a note when it is a client shoot, and the per-shoot screen decides that: a due date means a client, "just for me" means no note (see "When they come back to the report" in `parts/after-the-report.md`). So the note is written right after that screen, once you know the shoot's name and who it is to; if they named the shoot up front ("backup now, the Garcias"), you can write it during the backup and only ask who it is to afterward. How they write comes from setup (`client_note.py status` says on; if it says "never asked" because the setup was done before this existed, ask Question 5 from `parts/first-backup-setup.md` once, now).
   To write it: `client_note.py facts --night "<backup>" --shoot s1` gives you their sample or tone and sign-off, the shoot's name, day, the count, the due date from the editing queue in words, and "they_said": what the photographer told you about the shoot (at "What's on the card?", or since). **The note is written from "they_said" and nothing else** (the words are theirs, see the rules). If "they_said" is empty or doesn't cover this shoot, ask before writing, one plain question, no buttons: "What do you want to say to Sarah? Tell me about the shoot and anything you'd want in the note, however you'd say it, and I'll put it together." Wait for the answer; `client_note.py told --night "<backup>" --text "..."` keeps it word for word. Never write a note from the photos. Rules:
   - Short. Three or four short paragraphs at most: a hello, what's attached, what they told you to say, when the rest is coming, a thank-you.
   - Their details, their way: use what they said the way they'd say it, tidied. Never invent a specific: a name, a moment, what the client did or felt, a price, a date; the delivery date comes only from the queue's due date in words ("in about two weeks"), otherwise leave it as "soon" or out. Facts you may add as such: how many photos are attached, and the day of the shoot. Courtesies you may add: "it was so great shooting with you," "thank you for trusting me with this," "can't wait for you to see the rest."
   - If what they said is thin ("it was great, send her the usual"), write exactly that plus the courtesies, short and warm in their voice; thin and true beats padded. Don't ask twice. **Never assume** who the client is to anyone, what the shoot was for, or how it went; if the note needs it and they didn't say it, leave it out or ask.
   - Their voice: with a sample, match its greeting, length, rhythm, punctuation and sign-off, down to the exclamation marks or the lack of them; with a tone, write it that way. Sign it exactly as they sign.
   - Several shoots: a different note for each, from what they said about that shoot; the same note with the names swapped is wrong, and so is one shoot's story in another's note.
   - Nothing sends. Ever.
   Save it: `client_note.py save --night "<backup>" --shoot s1 --to "Sarah" --subject "<one short line>" --body-file <the draft saved to a file>`. That puts it on the report under the sneak peeks with a Copy button and rebuilds the report (the open page picks the change up by itself within a few seconds). Say one line: "Your note to Sarah is on the report. Edit the sneak peeks, copy the note, attach them, and send." If they want it changed ("shorter", "don't mention the dog", "more me"), rewrite and save again; `client_note.py show` prints what is there. A "just for me" shoot gets a note only if they ask ("write a note for the beach shoot"); then ask who it is to and write it.
