# After the report: each shoot, names, and renaming

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too.

## When they come back to the report: about each shoot

**Ask nothing by yourself (Dustin, Nov 19).** The backup may have finished while they were away. When they come back, there are no questions waiting for them: not what to call a shoot, not when it is due, not what else they are editing, not whether they want a tap before a due date. The report offers each of those as a button, and they can say any of them. Answer what they bring up, and nothing else.

The editing queue and the archive are simply part of the Assistant, never a yes/no: every shoot the Assistant backs up lands in Uploaded shoots on the queue page, and a shoot is in the queue when they add it with a due date. (If `edit_queue.py status` says "never asked", run `edit_queue.py setup --on` quietly; "off" only if they once said so.)

**What they already said stands.** If they said what is on the card when they said "backup now" (the name, who it's for, when it's due), use it: name the shoot from it, and never ask for it again. If they said something that doesn't fit the card ("the Smith family" but the card plainly holds two different days), say so in one line and ask.

**What each shoot has on the report (Nicole, Nov 13).** Under its name box: **Open this folder**, which opens that shoot's own folder (so they can look through the photos and import them into Lightroom), with what happened said right under the button; then the line "Does everything look right? If so, add it to your editing queue." above **Add to my editing queue**. When the card held more than one shoot, the top of the shoots says why there are several folders ("Since these were two days, I put them in two separate folders. You can name them in the boxes below." when each shoot was a day of its own; "Since these looked like two different shoots..." when it was not days). Under the shoots the page says the next step: "Open your folder to start editing. When your edits are done, come back to your home base and click Reels, Carousels, Stories or Captions." If they ask "where are my photos" or "how do I get them into Lightroom", point them at Open this folder on that shoot. If the button says "I can't find this folder anymore. If you moved or renamed it in Finder, tell the Assistant where it went.": there is no command that re-points a backup's records, so say so plainly, and that moving or renaming the folder back makes the button work again. Never offer a reel from a backup; the old closing line about turning favorites into a reel is gone.

**The due date is a button on the report, not a question (Dustin, Oct 28).** Under each shoot's name box sits "Add to my editing queue": one click opens a calendar right there, they pick the day, press Save, and the shoot is in the queue with that date (the report's server runs `edit_queue.py` for them; the card then says "In your editing queue, due Nov 11" with "Change the date" and "Show my editing queue", which opens the queue page right there). Never ask when a shoot is due; the page does it.

**What they can say instead of pressing, and what you do then:**
- **A name** ("the big one is the Smith family"): the name box and Rename are on the page; when they say it to you, rename it (below).
- **A due date** ("add the beach one to my queue, due the 14th"): `edit_queue.py add --night "<the backup's folder>" --shoot <id> --due <date>`.
- **A note to the client:** not part of a backup any more (Nicole and Dustin, Nov 23). Never offer one. If they ask outright, `parts/client-note.md` says what to do: write it in the chat from what they tell you, and save it nowhere.
- **Shoots they are already in the middle of editing** ("I'm also editing the Lees, due Friday"): `edit_queue.py add-manual --name "..." --due <when> [--shot YYYY-MM-DD]`; if one of them is a shoot the Assistant already backed up, use `add --night` instead so it gets its thumbnails. Never ask whether there are any.
- **A heads-up before a due date** ("tap my phone before things are due"): if this session can create a scheduled task, set up a daily one (late morning, their time) that runs `edit_queue.py reminders --mark` and sends each line it prints with the notification tool; if it cannot, say the heads-up is on the home page's My editing queue card and the editing queue page (the chat never opens with it). Remember the answer in the Studio File. Never offer it by yourself.
- **A second copy of this backup** ("make a second copy of the beach backup onto LaCie"): the section below, "A second copy, after the backup".
- **Another card of the same shoot, another way of naming folders, another drive next time:** each is a button on the report and something they can say; the section "What the report lets them change", below, has both.

## A second copy, after the backup

**Nobody is asked about a second copy before a backup (Dustin, Nov 19), and nothing about it is remembered (Nicole, Nov 23: "ask each time").** It is offered afterwards, on each backup's report, and they can say it any time. Which drive is asked every time; no drive is saved for the next backup.

**On the report.** Under the shoots is a section, "Anything you'd like to change?", with one line: "Your photos are safe either way. These are here if you want them." Its button **Make a second copy** opens a small panel right there: "Which drive should the second copy go on?", every other drive that is plugged in by its name and free space, **Look again**, and **Make the second copy**. There is no tick box for every backup any more. What happens is said beside the button: "Copying to LaCie Rugged: 240 of 3,931.", and "Keep your card in until this is done." while it is reading the card. When it is done the report says so in place of the button. You do nothing for a press on the page; if they ask how it is going, the same command below with `--check` tells you, or look at the report.

**From the chat** ("make a second copy of the beach backup onto LaCie", "copy that card to my other drive too"):

```
python3 <scripts>/studio_file.py drives                                  # the drives plugged in, to find the one they named
python3 <scripts>/second_copy.py "<the backup's folder>" --to "<that drive>" --check
python3 <scripts>/second_copy.py "<the backup's folder>" --to "<that drive>"     # in the background
```

- **Which backup:** the one they name; with no name, the newest. `"<the backup's folder>"` is the folder you use for its report (the card ledger's, or its records in the hidden `.studio` folder).
- **Run `--check` first, every time.** It writes nothing. If it says REFUSED, say its line as it is and stop: every line is plain words with the drive's name in it. It refuses a memory card (this backup's own or any other: anything a camera has written to), the drive the photos are already on (a copy there is not a second place), a folder inside the backup, a drive without room, a backup that has not finished, stopped with a problem or was a sample, and a second copy that is already being made. If the backup already has a second copy it says so and where.
- **Then run it in the background** and say one line: "Making a second copy on LaCie Rugged now. If your card is still in, leave it in until I say it's done." When it ends, say its last line as it is.
- **What it makes (Nicole, Nov 23: "it should look just like the main drive"):** on that drive, the same shoot folders with the same photo names as on their photo drive (Photos and Videos inside a shoot that has both), a photo's sidecar beside it. What the sort set aside (the camera's own housekeeping files) and the copy's own records are in a hidden folder there, `.studio-second`. Only the files the backup itself copied are in it: anything they added to a shoot's folder afterwards (edits, exports) is not. It can only be made once the backup is sorted into shoots; before that the check says so.
- **It follows a rename or a join.** When a shoot is renamed, or two are joined, on the photo drive, the second copy takes the same folders and names by itself (renames on that drive only; nothing is copied again, removed or written over), and the rename's last line says so: "Your second copy on LaCie Rugged has the same folders and names as Big Kahuna again." If that drive is not plugged in, the line is "Your second copy on LaCie Rugged still has the folders and names from before. Plug LaCie Rugged in and it will catch up.": say it as it is. If another second copy was being made at that moment, the line is "Your second copy on LaCie Rugged still has the folders and names from before. Another second copy is being made right now; this one catches up as soon as that is done.": say it as it is. The report says the same under Second copy made (and while it does, it does not say the copy has the same folders and photo names), and the copy catches up the next time the report is open or a backup runs with that drive in. To do it now: `python3 <scripts>/second_copy.py follow "<the backup's folder>"` (or `follow --all`). If it says something is in the way (a folder or file of theirs on that drive under a name the copy needs), say its line; never move, rename or delete their file to make it pass: they do that, and it is tried again by itself the next time the report is open or a backup runs (or run `second_copy.py follow` then, to do it at once). A follow that was cut off part way (the computer went off) is finished the same way; the report says "was part way through taking its new folders and names" until it is.
- **A second copy made before Nov 23** is the earlier kind: one folder on that drive named like the backup ("2026-11-19 thursday card") with every file at the place and under the name the card gave it. It is left exactly as it is, never converted, and it does not follow a rename; its report says so ("This second copy was made the earlier way: its folders and names are the memory card's, not your shoots'."). Say so if they go looking for shoot folders there. `second_copy.py follow` answers that it is left as it is.
- **Where the files are read from:** the card when it is still in and is the card of that very backup; otherwise the checked copy on their drive. A video clip the backup turned upright is always read from their drive, since the card still has it sideways and the second copy holds what their drive holds. Either way every file is checked against what the backup recorded for the file on their drive. Its last line says which, and you say it too, never more than it says: "It was made from the copy on Big Kahuna, since the card wasn't in.", "5 video clips are the upright ones from your drive." and "It has the same folders and photo names as Big Kahuna."
- **Exit codes:** 0 done (or nothing to do). 2 refused before anything was written. 3 stopped part way (a drive was unplugged or filled up): say its line; nothing was removed, and the same command again carries on where it stopped. 2 is also the answer when that drive already has a folder with the name of one of the backup's shoots that the copy did not make ("LaCie already has a folder called Smith Family_10-18-26 that isn't part of this second copy."): nothing is copied into a folder of theirs; they rename it or pick another drive. 1 problems: a file already in the copy's own folder is different (never written over), or a photo on their drive no longer matches the backup. Say the line it printed and stop; never delete or move anything to make it pass. **Photos changed since the backup:** when the card is not in and photos on their drive have been saved over since the backup (an editing program writing into a JPEG, say), the check refuses up front and says so: those photos are still on their drive, they are not lost, and a checked second copy needs the card. Say its line as it is; never say photos "could not be found" when it says they were changed.
- **"After every backup" from the chat** ("always make a second copy on the LaCie"): there is no such setting any more (Nicole, Nov 23). Say so in one line: "I ask each time now: after a backup, press Make a second copy on its report, or tell me and I'll make it." `--always` is still taken by the command and saves nothing. For one backup they can name the drive up front ("back this up, and a second copy on the LaCie"): `night.py ... --second "<that drive>"`; the copy is made after the shoots are sorted and put away, the same way. Never a cloud folder (Dropbox, Google Drive, iCloud).
- **Never say there is a second copy until the command's last line says it is made.** A copy that is running or stopped is not one, and the card's "safe to format" does not depend on it either way.
- **A second drive named for one backup had trouble** (it can't be written to, has no room, or a folder is in the way): the backup still finishes with one copy and the card is still safe. The report says there is one copy for now and why, with the same button; once the trouble is sorted, the button (or the command above) makes the copy.
- **A second drive saved by an earlier version** ("copy my shoots to the LaCie too", set up before Nov 23) is no longer used by itself. The first backup after that says so once on its report: "You had LaCie saved for a second copy after every backup. I ask each time now instead: press Make a second copy whenever you want one." If they ask, say the same; nothing of theirs was removed, and the copies already made are as they were.
- **"Eject my card" while a second copy is reading that card** answers "A second copy is still being made from this card. It won't be long." Say that and nothing more. If they pull the card anyway, the copy carries on from their drive.

## What the report lets them change

**Everything that used to be a question before a backup is a button on the report afterwards (Dustin, Nov 19).** They sit under the shoots, in "Anything you'd like to change?", on a backup that finished clean (not on a sample, an unfinished backup or one with a problem). Each button opens a small panel right there, and what a press did is said beside the button. You never need to run anything for a press on the page. When they say the same thing to you instead, this is what each one does and the command for it. A drive is always said by its name, never as a path.

**Make a second copy.** The section above.

**This shoot continues on another card.** The panel asks "Which backup is the other card?" and lists the other finished backups of the last 30 days, each by name, day and count ("tuesday card · Nov 17 · 1,840 photos"), then **Put them on one report**. The page answers "Done. Both cards are on one report now. Picked the wrong one? Tell the Assistant and it will take them apart." and becomes the shoot's page: the verdict for every card on top, then each card's own report under it. With no other backup it says "Back up the other card first: put it in and say backup now. Then come back here and pick it."
- It joins the reports only. "Each card's photos stay in their own folders." Say that if they expect one folder; nothing can merge two cards' photos into one folder today.
- From the chat ("this shoot was on two cards", "the wedding was on both of those cards", "put those two backups on one report"), the older card first:

```
python3 <scripts>/shoot_report.py link "<card 1's backup>" "<card 2's backup>"
```

  Then show the report of either one; it is the shoot's page now. A card that is still to be backed up: `parts/backup.md` (`night.py --with`).
- **"I picked the wrong card", "take those two apart":** `python3 <scripts>/shoot_report.py unlink "<the backup that does not belong>"`. It takes that card off the shoot's report; the other cards stay together (or, when one is left, it is a backup with its own report again). Records and pages only: no photo or folder is touched, and the record before is kept. Then show the report again (a report server already running needs stopping first). There is no button for this on the page; the page's own line says to tell you.

**Change how my shoots are named.** The panel asks "How should a shoot folder be named?" and shows the two ways with one of this backup's own shoots ("2026-11-19 Black-sand beach", "Black-sand beach, Nov 19 2026"), the one in use marked, a tick box **Rename this backup's shoots to match** (ticked), and **Save**. With the tick, each of this backup's shoots is renamed through the report's own rename queue, one at a time: the folder takes the new shape, the photos keep their names, every file is checked. The page ends on "Done. This backup's shoots are renamed to match, and your next backup's will be named like ...". Without the tick: "Saved. Your next backup's shoots will be named like ...". Under it the panel says "Name them your own way? Copy this into the chat:" with the line **Change how my shoots are named**.
- When that line arrives, or they say it ("change how my shoots are named", "I name my folders differently"): ask one thing, "Paste the name of one of your past shoot folders, just as it is on your drive." Read back what `studio_file.py pattern "<it>"` gives ("So Smith Family on Oct 18 would be 'Smith Family - 10.18.26'. That right?"), and on yes `studio_file.py set --example "<it>"`. If they would rather pick: `studio_file.py set --offered 1` (year first) or `--offered 2` (name first).
- That changes the next backup on. To bring a backup they already have into the new shape, rename each of its shoots to the name it already has: `rename_shoot.py "<backup>" --shoot s1 --name "<its name now>"`; the folder moves to the new shape and nothing else changes. Only when they ask for it.
- Photo names are not on the report. "Keep my camera's file names": `studio_file.py set --file-names original`, from the next backup on.

**Use a different drive next time.** The panel asks "Where should your next backup go?" and lists the drives plugged in by name and free space, the one in use marked, and the computer itself last ("This Mac"); then **Save**. The page answers "Saved. Your next backup goes to LaCie Rugged. The photos from this backup stay where they are." (when the photos will go into a folder on that drive it names it: "...goes to LaCie Rugged, in Photo Shoots."; a drive that can be read but not written to is refused in plain words). Where on the drive follows the first backup's rule: right on the drive, unless it has a folder plainly named for shoots with folders in it; for any other folder, the chat command below with the folder itself. Nothing is moved, ever: no button and no command moves a finished backup to another drive, so never say one will.
- From the chat ("use a different drive from now on", "put my photos on the LaCie next time"): `studio_file.py drives` to find the one they named, then `studio_file.py set --drive "<that drive, or its shoot folder>"`. Say the same line back: the next backup goes there, this one stays where it is.
- A drive an earlier version saved for second copies is no longer set apart: it can be chosen for the next backup like any other drive.

**There is no Write a note to my client button any more (Nicole and Dustin, Nov 23),** and a note an older backup had written is no longer shown on its report. The six photos on each shoot are "A first look at your shoot.", with **Pick again** and **Show me more** under them (`parts/sneak-peeks.md`).

**One quiet line when the photos are on the computer's own disk.** Under "Where your photos are" the report then says, once: "Your photos are on this Mac's own disk. An external drive is safer: if anything happens to the computer, the photos on it go too. Plug one in and press Use a different drive next time." That is the whole of it. Never say it before a backup, and never add a speech of your own; if they ask why, answer in a sentence or two.

## Naming the shoots after the backup, and putting shoots in one folder (Nicole, Nov 23)

**Nothing about names is asked before a backup.** The photos are made safe first; the names come after, on a page of their own, or by saying them to you.

**The page: Name your shoots.** It opens from the home page when a backup is done ("We found 3 shoots on your card · Name them") and from the report's **Put these shoots in one folder** (under "Anything you'd like to change?", on a backup with two or more shoots). To show it yourself ("let me name my shoots", "show me the naming page"):

```
python3 <scripts>/name_shoots.py url "<the backup's folder>"     # prints the page's address; load it in the pane
```

On it: "✓ Every photo is checked and safe on G PAPA › CLIENT WORK. It's safe to format your card." (only when that backup's own records say so; a sample or a backup with a problem gets a plain line that says to keep the card instead), then each shoot the sorting found with three pictures, when it was taken, how many, and a box that says "Shoot name". The folder's name is shown under the box and changes as they type ("Folder: Aurora_11-22-26": their own pattern, the photos' own date). **Save the names** renames each shoot they named, one at a time, as a job that carries on if they leave the page; a box left empty is left as it is. **Do it later** changes nothing. Then **All set** lists the folders as they are now, with **Back up another card** and **See all my backups**. You run nothing for a press on that page.

- Always "shoot name", never "client name" or "who is it for": not everyone shoots for clients.
- The photos take the first word of the shoot's name ("The Johnson Family" gives Johnson_0001_IMG_4471.JPG), keeping their number and the camera's own name. Someone who keeps the camera's file names (`studio_file.py set --file-names original`) gets only the folder renamed. Never say "your photos keep their own names" to anyone else.
- Two shoots can't be given one name (they would share a folder), and a name whose folder is already there is refused: the page says so under the buttons and nothing is renamed. They pick another name, or tick mini sessions.
- **A backup started from the home page names its shoots "Shoot 1", "Shoot 2"... until they are named.** On the page those are never typed into a box for them: each box is empty with the folder as it is now under it ("Folder: Shoot 1_11-22-26"). If they typed a name in the Back up a card box and the card turned out to hold more than one shoot, the page says it back ("Before the backup you typed “Aurora”. Put it on the shoot it belongs to.") and never guesses which shoot it was for; the same name is in what they said about the card (`client_note.py told`). A card with exactly one shoot was given the typed name by the backup itself, and its box shows it.
- **While a backup is still being made, nothing is renamed or joined.** `rename_shoot.py` and `join_shoots.py` stop with exit 2 and nothing changed, saying "This backup is still being made. Try this again when it's done." (a backup still sorting or putting its shoots away, or a page-started backup that has not finished its second copy yet) or "A second copy of this backup is being made right now. Try this again when it's done." Pass the line on and do it when the backup is done; never work around it.
- If a rename or a join was cut off part way, the page shows only "This was cut off part way. Nothing is lost. Press Finish it and it will carry on." and one button, **Finish it**; that is all they need to do, and the names come after. If they tell you about it instead, run `python3 <scripts>/join_shoots.py "<the backup's folder>" --finish`: it finishes a cut-off rename or join from its log.
- If the page says "This stopped on something I can't sort out from this page. Nothing is lost. Tell the Assistant and it will take a look.", pressing again will not help: a rename or a join has stopped on a problem, or twice over. Run the `--finish` command above and read what it prints (a photo that is not where the log says, a folder in the way, a file a program holds open). Say what it found in plain words and stop; never move or rename anything by hand to make it pass.
- A sidecar their editing program saved again while a rename or a join was under way is theirs and is left as they saved it, beside its photo under the new name; that is never a problem.

**"These are mini sessions. Keep them in one folder."** Ticking that on the page and pressing Save the names puts every shoot of that backup into one folder (named "Mini sessions" unless they type a name), the photos numbered on in shooting order across all of them.

**From the chat** ("join the two beach shoots", "those two are one shoot", "these are mini sessions, keep them together", "put the first two in one folder"):

```
python3 <scripts>/join_shoots.py "<the backup's folder>" --list                                  # its shoots: id | name | folder | how many
python3 <scripts>/join_shoots.py "<the backup's folder>" --shoots s1 s2 --name "Beach" --dry-run # the plan, nothing touched
python3 <scripts>/join_shoots.py "<the backup's folder>" --shoots s1 s2 --name "Beach"
python3 <scripts>/join_shoots.py "<the backup's folder>" --all --name "Mini sessions"            # every shoot of the backup into one
```

- **Which shoots:** work it out from `--list` and what they said ("the two beach ones"). If you can't tell which they mean, ask one short question. With no name given, the joined shoot keeps the first shoot's name; ask for a name only when they hinted at one.
- **What it does:** every photo and video clip of those shoots goes into one folder named their way, numbered on in shooting order, each one checked to be the very same file afterwards. A sidecar travels with its photo. Photos and video clips together become Photos and Videos inside the folder. The emptied folders are removed only when truly empty; nothing is deleted or written over, and the memory card is never touched. Say back in one line what it is now: "Done. Those two are one shoot now: Beach_11-22-26, 412 photos."
- **Exit 2 is a refusal with nothing changed:** say its STOP line as it is; every one is plain words. It refuses a shoot that is in the editing queue with a due date ("Take it out of your queue first, then join them."), a delivered shoot, a shoot a post kit was made from, a name whose folder is already there, a backup that is not fully checked, and a second copy that is being made right now.
- **Exit 1 is a stop part way:** nothing is lost and nothing needs undoing; never move or rename anything by hand. Run the very same command again (or `--finish`) and it finishes from its log. On a PC the reason is usually a photo held open by Lightroom or a folder window: pass its line on, and on go run it again.
- **A folder "is still there because it has things in it that I didn't put there":** that is theirs (an export, a contract). Say so; they can move it and delete the empty folder themselves.
- **Shoots from two different backups can't be joined yet** (two cards of one shoot stay in their own folders; `shoot_report.py link` puts them on one report). Say so plainly; never move photos between backups by hand.
- **Taking a joined shoot apart again is not built.** The log (`join-log.json` in the backup's records) holds every move; say it can't be undone with a button and ask what they need.
- Run twice, the second run says "Already joined" and changes nothing. After a join or a rename, a second copy on another drive is brought in line by itself when that drive is plugged in.

## When they name or rename a shoot

They will say it loosely: "the big one is the Smith family," "call the sunset one Kaia," "the yoga day is Marisol's." Work out which shoot they mean from `shoots.json` (its recap, date and photo count), confirm the spelling only if it is unusual, then:

```
python3 <scripts>/rename_shoot.py "<session>" --shoot s2 --name "Kaia sunset"
```

It renames the folder and every photo, keeps the original frame name inside each filename, re-checks every file, and prints "All done." Say that back in their words. **On the report page they can do it themselves:** each shoot's name box has a Rename button (Enter works too); the Assistant's little report server runs the rename right then, one shoot at a time if they press several, and the card shows "Renaming..." then "Done." or what stopped it. You don't need to run anything for those; if they tell you a rename on the page stopped, run the command above yourself and say what it said. (`--from-names` still applies `_studio/names.json` for the older page.)

**When a rename stops** (exit 1, or a STOP line), nothing is lost and nothing needs undoing; never move or rename anything by hand. What it says, and what to do:
- "Something has a photo in this folder open (Lightroom, or a folder window). Close it and say go..." (on a PC only: Windows won't rename a folder another program has open): pass that line on as it is; on go, run the very same command again, which finishes it from its log. On the report page, pressing Rename again after closing Lightroom finishes it too (the card then says "Already called that.").
- "STOP: the new name would make a photo's place longer than File Explorer can open ... Nothing renamed. A shorter name fixes it." (on a PC only): ask for a shorter name, then rename with that.
- "STOP: the rename stopped part way on something unexpected. Nothing is lost: every photo is still in the shoot's folder, and the backup's records say where each one is now.": pass that line on, and read the line just before it yourself (the details: the drive went away, a name the drive won't take). Running the same rename again tries to finish it, and nothing settles until it does.

## When they give you a name ("call yourself Sherman", "your name is Max", "I'll call you Ace")

Photographers name their first assistant, so they can name you. `studio_file.py set --name "Sherman"` and from then on answer to it, sign off with it when a sign-off fits, and every page shows it in the top bar (the product's name sits small beside it). One line back: "Sherman it is." A new name replaces the old one the same way; "just go by the Assistant again" is `--name ""`. Nothing else changes: folders, files and records keep their names.
