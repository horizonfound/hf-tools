# Canva: the designed carousel, cover and stories

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too. This is the Canva half of the post kit (parts/post-kit.md).

**The Canva half: the carousel and the reel cover, designed (Dustin, Oct 30: "as much done for you as possible").** The kit's plain slides and cover photo are the fallback; the finished look comes from their own Canva templates, filled in by the Assistant through the Canva connector (the Canva connector's tools, whose names end in `search-brand-templates`, `autofill-design`, `export-design` and so on, with a prefix that differs from one setup to the next: connected once in claude.ai, Settings › Connectors; no developer account; works on Canva Pro, which Brand Templates need). If those tools are not in this session, say so once ("To have your carousel and cover designed in Canva, connect Canva to Claude once, under Settings › Connectors. Until then the plain slides are in the kit.") and carry on without them.
- **Setup, once:** `canva_kit.py templates` says which templates are set. If none, `search-brand-templates` (dataset `non_empty`); if they have a carousel template and a cover template with fields, ask which is which and `canva_kit.py templates --carousel <id> --cover <id>`. If they have none: offer to make them from a design they already use ("Which of your Canva designs is a reel cover you like? A carousel? A story with text on it?"); record a story template with `--story <id>`. To make one: `create-brand-template-draft` is for a design that is already a template; for a plain design use the connector's editing tools (their names differ by version: `start-editing-transaction`, `perform-editing-operations` tagging the photo spots `photo_1`, `photo_2`... and the text spots `hook` / `title` with `autofill_field_label`, `commit-editing-transaction`) and `publish-brand-template`. Say what was tagged. Horizon Found's starter templates, when they exist, are the same step with a copied design.
- **Stories in Canva, with their words on them (Dustin, Nov 5).** When `canva_kit.py templates` shows a story template: one design per story. The words come from them ("the ideas are theirs" in SKILL.md's rules): if what they told you about the shoot gives the story lines, use those; if not, ask once, "What should the stories say? One line each, or one line for all of them," and keep it (`kit.py told`). Then for each story pick, in order: `create-upload-url` + `canva_kit.py upload "<the photo from the kit's Photos folder>" --url ...` (the photo itself, not the padded Story file; the template's frame does the fitting), `autofill-design` with the story template (`get-brand-template-dataset` names its fields: the image field gets the asset, the text field the line; title "<shoot> story N"), `export-design` (jpg, 1080x1920), `canva_kit.py fetch "<kit>" --into Stories --start N --urls '<list>'` (lands as "Story 0N (Canva).jpg"; the other stories are left alone), `canva_kit.py note "<kit>" --piece story --n N --design-id ... --edit-url ...`. The kit page then shows the Canva stories first and a "Story N in Canva" link under them. **The export is made first, then they get the link (Dustin): done unless they want to touch it.** "Export story 2 again" is `canva_kit.py which "<kit>" --piece story --n 2`, `export-design`, `fetch --into Stories --start 2`. No story template set: the plain 9:16 stories stay, and once, in one line, offer to set a story template up ("Have a Canva story design you use? Tell me which and I'll put your words on it next time."). Never ask about fonts or colors: those live in the template.
- **Every kit, after `kit.py build`:** read the template's fields (`get-brand-template-dataset`). For each photo field, in order: `create-upload-url`, then `canva_kit.py upload "<the kit's slide or cover file>" --url "<that link>"` (one link per file, used once; it prints the asset id). Then `autofill-design` with `brand_template_id`, the image fields as `{type: image, asset_id}`, the text fields (`hook` = the hook they chose or the first one, `title` = the shoot's name) as `{type: text, text}`, and a title like "<shoot> carousel". Then `export-design` (jpg, quality 90, every page; the cover: page 1) and `canva_kit.py fetch "<kit>" --into Carousel --urls '<the urls list>'` (Covers for the cover). Then `canva_kit.py note "<kit>" --piece carousel --design-id ... --edit-url ...` so the kit page gets an "Open in Canva" button beside "Open the Carousel folder". Fewer photos than fields: leave the extra fields out (the template keeps its defaults) and say so. Anything Canva refuses (a plan without Brand Templates, a template without fields): say it plainly in one line and keep the plain slides.
- **"Export that again" (Dustin, Oct 30: the fill gets it nine-tenths there; they move a sticker or two in Canva, then say so).** When they say "export the beach carousel", "export that and get it ready", "I fixed the cover, pull it in": find the kit they mean (`posts.py list` names every kit in Your Content), then `canva_kit.py which "<kit>" --piece carousel` (or cover) gives the design id and which folder it goes in; `export-design` on that id, then `canva_kit.py fetch` as above. The fresh slides take the same names; the earlier Canva files are set aside, dated, in the kit's hidden folder (never deleted), and the kit page and the posting queue's piece show the new ones. If a download fails, nothing in the kit changes; ask Canva for a fresh export. Then: "Pulled in the new slides. The beach carousel's in your kit." No id, no file name, no question.
