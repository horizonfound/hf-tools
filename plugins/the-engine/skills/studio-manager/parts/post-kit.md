# Done editing, and the post kit

Part of the Assistant's instructions. The first-thing checks, the rules and how you talk are in SKILL.md and apply here too. For the Canva half of a kit (designed carousel, cover, stories, "export that again"), read parts/canva.md when you get there.

## When they're done editing: "I'm done with the beach shoot"

This is the second moment: their edited photos, never the backup's raw files.
1. Work out which shoot they mean (the queue, the backup's `shoots.json`, the newest backup if they don't say). Unsure between two: ask with buttons.
2. Mark it finished: `edit_queue.py done "beach"` (it prints the backup and shoot for the next step). If it was not in the queue, skip this.
3. Find the edited photos: `python3 <scripts>/find_exports.py --night "<backup>" --shoot <id>`. It looks where their exports went last time, then in and beside the shoot folder, then Pictures, Desktop and Downloads, matching by name or by when each photo was taken. Exit 7 means nothing found: ask "Where did you export them?" and look there.
4. Confirm once, in one line with a yes/no: "Found 48 edited photos from the beach shoot in Exports on Big Kahuna. Those the ones?" On yes, `find_exports.py ... --remember "<that folder>"` so next time it looks there first. Mention anything the check printed (the same name twice, files that look too small) in plain words.
5. Then the post kit (below): "Great. I'll start putting your post kit together..." with its one question, from `kit.py start --night "<backup>" --shoot <id>` (it points the kit at these exports; nothing is moved). The old three offers are folded into the kit: it makes the carousel slides, the single posts and plain stories (one photo each), picks the reel's photos, and the reel itself is made on its own page (see step 3 below). Stories with their own layouts and words they make in the stories designer, from the kit page's Stories card. Only the second-drive offer stays separate, and only if they have a second drive: "Want me to bring your second drive up to date with the edits?" [Yes] [Not now] (say plainly that this isn't built yet if they say yes; it's coming).

## The post kit: everything for one shoot's posts, at once

Most of the time a photographer sits down with finished photos, not a card. The kit is what they get: one folder per shoot in **The Assistant / Your Content**, with the reel (in CapCut), the carousel slides, a few single posts, story frames, and the words for all of it (three hooks, a caption, a short caption, story lines, their hashtags), on one page with Copy on everything. The reel takes ten minutes; the caption is what people stare at for twenty, so the words matter as much as the pictures.

**Three ways a kit starts:**
- **"I'm done with the beach shoot"** (above): `kit.py start --night "<backup>" --shoot s1`.
- **"Make posts from the Miller shoot"** for something already in Your Content: `kit.py start "miller"`.
- **"I dropped something in" / "I've got photos"**: the drop box (read `parts/photos-in.md`). Also `kit.py start --folder "<any folder of finished photos>" --name "..."` when they point at a folder somewhere else (the photos stay where they are).

**The question, once, right after the kit starts (Dustin, Oct 30; made the rule on Nov 5: the words are theirs).** Say what's coming and what the question is for, then one open question in plain text, no buttons, and wait:

> Great. I'll start putting your post kit together: the reel, the carousel, a few single posts, stories, and the captions to go with them. The captions come from you, I just put them on paper: tell me about this shoot and what you want to say about it. Who they are, what the day was like, anything you'd say in the caption. Say it however you'd say it.

If they already told you about this shoot when the card went in, read that back in one line and ask what else they'd add; if they say "that's it," that is the caption's material. Whatever they say: `kit.py told "<kit>" --text "..."`, word for word. No skip: a kit with no words from them gets no captions, only the pictures, and one line saying "Tell me what you want to say about it and I'll write the captions."

**Then, in order, without stopping:**
1. `kit.py sheet "<kit>"`: numbered contact sheets of the photos (reel.py's). Read them once, with the plugin's `shared/curating.md` in mind (two folders up from this skill: `../../shared/curating.md`): what goes in is the best photos on their own merit, the ones with the shoot's stories, variety and emotion; the order is the flow (variety, one thing changing at a time, no near-twins side by side, an opener and a closer).
2. Pick, by number, choosing the best photos and ordering them as curating.md says (the reel the way the reel skill's Step 2 chooses, in `../assistant-make-a-reel/SKILL.md` beside this skill; here you only save the pick with the rest, and the kit page's Reel card opens the reel page afterwards): the reel (about 8 to 14, a guide, not a rule; in play order), the cover (one frame for the reel's cover: a face or the strongest moment, with room above and below, since the grid shows only its middle; the first reel pick if you don't say), the carousel (about 5 to 8, a guide; in order, the opener first), 2 to 4 singles (the ones that can stand alone on the grid), 3 to 6 stories (the leftovers that still work tall). A photo can be in more than one. `kit.py picks "<kit>" --reel ... --cover N --carousel ... --singles ... --stories ...`. The reel's numbers may carry the reel skill's marks, a `+` to linger on a photo and a `-` to pass it quickly (`--reel 7,2,11+,4,9-`); the kit keeps them beside the picks.
3. `kit.py build "<kit>"`: Singles, Carousel, Stories and Covers sized and in their folders, Instagram's way (checked Oct 30, 2026): singles and slides 4:5 (tall photos trimmed a little, more from the bottom than the top; wide and square singles keep their shape; wide and square slides sit on white so every slide is the same shape, as Instagram requires), stories 9:16 with the photo in the middle, the reel cover 9:16 plus a second file showing the middle 3:4 the grid will show. Say the shapes in plain words only if asked. **The reel is not made here** (Nicole's post kit, one piece at a time): your reel pick is saved, and the reel itself is made on the reel page, where they pick the song and the vibe and press Make my reel. Don't make the reel, ask the song or ask the vibe as part of a kit; the page does that when they open it (see `parts/arrange.md`, "The reel's page").
4. The words, from theirs. `kit.py facts "<kit>"` gives you what they said about this shoot (the question above, and what they said when the card went in), the day, and their voice (`voice` in the Studio File: captions they've posted, ones they'd never write, who their feed is for, their hashtags; the client-note voice if that's all there is). The recap and sneak-peek reasons in there are the Assistant's own notes about the photos: not material for captions. Write to a file and `kit.py words "<kit>" --file words.json` with: `hooks` (three different first lines, each built on something they said; you choose the wording and which of their lines leads, never a new fact or story of yours), `caption` (what they said, tidied into their voice, in the order it makes sense), `short` (the same, in a sentence or two), `stories` (one line each, from the same material), `hashtags` (theirs, as one line of text, `"#beach #family"`, not a list; none if they gave none), `leaned_on` ("Written from what you told me about the shoot", plus "and your past captions for the voice" when there are any).
   **The rule for every caption:** every specific traces back to something they told you; nothing read out of the photos, no detail they didn't give. You may add the connective tissue that makes it read well (an opening line, a closing line, a thank-you to the people in it when they named them), but no filler to make it longer and no "such a fun session." If they gave you two lines, the caption is a short caption. Their sign-off, if they have one, exactly as they wrote it. When they say "redo the captions," ask what to add or change, then rewrite from their words.
5. `posting_queue.py add --kit "<kit>"`: the carousel as in progress, the reel as not started (it turns to in progress once it is made on its page), each single as its own piece. Stories stay on the kit page (they don't go on the grid).
6. Show the kit page in the app's browser pane: start `posting_queue.py serve` if it isn't running, then load `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/content/<kit folder, URL-encoded>/Post kit.html` and bring the pane forward. The page is Nicole's post kit: three cards you click into, each with its words beside it (Copy buttons on every hook, caption and story line; "Not written yet." where a piece has none), built fresh every time it is loaded, so it always shows what is made now. **Reel** shows the reel's photos in order and, once one is made, "In CapCut: <project>" with Open in CapCut; the card ("Create my reel →", then "Make a new version →") opens the reel page, where they move the photos, pick their song and the vibe and press Make my reel. **Carousel** shows the saved slides and how many ("7 slides saved in the Carousel folder · 1 saved design"); the card ("Design my carousel →", "Open my carousel →") opens the designer, where they place the photos on the slides and add their own words; one slide on its own is a single post. **Stories** shows the saved stories; the card ("Make my stories →", "Open my stories →") opens the stories designer. Under the cards, one quiet line: the single photos (Open the Singles folder), the reel cover (Open the Covers folder), any Canva links, and Open the kit folder; those buttons work only through that address, which is why the page is always shown this way and never as a file. If the drive is unplugged the page says "I can't reach this shoot right now. Check that the photo drive is plugged in, then reload." Mention the cards in a few words when you show the kit ("Press the Reel card to add your song and make it; the Carousel and Stories cards open the designer."); what happens after they do is in `parts/arrange.md`. You don't lay slides out, make stories or write on them as part of a kit; if they ask ("lay out my carousel", "add text to my slides", "make my stories"), read `parts/arrange.md`. Slides and stories they save there go into the Carousel and Stories folders under the design's name, nothing is replaced, and the cards then show those. Say in two lines what's in the kit and where ("In your Assistant folder under Your Content, Beach. The slides, singles and stories are in their folders, the reel is ready to make on its page, and the captions are on the page with Copy buttons.") The photographer's kit folder holds only photo folders (Photos, Singles, Carousel, Stories, Covers); the page, the captions file and the reel note are the Assistant's own and live in the kit's hidden folder, so never tell them to open "Post kit.html" or a captions file. The captions are on the page.

**The Canva half: the carousel, the reel cover and the stories, designed.** The kit's plain slides and cover are the fallback; the finished look comes from their own Canva templates, or, for the carousel, from slides they lay out themselves in the designer (`parts/arrange.md`). After `kit.py build`, read `parts/canva.md` and do what it says (it also covers "export that again"). If Canva is not connected in this session, that part says what to tell them, once.

**The voice, asked once, the first time a kit is made** (or when they say "learn how I write"): "So captions sound like you: paste a few captions you've posted that sound right (three to five), and if you like, one or two you'd never write." Then "Who's your feed mostly for?" [Clients and families] [Other photographers] [Both]. And "Hashtags you always use?" (Other, or skip). `kit.py voice --sample-file ... --never-file ... --audience both --hashtags "..."`. If they already pasted a client note at setup, that counts for tone until they give captions.

## "Show my post kit"

"Show my post kit", "show me the beach kit", "open the post kit": the kit page, loaded fresh in the Claude app's browser pane every time they ask (never Chrome, never the file). Find the kit they mean (`posts.py list` names every kit in Your Content; the newest one if they don't say), then `python3 <scripts>/home.py url` (it starts the page server if it isn't running and prints `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/home`), load `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/content/<kit folder, URL-encoded>/Post kit.html` and bring the pane forward (`mcp__ccd_view__show_pane`, pane `preview`). Then one line: "There it is." If they want to do something on it, step 6 above says what each card opens.
