# Interview questions

Ask one at a time. Keep your wording casual. The follow-ups are optional; use them only when an answer is thin.

## Part 1: The facts (~5 min)

1. What's your name, and what's your business called?
2. What do you shoot most? Anything else you shoot on the side?
   - Follow-up if they list a lot: "If you could only book one kind of session next month, which would it be?"
3. Where are you based, and how far will you travel for a session?
4. Where do your clients come from? Offer three options:
   - **They come to me**: local sessions (families, newborns, weddings, seniors, couples, events)
   - **They ship to me**: clients send products or work with me remotely (product, brand, e-commerce)
   - **Both**
   - Add a fourth if it fits: **I travel to them** (weddings, destination, campaigns). This changes what their posts talk about and who they're trying to reach. Note it clearly.
5. What's your main offer right now, and roughly what does it cost? (A range is fine.)
6. Describe the client you'd love ten more of. Who are they, what do they care about, what's going on in their life when they book you?
7. Where do new clients find you today? (Instagram, referrals, Google, word of mouth...)
8. What's your Instagram handle, and what's the link where people inquire or book?
9. How often do you post right now, honestly? And how often do you wish you posted?
10. What's the hardest part of posting for you? (Picking photos, writing captions, making reels, finding time, not knowing what to say...)
11. What do you want more of in the next six months? (More bookings, higher prices, a new type of client, fewer but bigger sessions...)

Recap, confirm, move on.

## Part 2: Their voice (~10 min)

Explain in one line why: "Everyone describes their own voice as 'warm and authentic,' so instead of asking, I'd rather hear how you actually talk."

1. Paste 3 to 5 Instagram captions you wrote yourself. Pick ones that felt most like you.
2. Paste a text or email you sent a client. Any normal one is great: a booking reply, a "your gallery is ready" note, a thank-you.
3. Now the fun one. Tap the microphone on your keyboard and just talk for about two minutes: **tell me about a session you loved.** Who was it, what happened, what made it special? Don't clean it up. Rambling is perfect.
   - This is the most valuable sample. Spoken words show their real rhythm. If they skip it, ask once more, kindly, then move on.
4. Paste 3 or more client reviews or kind messages.
5. Any words or phrases you use all the time? Anything you'd never say?
6. Emojis: never, sometimes, or always? Any favorites?
7. How do you feel talking about price? (Upfront, only when asked, prefer not to...)

After this part, tell them what you noticed in plain words. For example: "You write in short sentences, you joke about the chaos of kids, you say 'y'all', and you never use exclamation points." Ask: does that sound right?

## Part 3: Their edge (~5 min)

1. What do clients always say after a session or when they see their photos?
2. What do you do that other photographers near you don't?
3. Why did you start doing this?
4. What will you never do, even if a client asks? (Heavy editing, posed-only, certain trends...)
5. Tell me one or two things about you outside of photography. (Hobbies, family, what you're known for among friends.) These sometimes make great hooks.

Recap, confirm, move on.
