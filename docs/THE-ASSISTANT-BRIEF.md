## 1. In one paragraph

A photographer buys a short course and a plugin for the Claude desktop app. The course teaches them how to show up
on social media as themselves (Nicole's lesson), then the plugin interviews them for forty minutes and writes their
Brand File. From then on they decide what to post, one piece at a time, and tell the Assistant to make it: a reel
from a session's clips and photos, cut to the beat, with hooks to pick from; a carousel from a folder of photos in
their templates, in an order that keeps people looking; a few story frames spun off that day's post. It works in
their voice and their look, hands back something they can still edit, and never posts anything. One price, no
subscription to us, updates automatic. **[both]**

Nicole's one-line version, Oct 20: "It's posting. It's making sharing your work as a photographer easier.
Bringing in clients easier." **[N]**

The bar she set the same night, pointing at Meg's launch post: "she fixed a specific job for a specific person. we
gotta slay that for photographers. this is the one we really gotta remember." **[N]** Meg's words: "your voice, your
ideas, your brand. it just does the clicking."

## 2. The rules that shaped it (these are the feedback; nothing gets built that breaks one)

1. **They drive. It makes.** The photographer decides what to post this week, from the lesson. The Assistant makes
   one piece at a time. It never plans their week for them: "the more free rein the AI has, the more room for things
   that sound like AI and aren't you." **[N, Oct 17 1am; again Oct 17 evening; again Oct 20]**
2. **Photos are the point.** "It's not caption heavy. It's not these elaborate text-heavy carousels. Everything is
   based on the photos." Text only where it earns it: a carousel cover, a reel hook, a mini announcement. **[N]**
3. **It sounds like them because it interviewed them.** Not from their captions ("most people use AI, so they're just
   going to sound like AI"). From a real interview, voice first, that digs for the second answer. **[N, Oct 16 + Oct 20]**
4. **A shared understanding between the person and the AI.** "The AI can only go as deep as they do." So: a lesson
   before the interview, taught by Nicole, and the same thinking written into the plugin as the playbook. **[N]**
   Dustin: "the actual product that we are selling is your marketing mind." **[D, Oct 17]** Nicole agreed once she
   knew what "playbook" meant. **[N, Oct 20]**
5. **Guardrails every output is checked against.** Approved list, Oct 20 **[N]**: speaks to their client, whoever that
   is · photos first · trial reels never go to the feed; feed posts are for the people who already follow you ("two
   separate strategies") · never invent an offer, a date, a price or a name · nothing posts, nothing sends ·
   every multiple-choice question has "other, tell me" · personal-story material only after asking.
6. **An immediate result.** "They upload their behind the scenes and in ten minutes they get a finished video back.
   That's the thing that's going to make people lose their minds." **[N, Oct 17]**
7. **Warm, encouraging, in Horizon Found's voice, never makes anyone feel behind.** After the interview: "that was a
   lot, and you're amazing for doing it; this is the catalyst for everything we build; from here it's go time."
   **[both, Oct 17]** The interviewer "sounds like me in a way... never makes them feel bad." **[N, Oct 20]**
8. **It's for photographers, and that's the edge.** Meg's is for talking heads. Ours knows how photographers post:
   behind the scenes into photos, montages, galleries. **[N, Oct 17]**
9. **It's Claude, and we say so.** Not a hidden app. **[D, Oct 16]**
10. **Honest claims only.** Nothing on a sales page that hasn't run on a real Mac. **[both]**

## 3. What's in

### The course (in Kajabi)
Order, Oct 20 **[N]**: the lesson first → install the Assistant → the interview → a lesson for each tool.
A "full course library dashboard where people go through each section," like Meg's. **[N]** Checkout, delivery and
the course all stay inside Kajabi. **[D, Oct 21]**

**The lesson.** Nicole teaches branding and showing up on social media: pillars, bandwidth, a posting cadence you can
keep, who you're talking to, how much of you vs. the business, trial reels as free ads vs. feed posts for your
current following, the kinds of posts a photographer can make from one shoot. Replaces the workbook ("they're gonna
overthink it, it's gonna take too long, they're never gonna do it"). **[N, Oct 20]** Nicole writes it; it is also the
raw material for the playbook.

### Start Here (the interview, kept at about 40 minutes) **[N, Oct 20]**
- An interviewer, not a form. Digs: "I love shooting families" gets "what kind, what style, what are you drawn to."
  **[N]** Pulls answers out instead of asking "what makes you different" (she froze on it). Options built from what
  they already said. **[N]**
- Voice first. "They have to speak to it themselves." **[N]**
- Never asks for their captions. **[N]** Asks where clients come from with an "other." **[N]** Asks how much of
  themselves vs. the business they want to show. **[N]** Asks what they want their business to become, not what it
  is. **[N]**
- Says "they're in the driver's seat" all the way through. **[N]**
- Colors as swatches, never hex codes. Sample posts at the end, so they see it, not read it. **[N, Oct 16 + tester
  notes]**
- "note:" mid-interview saves straight to tester notes. **[N]**
- Ends warm and specific: why they're amazing, what we're about to do together. **[N, Oct 20]**
- The look section opens with a question: do you already have a brand identity, fonts, colors? If yes, send them;
  we check the fonts exist in CapCut and Canva or find the closest; we build around it. If no, we build it from the
  library. **[N, Oct 16 + Oct 20]** Two fonts (a headline and a sub) that exist in both CapCut and Canva. **[N]**
  Their colors as swatches "if needed; in a lot of cases their colors don't matter, this is photography focused."
  **[N, Oct 20]**

### The style pack (how things look)
Nicole, Oct 20, on Meg's "build your own style pack" lesson: "THIS IS A GREAT WAY TO ORGANIZE THE STYLING PART OF
THE ENGINE." **[N]** Three ways in: start from a look in our library and make it yours · build it from the branding
you already have (website, screenshots, fonts) · style a Canva template and the Assistant reads it. **[N]**
Carousel templates live in Canva, photographer-focused, one folder. **[N, Oct 17 + 20]** "We don't need 50
templates." **[N, Oct 20]** The library is designed once by a human; the Assistant picks and fills, it never designs
from scratch (the Round One mockups were "shitty" because it was improvising layouts). **[N; C agreed]**

### Make a reel **[N + D]**
- From a session folder: behind-the-scenes clips, finished photos, a talking clip if there is one, the song.
- Recipes a photographer actually posts: behind the scenes · behind the scenes cut to music · behind the scenes
  into the photos · photo montage · montage with a client's review over it · the behind-the-scenes moment that
  matches a finished photo ("find the matching moment so it can land"). **[N, Oct 17]** Oct 21 list from Nicole:
  behind the scenes into photos, photos only, B-roll, a behind-the-scenes sneak peek, a DM testimonial with photos
  flashing behind it.
- Cut to the beat. Non-negotiable: "unless it's falling on the right beats, the video is gonna suck." **[N, Oct 20]**
  The beat detection is proven (grader, Oct 21); the open question is only where the song file comes from.
- Clean sound with the voice over the ducked song, text set like a designer would and kept off faces. **[D, Oct 21]**
  Built into 0.3.1. Photo motion was built the same day and then taken off the table by Nicole: "it's not even trying
  to be a video editor." Hard cuts. **[N + D, Oct 21 evening]**
- Ten to twenty hook options for the on-screen text. Pick one for the feed; the others become trial reels of the
  same cut. **[N, Oct 17 + 20]**
- Text on, placeholder, or none ("so I can go in there and add it myself"). **[D, Oct 17]**
- A caption only if they ask for one. **[N, Oct 20]**
- Comes back editable: "it's either ready and you post it, or it's completely editable" in CapCut. **[D, Oct 17]**
  Proven Oct 21: a CapCut 9.6 draft written by script opens and exports on Dustin's Mac. MP4 default, CapCut project
  as "open it if you want to change anything."
- Scrubbing two hours of footage for the moment is "gnarly" for a human and the hardest ask for the tool. **[N]**
  Not promised until it works on her footage.

### Make a carousel **[N]**
- Photos already in a Canva folder for that shoot; "locate this folder, these are the ones I want, put them in
  order." Solves the 20-photo upload limit and the file-naming pain. **[N, Oct 17]**
- The order is the craft: "starting with the most impactful, beautiful shot first," an order that keeps someone
  swiping. "Putting the carousels into a flow is going to be very important." **[N, Oct 20]**
- Our templates; a few cover options, with or without text. **[N]**
- Gallery feedback on the way in: "these are all tight crops, do you have any wides?" Asked for three times. **[N]**
- No caption unless asked. **[N]**

### Make stories **[N]**
- Spun off that day's reel or carousel: a few frames that go deeper on the shoot, for the warm people who watch
  stories. "I think we should keep the stories in the launch." **[N, Oct 20]** Decided: in.

### The playbook + guardrails **[both]**
Nicole's thinking about social media, written down, that every tool reads before it does anything: pillars, cadence,
trial reels vs. feed, post types from one shoot, what makes a gallery dynamic, who the post speaks to. Plus the
hard-yes/hard-no list in section 2. The lesson is the human version; the playbook is the version the Assistant reads.
Made from: Nicole talking it through, the way she records her notes. First draft in `plugins/the-engine/shared/`;
lines marked "[draft, Nicole to confirm]" are Claude's fill-ins.

### The menu **[D idea; N "looks good"]**
Open a chat and it asks "What are we doing today?" with the tools as choices. For testing, a chat menu does the
job; a screen is a later upgrade.

### Personality **[both]**
Two voices kept apart: Horizon Found's voice to the photographer (warm, funny, encouraging, patient), the
photographer's voice in everything it writes for them. Never mixed.

## 5. Open decisions

1. **The name.** "The Assistant" inside "The Studio." Nicole lukewarm on "Assistant" on Oct 20; "The Studio" lit both
   of you up on Oct 21. Check the trademark and handle.
2. **The order.** Lesson first (Nicole's stated order) vs. install → first reel → lesson. Not decided.
3. **Where the song comes from.** A song file in the session folder works today. Dustin's direction (Oct 21): default
   is picking the song inside CapCut (its library has the trending sounds; CapCut keeps a copy the tool can read;
   untested); fallback "find me one" from a free library (not built). Nicole (Oct 21): song preview before picking.
4. **Nicole's Brie Groves question, Oct 20: "Could that ever happen with Claude?"** Honest answer: any platform can
   change its mind. What protects us: the product is a folder of plain-text instructions and a few scripts, the Brand
   File and the playbook are files the buyer owns, the zip backup exists, and the same folder runs in Claude Code
   (now proven: this repo is the plugin). Say that on the sales page in one calm line.

## 6. Risks, said plainly
- The reel tool has run on one Mac with test files. A real session (real photos, a real song, a clip of someone
  actually talking) is the test that counts; the sound mixing under a voice has been built but not heard.
- "Graded PASS" is only as honest as what the grader checks. Standing rule: every "it works" claim comes with the
  file to watch, not the grade.
- The Canva folder route and template filling are untested on Nicole's account.
- The song has to be a file. If students expect to cut to Instagram audio they don't have, the beat promise breaks.
- The lesson and the playbook are Nicole's to write, and everything in the voice depends on them.
