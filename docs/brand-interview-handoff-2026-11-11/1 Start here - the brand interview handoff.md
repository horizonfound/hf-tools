# The brand interview: a handoff for Nicole and her Claude

From Dustin and his Claude, Nov 11, 2026.

**Nicole:** the Assistant needs the questions a new student is asked, so it has a Brand Homebase to write their captions and hooks from. We'd love you to work them out in conversation with your Claude, trying them on yourself. What comes back is one file: the exact questions, and what a finished Brand Homebase looks like.

To start: give your Claude this file, the October question list Dustin sends with it, and your own Brand Homebase, and say "let's design the brand interview." The rest is for your Claude.

## Who decides what

The questions and their wording are Nicole's to shape. How long the interview runs and where it sits in a student's first days come back as her recommendation; Dustin and Nicole agree on those together. Dustin's Claude then builds what the two of them approve, word for word.

## Before you start

You can't see our project folder, so what you need from it is here. Read three other things first:

- **The writing rules** from Nicole's v20 handoff (the "How to write it" block in the installed `words.py`). They are the real spec for what a Homebase feeds.
- **Nicole's own Brand Homebase,** which the October interview produced.
- **The October question list,** attached by Dustin.

## Why it matters

**What the Assistant does with a Brand Homebase today.** It reads it only when writing the words for a post (a reel's caption and ten hooks, a carousel's caption, polishing their own caption). It reads the whole file each time and needs no particular headings, so the layout can be whatever serves the writing. Length is open, but every post costs a full read on the student's own Claude plan.

What the writing rules lean on it for:

- how they sound, the words they use, the words they never want to see
- their house rules (Nicole's file says mini sessions always show the price clearly, so the Assistant asked her for it)
- who their ideal client is and what that person wants
- their personality (fun, heartwarming, a mix), so the hooks match
- their edge: what only this photographer would say
- how they close a caption, used only when the Homebase shows that habit or they said it for that post

It is never a source of facts about a shoot; those come from what they say per post.

**Where it lives.** One file, named exactly `brand-homebase.md`, in the photographer's Assistant folder (the folder Your Content sits in). (The code also accepts that name in the hidden `.studio` folder inside it, and in the Assistant's records folder on the computer.) It never searches further, on purpose: another business's brand file is the wrong voice. The file name stays, whatever the spoken name becomes.

**With none,** the Assistant asks once per conversation, "I don't have your Brand Homebase yet, the page or two that says how your photography business sounds. Do you have one?" If not, it writes from what they said plus a few pasted captions, and ends "It isn't checked against your brand yet." Every student is there today.

**What does not use it.** Client notes, choosing photos, the designer's colors and fonts.

## The students and their first days

Photographers of every kind, not only client work, on Mac and PC. Many don't shoot often; most come for the posts. Many have never thought about "brand" and don't know the vocabulary. Most have barely used Claude.

A new student starts one of two ways. With a memory card: a few button questions (introduced with "It takes about a minute, and it's only this once"), then the backup runs by itself. With finished photos: one question, then the post kit, where a reel and a carousel are made and their words written.

The interview meets three people: one with a clear brand, one with none yet, one who already has a Brand Homebase file. Some have more than one business, as Nicole does; the Homebase is the photography one.

## What must hold

**Product rules. The interview is designed inside these.** If Nicole wants one changed, note it under what's left to decide.

- Specifics come only from the photographer. Nothing is filled in because it is probably true. Ask, don't guess.
- The Assistant is the editor. It never says the words will "sound like you". Nicole's line: "I'll shape what you said like an editor would."
- The photographer is "you" or "they", never "she" or "he": the product is for every photographer.
- It works on a Mac and a PC.
- Nothing posts, nothing sends.
- Button questions have two to four buttons, labels of a few words, one or several pickable, and an "Other" box added by itself. One button alone is not possible; a question with no buttons is simply asked in the chat.
- Talking happens in the chat box, with its own microphone, not on a page. The interview may mention it. (Untried by us on a PC.)

**Nicole's preferences so far, hers to revise:** one question at a time; buttons where there are likely answers, the likeliest first; a short line on why each question matters.

**What Dustin's side would ask for; push back if wrong:** safe to stop part way and pick up later; a student who already has a Brand Homebase is not interviewed again; not wasteful of the student's Claude plan.

**Not settled: "Claude" or "your assistant".** Nicole's preference is never "Claude" on anything a photographer sees. Our pages still say "Tell Claude" in places, and a product rule says we never hide that it is Claude. For the interview, write "your assistant" and flag lines where it matters.

## Open questions for the two of you

1. How long should it take? One interview, or a short one first and more later?
2. Where does it sit: before the first backup or first posts, at the first "write my words", or offered and skippable?
3. Which things must the Assistant know to write well, and which are nice to have?
4. When a student doesn't know an answer: teach, offer examples, skip, come back later? What goes in the file?
5. How does the Homebase get updated as their brand changes?
6. Past captions. Nicole said in October not to learn from them ("Most people write those with AI now, so they'd teach me to sound like AI instead of you"). Our stand-in, written later, asks for them anyway. Does Nicole's rule still stand, and what of theirs is collected instead?
7. What happens to the stand-in questions, and to "Do you have one?" and "It isn't checked against your brand yet."?
8. The name: "Brand Homebase" (the Assistant today) or "Brand File" (October, and the title of Nicole's own file)?
9. What does the Assistant say to someone with two businesses, to make sure this is the photography one?
10. Should look (fonts, colors) or posting plan be in the Homebase?
11. What does the finished Homebase look like, and is the student shown it to approve?

## A suggested way to work

A suggestion, not a script. Work backwards from the writing: ask what the v20 rules need to know for a caption or hook to be right, and draft questions from that. Run the draft on Nicole as a student, then with Nicole playing a beginner with no brand. Write the Homebase each run produces, try a caption and hooks from it, and see which questions earned their place.

Nicole's Claude: label your own suggestions as yours, and don't turn a remark about one example into a rule Nicole didn't state.

## What to send back

One file, `brand-interview-from-nicole.md`, sent to Dustin like the other handoffs. Exact wording matters. An answer to each open question (a recommendation for 1 and 2), plus:

1. **The questions, in order.** For each: the exact wording; its line of why; its buttons and what each leads to; required or skippable; any follow-up and when; what writing it feeds; where its answer goes in the Homebase, in their words or tidied.
2. **The three paths:** how the Assistant finds out which kind of student it has, and the words for each.
3. **The opening and closing words,** including showing them the finished Homebase.
4. **Stopping and coming back:** the words when they stop and when they return, and whether a part-finished Homebase is used for writing meanwhile. (The Assistant can save a file and read it next conversation; how is ours to build.)
5. **The Homebase template,** every heading, with one filled-in example for a made-up photographer.
6. **Decisions made, and who made each,** with Nicole's words quoted where they matter.
7. **Anything left** for Dustin and Nicole to decide together.
