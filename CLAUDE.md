## What the product is, in one paragraph

**The Studio** is a staff that lives on a photographer's own computer and runs on her own Claude plan. The **Studio Manager** handles everything around the art from the moment a card goes in: backup to the drive she chose (a second folder only if she wants one), every file verified byte for byte, blinks and near-duplicates marked for review only, a mini-session day sorted into families by the gaps in the photo timestamps, sneak peeks, the client note, the delivery date on her calendar, the empty gallery, the export check, the upload, and then the handoff of her favorites to the **Assistant** (the old "Engine"), which cuts a reel on the beat of the song she picked, builds a carousel, writes hooks, captions on request, and stories. The **Concierge** (inbox, milestones, testimonials, gifts) is the first add-on, 2027. One product, one install, one-time purchase. Launch target: January 1 (or January 6), 2027. Black Friday is off.

The full story: `docs/STUDIO-PLAN.md` (the plan and feasibility read), `docs/the-studio-brief-v3.md` (Nicole's brief, the one that counts), `docs/THE-ASSISTANT-BRIEF.md` (the Assistant, traced to who said what).

## Rules that nothing may break

1. **The memory card is never touched.** Nothing on it is edited, renamed, moved or deleted; it stays exactly as the camera wrote it. The Studio Manager only reads from it. Copy, verify, report. The worst outcome is a job that did not finish, never a lost photo. A file that already exists at the destination is compared, not replaced. She formats her own card, after the report says "safe in two places" (the card and her drive, verified identical; see "Where we draw the line"). **The copy on her drive is ours to organize**, once it is verified: sort it into session folders and rename, keeping the original filename inside the new one (`Smith-Family_IMG_4471.CR3`), so every photo can always be traced back to the card. Never delete or overwrite there either.
2. **Stop loudly on any problem.** A copy error, a hash mismatch, a missing drive, a full cloud folder: stop, put it at the top of the report. Never guess.
3. **Culling is a marker, not an action.** Blinks, soft focus, duplicates are labeled for her review and stay where they are.
4. **Every frame is theirs.** No AI-generated pixels or motion. The Assistant is not a video editor: song first, hard cuts on the beat, no transitions, no effects, no Ken Burns. Photo motion exists in the code and is to be off by default.
5. **Nothing posts, nothing sends.** Drafts wait for her approval. Nothing the Assistant makes is ever published by us.
6. **Heavy lifting in scripts, not in Claude.** A 2,000-image session must not be looked at photo by photo by the model. Scripts score and shortlist; Claude looks at the shortlist. This keeps her plan cost near zero.
7. **Honest claims only.** Nothing on a sales page that has not run on a real Mac. Placeholders are marked ([PRICE], [DATE], [QUOTE], "confirm after beta"). Only real quotes.
8. **It is Claude, and we say so.** No hidden app.
9. **Study Meg's engine (The Startup Mom), never bootleg it.**
10. **Nothing goes to Nicole or Slack without Dustin's say-so.**

## Where we draw the line (Dustin, Oct 23)

Our job: copy the card to her drive, verify every file, keep errors from happening, organize what we copied, and report it plainly. "Safe in two places" means the card and her drive hold identical photos. From there, how she backs up is her business.

- **A second copy is optional.** Setup asks once whether she has a second backup folder. If yes, `--second` points at it. If no, nothing nags. Most photographers will have one drive.
- **Cloud backup is hers.** Dustin uses Backblaze (about $9/month, unlimited, backs up the drive on its own) and we may recommend it (they run an affiliate program, 10% recurring). The Studio Manager never touches it, never verifies it, and the report never mentions it. One Backblaze fact for the setup guide: an external drive unplugged for 30 days gets dropped from the cloud copy unless Extended Version History is on. At most, if she tells us she uses it, offer a reminder to plug the drive in. That is as far as we go.
- **Report wording:** "Everything is on your drive and verified. The card is safe to format when you're ready." Say what we checked, nothing we didn't.
- **Nothing starts without her saying so (Dustin, Oct 23).** A backup begins only when she says **"backup now"** (the cue; "card's in" was the earlier draft). A watcher may notice a card and nudge her with a notification, never copy on its own: browsing an old drive must never start a backup. Open question for Nicole: once she has said the word, do later cards the same night start on their own ("card 3 safe, next card when you're ready"), or does each card need the word?

## Any photographer, any photos (Dustin, Oct 23)

The Studio is for all photographers, not just the ones who shoot clients. A card might hold a wedding, eight mini-session families, a week in Yosemite, cars at a show, a kid's birthday, or all of those at once. Nothing in the Studio Manager may assume "client," "session" or "family." It groups by what is actually there (time gaps, place when the camera recorded it, and what Claude sees in one preview per group), suggests names from the facts it has ("Yosemite, Oct 12 to 15," "Smith Family" only when she said so), and uses plain words that fit the photos ("trip," "shoot," "day") rather than studio jargon. The Studio File asks what she shoots so the defaults fit her, but every kind of card must come out organized well, with nothing to undo. Being excellent at organizing is the product; being excellent at *client* organizing is one case of it.

**A drive with a lot on it (Dustin, Oct 23).** A card is simple: one `DCIM`, back it all up. A portable drive may hold years of mixed things with one shoot in a folder somewhere. When the source is not obviously one shoot, the Studio takes a quick inventory (folder names, photo counts, date ranges; seconds, nothing opened), shows it, and asks one plain question: "I see Jones Wedding (2,140 photos, May 3), Iceland (880, June), and a Lightroom catalog. Which do you want backed up?" If she already said it ("back up the Jones folder on the orange drive"), no question. It never silently copies a whole mixed drive, and it never guesses which folder she meant.

## How she talks to the Studio (Dustin, Oct 23)

Voice first, everywhere, not just for naming. She talks to the Studio Manager the way she would talk to a real studio manager standing in the room: "backup now," "that's the Smith family," "send the Johnsons their sneak peeks tomorrow," "the wedding's on three cards." The Studio works out what she means, asks one short question only when it truly can't tell, and gets on with it. Every screen and every reply is written so that talking is the obvious move: example sentences instead of forms, plain questions instead of menus, no commands to memorize. Typing is always there; it is never required. The goal is her life as simple as it can be: say it, walk away, find it done.

**Names get locked in, not guessed.** A spoken name goes into folders, filenames, the client note and the gallery, so a wrong spelling spreads everywhere. Common names she has said before, or that match a past client folder, go straight through. Anything the Studio is not sure it heard right (an unusual name, a name it has never seen, two plausible spellings) gets one short check before anything is named: show the spelling and ask her to confirm, or ask her to type just the last name right there. Never more than that, and never for "Smith."

## Where things stand (Oct 23, 2026)

- **Studio Manager safety core: built and proven once.** `backup.py` copies a card (or any folder) to the session drive and optionally a second folder, sha256 while copying, write to `.studio-partial` then rename, re-read verify, never overwrite, resumable from `_studio/manifest.json`, `--budget` seconds to pause cleanly (exit 3), morning report with problems first. Ran on Dustin's R5 card to his RAID "Big Kahuna" (`/Volumes/Big Kahuna/The Engine Test/`): 6.9 GB, 5 files, all verified; independent `sha256sum` matched. Exit codes: 0 safe, 1 problems, 2 stop (missing source or space), 3 paused.
- **Second real run (Oct 24): Nicole's card, 3,931 files, 126 GB, 7 minutes, all verified**, independent `shasum` matched. Found and fixed: the report now says "All N files have been safely backed up and verified," counts the hidden Mac `._` files it skips, and says the card is safe to format.
- **The morning report is also a page: `_studio/report.html`**, written by `report_page.py` at the end of every `backup.py` run. The look is borrowed from the sales-page mock-up ("Fresh": cream, coral, Fraunces + DM Sans) as a placeholder until Nicole settles the Studio's look; it is one block of styles. `previews.py` pulls small JPEGs out of the verified copy with the Mac's own `sips` (nothing to install, about half a second per RAW) into `_studio/previews/`; the page shows the six listed in `_studio/teasers.json`, which is where Claude's pick from a contact sheet goes. To see it inside the app: `.claude/launch.json` (ignored by git) runs `serve_report.py`, which serves the `_studio` folder at `http://localhost:8765/report.html` and opens folders in Finder when the page asks (only folders the report names); a photographer just double-clicks the file and the same link works from Safari. **One button, "Take me to the photos" (Dustin, Oct 24):** no card button, no second button. It opens the folder the photos are in. Rule: one shoot in one folder, open that folder; several folders, open the folder above them so she picks. (The page finds the deepest folder every photo shares.)
- **The index: `_studio/index.json`**, built by `index.py` after every clean backup (about 40 s for 4,000 photos). `exif_read.py` reads capture time, camera, serial, lens and settings out of the file with nothing installed (TIFF RAWs, CR3, JPEG, RAF, MP4/MOV clip times; HEIC not yet). Six dates checked against Spotlight on Nicole's card all matched to the second. Clip times from Canon MP4s were about an hour off Spotlight's on the one clip tried; photos are exact.
- **The gap finder: `groups.py` → `_studio/groups.json`.** Reads the index, starts a new group after 15 quiet minutes or a new day, pulls four previews per group into `_studio/groups/`. It over-splits on purpose; Claude looks at one contact sheet (one row per group) and merges, writing `_studio/shoots.json` (which groups make one shoot, a suggested date-based name, a recap, the reason). Proven on Nicole's card: 8 groups, one look showed 3 shoots (six outfit changes in one morning). The sheet-and-merge step is still done by hand in a session; it needs to become part of the skill.
- **The first look: `first_look.py` → `_studio/first-look.html`.** One card per shoot (three previews, date and lenses, the recap, a dashed name box that says "Type a name here, or just tell Claude"), the "Just tell me who's who" panel at the top, and "Lock the names in," which saves to `_studio/names.json` through `serve_report.py`. Dustin's calls (Oct 24): no time window or minutes chips; no mic button on the page (the app's preview pane blocks the microphone for every page, so talking happens through Claude, not the page); placeholder in the clean font, grey italic. **The sorter is built and proven: `sort.py`.** Takes `names.json` (or the suggested date names), makes one folder per shoot on the copy, renames every photo `<Shoot-slug>_<NNNN in shooting order>_<original name>`, sidecars travel along, undated and non-photo files stay put. Gate: every file verified, no problems. Checks the whole plan first (sources present at recorded size, no destination exists), writes `_studio/sort-log.json` before moving, renames on the same drive only (never copy-and-delete), then re-reads every moved file against the backup's hashes; `index.json`, `groups.json` and `shoots.json` are updated to the new paths with the originals kept. Ran on Nicole's session: 3,929 files into three folders in seconds, all re-verified in 3 min, zero problems, card untouched. `--dry-run` shows the plan. **A near-miss worth remembering (Oct 24):** the first version, run a second time on the same session, renamed every photo again (double prefix) and its verify pass "passed" because it had no hash to check. Nothing was lost; the log undid it in seconds. Two guards went in: a photo that already carries its original path is never planned again, and a move with no verified hash behind it stops the run, and a verify with no hash is a problem, never a pass. The lesson: every step that touches her files must be safe to run twice, and "verified" must mean a hash matched, never that a check was skipped. **Tidy (Dustin, Oct 24):** `_studio` is hidden in Finder (`chflags hidden`; name unchanged); the sorter sets non-photo leftovers (Canon `.CTG`, Finder `.DS_Store`) aside in `_studio/from-the-card/` with their original paths and drops the empty card folders, so her session folder shows only the shoots. Nothing deleted; the report says what was set aside.
- **Planned: the shoot archive (Dustin, Oct 24).** A page she asks for from inside Claude ("pull up the archive") and sees in the pane, like the morning report; where the file sits is the Studio's business (a `Studio` folder on her session drive), she never digs for it. Rebuilt at the end of every backup: every shoot the Studio has logged on that drive, newest first, each with three thumbnails, her name for it, the date, the count, and a "Take me to the photos" button, plus a filter box. It reads every session's `_studio/shoots.json`, previews and `names.json`; nothing new is stored. `serve_report.py` serves it as its third page; the skill answers "show me the report," "pull up the archive," "show me what's on the card." Open: whether pre-Studio sessions get a one-time "index this drive" run.
- **The night runs on its own, all the way through (Dustin, Oct 24).** "Backup now" → copy and verify → index → groups → Claude reads the one contact sheet, merges into shoots, writes a recap and a suggested name each → **the sorter runs with the suggested names** (folders, renamed photos, leftovers set aside, re-verified) → the report and the first look are waiting. Nothing waits on her. Her one decision is on the first look: leave the names, or change any; a change renames that shoot's folder and photos to her name, logs it, re-verifies, and says "All done." If she is there at 11pm she names them then; if not, the suggested names are already on the drive. This needs one more sorter command: **rename a shoot** that is already sorted (by shoot, from the `original` records; never a re-run of the whole sort, which the Oct 24 guard rightly stops).
- **Rename a shoot: `rename_shoot.py`**, built and tested (Oct 24). `--shoot s3 --name "..."` or `--from-names` (what "Lock the names in" leads to). Moves the folder in one step, renames each photo keeping the running number and original frame name, sidecars too, re-reads every file against the backup's hash, updates index/groups/shoots/names, logs to `_studio/rename-log.json` first. - **Mini-session sorting, as Dustin described it (Oct 23):** no booking screenshot needed. A script reads the capture times and finds the gaps on the day (twenty minutes of shooting, a pause, twenty more). Claude compares one photo from each cluster to confirm same shoot or different shoot (shortlist only, rule 6). - **The first look (Dustin, Oct 23):** on "backup now," the copy starts at once in the background. Meanwhile a quick scan reads capture times, finds the groups, and pulls two or three of the camera's embedded JPEG previews per group (seconds, no RAW conversion). She sees one local page: a row of thumbnails per group, the time span and count, a box to name it, merge and split buttons. If she skips it, groups get date-and-time names and the morning report asks again with the same page. Returning client names are suggested from her drive. Claude looks at one preview per group to confirm the split, nothing more, before she names. **Talking first, typing second:** she should be able to say "the first one is the Smith family from this afternoon, then the Johnsons, the last two are the same family" and have Claude work it out and fill the page in. - **Many cards, one session (Dustin, Oct 23):** the session is the unit, the card is a delivery. Each card is recognized when it mounts (volume ID plus a fingerprint of its files, kept in `_studio/cards.json`), copied into its own `Card-NN/` folder, verified, and reported card by card with the day as one timeline (holes flagged: "no photos between 3:10 and 4:45, is there another card?"). Dual-slot mirror cards are detected, not copied twice. ## Why we moved to Claude Code

The Claude desktop app's workspace stops any process the moment a step finishes (180 s per step), so a long backup had to run as resumable chunks. Claude Code runs commands directly on the Mac, can run them in the background, and can keep going while Dustin is away. The overnight jobs belong here. The Assistant and the morning report may stay in the desktop app, where she talks to it like a person. Test both; decide from what happens.
